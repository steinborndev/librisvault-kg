# MIR146A

MIR146A encodes a precursor of the microRNA miR-146a, a small non-coding RNA that represses target messenger RNAs after transcription.

## Connections

- Its interaction with [[TLR2]] was reported in patient samples, miR-146a is raised in postmortem Alzheimer brain, and it has been proposed as a switch toward a less pro-inflammatory microglial state; some studies found no difference in fluids.
- Variant: [[MIR146A rs2910164]]
- Disease: [[Alzheimer disease]]; see also [[MicroRNA-target interaction databases]]
- Study: [[Circulating microRNA biomarkers for Alzheimer disease by database mining]]
- Serum miR-146a-5p fell significantly at days 14 and 21 after intrahippocampal Aβ1-42 infusion in rats, but was unchanged in APPswe-PS1dE9 mice and higher in rat CSF; in rat astrocytes Aβ1-42 raised it through [[NF-kappaB signaling]], and blocking it let [[IRAK1]], [[TRAF6]], [[IL6]], [[IL1B]] and [[CXCL1]] rise, supporting an anti-inflammatory feedback role. Study: [[Rat intrahippocampal amyloid-beta model identifies miR-146a-5p as blood marker]]
- miR-146a was higher in mouse microglia kept 16 days than 2 days in culture, and amyloid-beta 1-42 raised it in the young cells without reaching the aged level; it served as a senescence marker (see [[Cellular senescence]]). Study: [[Aged cultured microglia respond less to amyloid-beta than young microglia]]
