# BIN1

BIN1 encodes an adaptor protein with many splice isoforms; the largest (about 65 kDa) is neuron-specific and mainly presynaptic. It restrains clathrin-mediated [[Endocytosis]] sorting of [[BACE1]] away from [[APP]] in early endosomes, and it binds the proline-rich region of [[MAPT]] through its SH3 domain. It is a genome-wide association locus for late-onset Alzheimer disease.

- Reviewed as a synaptic trafficking gene: brain expression is contradictory (transcript up, protein down; neuronal isoform down, ubiquitous isoform up); human BIN1 transgenic mice show early entorhinal-dentate neurodegeneration before plaques; Bin1 loss impairs vesicle recycling and AMPA currents (GluA1 surface expression), and it controls early-endosome size through [[RIN3]]. See [[Synaptic vesicle cycle]], [[AMPA receptor trafficking]]. Study: [[Endocytic trafficking genes and synapse dysfunction in Alzheimer disease]]
- Transcripts of the neuronal clathrin-binding isoform 1 fell and intron-retaining transcripts rose in Alzheimer temporal cortex, and isoform 1 protein was lower on western blots of Alzheimer frontal cortex and hippocampus (three controls, six cases). Study: [[Differential transcript usage in Alzheimer disease brain RNAseq by lobe]]
