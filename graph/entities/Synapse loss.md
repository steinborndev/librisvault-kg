# Synapse loss

Synapse loss is the reduction of synapse number and density in the brain, and in [[Alzheimer disease]] it is the best pathological correlate of cognitive impairment, exceeding and preceding neuronal death. Synapses become dysfunctional before plaques and tangles form.

## Connections

- Reviewed as the earliest event in familial and late-onset disease, with disrupted presynaptic vesicle exo- and endocytosis and postsynaptic glutamate receptor endocytosis as the common features. Study: [[Endocytic trafficking genes and synapse dysfunction in Alzheimer disease]]
- Mechanisms: [[Synaptic vesicle cycle]], [[AMPA receptor trafficking]], [[Long-term potentiation]]; genes: [[APOE]], [[BIN1]], [[SORL1]]
- Diseases: [[Familial Alzheimer disease]], [[Late-onset Alzheimer disease]]
- By [[Array tomography]], pre-synaptic density in temporal cortex was lower in Alzheimer cases than controls and lowest near plaques, and the loss near plaques was worst in APOE4 carriers, where synapses also more often held oligomeric [[Amyloid-beta]] and [[CLU]]. Study: [[Clusterin and amyloid-beta accumulate in synapses of APOE4 carriers in Alzheimer disease brain]]
- Proteomic profiling of Alzheimer brain found loss of NMDA receptor 1 and GluR2 (up to 40% at the postsynaptic density), higher Shank2 and lower, ubiquitin-modified Shank3, as candidate drivers of postsynaptic density destruction. Study: [[Shank proteins and glutamate receptors at the Alzheimer postsynaptic density]]; see [[Postsynaptic density]], [[SHANK3]]
- NNT deficiency alone was sufficient to cause Alzheimer-like synapse loss in mice; [[NNT]] supports pro-synaptic gene expression and synaptic protein homeostasis. Study: [[NNT deficiency impairs synapses and cognition in Alzheimer mouse models]]
- Electron microscopy showed lower synapse density, and Golgi staining fewer dendritic branches and spines, in 9-month-old [[ZDHHC21 T209S knock-in mouse]] hippocampus. Study: [[ZDHHC21 p.T209S in a Chinese Alzheimer family and knock-in mice]]
- In 5-month-old [[5xFAD mouse]] CA1, spine density and mushroom spines fell while thin spines rose; [[EDR peptide]] and [[KED peptide]] limited the loss (see [[Dendritic spine]]). Study: [[EDR and KED tripeptides restore dendritic spines in 5xFAD mice]]
