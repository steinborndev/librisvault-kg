# Down syndrome

Down syndrome is the disorder caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21, with intellectual disability and a high risk of early Alzheimer disease.

## Connections

- The review ties the added risk of tau pathology to the extra copy of [[DYRK1A]], a tau kinase on chromosome 21, though it notes that DYRK1A overexpression is not general in other Alzheimer patients. Study: [[Amyloid-beta and tau interplay in Alzheimer disease review]]
- Related: [[Alzheimer disease]]
- Two chromosome 21 microRNAs, [[MIR155]] and [[MIR802]], are overexpressed in the brain of the [[Ts65Dn mouse]]; sponging them rescued [[MECP2]], [[INPP5D]] and [[FOXM1]] and defined further targets ([[RUFY2]], [[NOVA1]], [[NAV1]], [[THOC1]], [[SUMO3]]). Study: [[miR-155 and miR-802 targets in the hippocampus of Ts65Dn Down syndrome mice]]
- In three Down syndrome brains with Alzheimer pathology, [[TMCC2]] marked dense-core plaques as in late-onset disease, and in two cases also spicular, thread-like methoxy-X04-positive but APP-negative lesions not seen in late-onset or familial cases; TMCC2 lies on chromosome 1, so this is not a dosage effect. Study: [[TMCC2 in dense-core plaques and Down syndrome pathology in Alzheimer brain]]
- Diffusion MRI shows early change in late-myelinating tracts before dementia, matching the retrogenesis model. Study: [[White matter injury, cholesterol and amyloid in Alzheimer disease review]]
- The 1987 mapping of APP to chromosome 21 and the early Alzheimer disease with abundant plaques in survivors to middle adulthood were read as evidence that too much APP is harmful, supporting the amyloid view; APOE4 influences risk and onset age here too. Study: [[Genetics and failed Alzheimer disease therapeutics perspective]]
- The three-copy dosage of chromosome 21 genes is modelled in flies by overexpressing human tau, Aβ42 or the [[DYRK1A]] orthologue; the DYRK1A inhibitor [[PST-001]] corrected eye degeneration, climbing, sleep and memory deficits, which the authors read as support for DYRK1A inhibition in Down syndrome with Alzheimer disease (about 90% lifetime incidence cited). Study: [[PST-001 DYRK1A inhibitor rescues fly models of Alzheimer and Down syndrome]]
- Plasma complement is broadly dysregulated in adults (n = 71 versus 46 controls): higher C1q, C3, C9, TCC, iC3b, C1 inhibitor, clusterin, factor H and FHR4, lower factor I and sCR1; those with dementia had lower C3 and factor I. The authors read this as generalised immune dysregulation, with [[CR1 rs6656401]] raising sCR1 and APOE ε4 having no effect. Study: [[Plasma complement dysregulation in Down syndrome with and without dementia]]
