# Pittsburgh compound B

Pittsburgh compound B is a thioflavin-derived radiotracer labelled with carbon-11 that binds fibrillar amyloid-beta and was the first widely used tracer for amyloid PET.

## Connections

- Named among the PET tracers that bind amyloid-beta deposits in the brain and so allow plaque burden to be visualised and quantified in living subjects. Study: [[Amyloid PET imaging in Alzheimer disease review]]
- Method: [[Amyloid PET]]
- Target: [[Amyloid-beta]]; disease: [[Alzheimer disease]]
- Described as a radiolabeled thioflavin-T analog that crosses the blood-brain barrier and binds fibrillar Aβ, allowing plaque load to be screened and followed in trials; the perspective suggests such imaging made amyloid easy to focus on.
