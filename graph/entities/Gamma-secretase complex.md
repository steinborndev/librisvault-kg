# Gamma-secretase complex

The gamma-secretase complex is the membrane-embedded protease whose catalytic core is [[PSEN1]] or [[PSEN2]]. It first cuts the [[APP]] C-terminal fragment C99 (endopeptidase-like step) and then trims the resulting long peptides stepwise (carboxypeptidase-like processivity) into [[Amyloid-beta]] of 37 to 43 residues.

## Connections

- Familial Alzheimer disease presenilin mutations impair processivity and favour longer peptides such as Aβ42; see [[Familial Alzheimer disease]] and [[Amyloid-beta formation]].
- Subunit gene: [[APH1B]]
- In cultured wild-type mouse cortical neurons, activity read out by a FRET sensor varied from cell to cell; the roughly 20% of neurons with the lowest activity also had a higher Aβ42/38 ratio, enlarged [[Endosome]] and [[Lysosome]] compartments and quicker death after glutamate or oxidant. Inhibiting the complex with [[DAPT]] reproduced the enlargement and the vulnerability. Study: [[A wild-type neuron subpopulation with familial Alzheimer-like phenotypes]]
- Method: [[FRET biosensors of gamma-secretase activity]]
- Transmembrane helices of presenilin are thought to form the catalytic site, cytoplasmic parts to recruit and retain APP; variants in the two regions gave different neurodegeneration despite equal amyloid, which the authors attribute possibly to calcium dysregulation, non-APP substrates such as NOTCH, or unmeasured Aβ species. Study: [[PSEN1 transmembrane versus cytoplasmic variants and progression in DIAN]]
