# PRKN

PRKN (formerly PARK2) encodes parkin, an E3 ubiquitin ligase that helps clear damaged mitochondria; loss-of-function variants cause early-onset recessive [[Parkinson disease]].

## Connections

- Cited as a gene near histone co-acetylation module m434, which was enriched for a neuromuscular process controlling balance and tied to [[Parkinsonism]].
- Study: [[Alzheimer polygenic risk score and motor impairment in older adults]]
- Two heterozygous PRKN changes (p.S167N, p.V380L), known Parkinson variants, were found in seven of eight Thai early-onset Alzheimer patients; the authors suggest they may contribute to Alzheimer disease. Study: [[Fifty-gene neurodegeneration panel in eight Thai early-onset Alzheimer patients]]
- Listed as PARK2, co-expressed in brain with the five upregulated genes [[ATP6V1A]], [[BNIP3]], [[CAMK4]], [[TIPRL]] and [[TOMM70]]. Study: [[Multiomic analysis of Alzheimer brain finds five upregulated genes and antrocin]]
- Canonical PINK1-Parkin mitophagy was the comparator pathway for [[ALT001]], which worked in Parkin-expressing [[HeLa]] cells lacking [[ATG7]] and so does not use it. Study: [[ALT001 induces Rab9-dependent alternative mitophagy in Alzheimer disease models]]
- Parkin protein and PINK1-Parkin co-localization fell with [[D-ribose]] in [[Neuro2a]] cells and APP/PS1 mouse hippocampus and were restored by [[Berberine]]. Study: [[Berberine rescues D-ribose-induced Alzheimer pathology via mitophagy]]
