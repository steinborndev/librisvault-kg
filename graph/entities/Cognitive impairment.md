# Cognitive impairment

Cognitive impairment is a decline in thinking functions such as memory, attention and reasoning; it is the defining clinical feature of [[Alzheimer disease]] and of its prodromal stage, [[Mild cognitive impairment]].

## Connections

- Alzheimer-like cognitive deficits arose from NNT deficiency alone, independent of amyloid or tau pathology, in mice. Study: [[NNT deficiency impairs synapses and cognition in Alzheimer mouse models]]; gene: [[NNT]]
- Related phenotypes: [[Memory impairment]], [[Synapse loss]]
- The proband of a Han Chinese family carrying [[ZDHHC21 p.T209S]] scored MMSE 5 and CDR 3 at age 55; knock-in mice carrying the change had impaired spatial learning and memory. Study: [[ZDHHC21 p.T209S in a Chinese Alzheimer family and knock-in mice]]
- MMSE and CDR Sum of Boxes worsened faster with expected years to onset in transmembrane than cytoplasmic PSEN1 carriers, the MMSE gap diverging about 3.8 years before onset and fully mediated by hippocampal volume. Study: [[PSEN1 transmembrane versus cytoplasmic variants and progression in DIAN]]
- [[D-ribose]] impaired spatial learning and memory in [[APP-PS1 mouse]] animals and [[Berberine]] reversed it through mitophagy ([[Morris water maze]], [[Two-way shuttle avoidance task]]). Study: [[Berberine rescues D-ribose-induced Alzheimer pathology via mitophagy]]
- Decline in Mini-Mental State Examination score was modelled as a curve with a midpoint age and a rate; [[APOE]] ε4 and [[ABCA7 rs3764650]] brought the midpoint earlier, [[SORL1 rs3737529]] later. See [[Bayesian hierarchical model of cognitive decline trajectories]]. Study: [[Genetic effects on cognitive decline trajectories in Han Chinese in Taiwan]]
