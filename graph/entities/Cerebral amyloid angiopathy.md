# Cerebral amyloid angiopathy

Cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) is deposition of amyloid beta in the walls of small brain vessels, causing ischemic infarcts and lobar haemorrhage.

## Connections

- Genes in which mutations cause familial forms include [[APP]] and [[CST3]]; PSEN1 mutations cause it rarely and [[PSEN2]] not at all. Study: [[Alzheimer disease genes in small vessel ischemic disease and mouse hypoperfusion]]
- Related: [[Small vessel ischemic disease]], [[Alzheimer disease]]
- Enlarged perivascular spaces, which impair drainage of interstitial fluid, have been linked to this disease in cognitively impaired patients; in unimpaired adults they tracked CSF tau markers instead. See [[Enlarged perivascular spaces]] and [[Perivascular spaces and CSF biomarkers in cognitively unimpaired Alzheimer continuum]]
- The APOE ε2 and ε4 alleles are both associated with its risk, and barrier dysfunction from it can worsen neuroinflammation; the genotype-stratified transcriptome study [[Blood and brain transcriptome of Alzheimer disease by APOE genotype]] framed its vascular-injury analysis on this.
- Amyloid angiopathy is a feature of [[Familial British dementia]] and [[Familial Danish dementia]], caused by [[ITM2B]] mutations. Study: [[BRI2 in familial British and Danish dementia and Alzheimer disease review]]
- Seen with some APP mutations, its prominent vascular phenotype was little questioned in the early- versus late-onset comparison, and the perspective proposes that the overlap of cerebrovascular disease with late-onset disease deserves action, including stroke prevention. Study: [[Genetics and failed Alzheimer disease therapeutics perspective]]
