# KED peptide

KED (Lys-Glu-Asp, marketed as Vesugen) is a synthetic tripeptide originally isolated from a bovine vessel peptide complex and proposed as a vasoprotective and neuroprotective bioregulator.

## Connections

- In [[5xFAD mouse]] animals given 400 μg/kg daily for 2 months it restored the CA1 mushroom spine fraction to the control level and lowered thin spines in the pooled group, with a stronger spine-density effect in males (22%) than in females, where only thin spines fell; it showed a non-significant trend toward restoring [[Long-term potentiation]]. Docking gave a weaker best DNA complex (ICM score -26.6) than [[EDR peptide]]. Study: [[EDR and KED tripeptides restore dendritic spines in 5xFAD mice]]
- Method: [[Molecular docking]]
- Disease context: [[Alzheimer disease]]
