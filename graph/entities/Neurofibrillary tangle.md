# Neurofibrillary tangle

Neurofibrillary tangles are intraneuronal aggregates of abnormally phosphorylated tau assembled as paired helical filaments, a defining neuropathology of [[Alzheimer disease]].

## Connections

- Tangle density tracked both the Alzheimer [[Polygenic risk score]] and motor impairment, unlike beta-amyloid burden, which tracked mainly the score.
- Related: [[Motor impairment]], [[Parkinsonism]]
- Study: [[Alzheimer polygenic risk score and motor impairment in older adults]]
- Reviewed as rising with chronic stress in animals and as promoting neuroinflammation and barrier dysfunction once formed. Study: [[Stress, mast cells and blood-brain barrier disruption in Alzheimer disease]]
- Tangles analogous to those of Alzheimer disease occur in [[Familial British dementia]] and [[Familial Danish dementia]]. Study: [[BRI2 in familial British and Danish dementia and Alzheimer disease review]]
- Reviewed as forming when hyperphosphorylated tau detaches from microtubules, assembles into paired or straight filaments and spreads; tangles are said to obstruct axonal transport and synaptic communication. Only 1.21% of layer 2/3 neocortical neurons in rTg4510 mice held tangles yet cortical activity was strongly reduced, which the review takes to mean soluble tau silences neurons. See [[Tau hyperphosphorylation]]. Study: [[Amyloid-beta and tau interplay in Alzheimer disease review]]
- Tangle number and distribution correlate better with clinical disease than plaque load, yet they were excluded from the first neuropathology diagnostic criteria; tangle-bearing neurons die and leave the tangle behind. Study: [[Genetics and failed Alzheimer disease therapeutics perspective]]
