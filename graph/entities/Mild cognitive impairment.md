# Mild cognitive impairment

Mild cognitive impairment is a state of measurable cognitive decline that falls short of dementia, regarded as the transition between normal ageing and [[Alzheimer disease]].

## Connections

- Brain amyloid deposition is described as the key feature for identifying the stage at which mild cognitive impairment is a prodromal form of [[Alzheimer disease]].
- One of four patients, with slightly raised tau and very low amyloid beta 1-42 oligomer in plasma, was read by the nanoparticle kit as probably having mild cognitive impairment; see [[MAPT]] and [[APP]].
- Study: [[Fluorescent nanoparticle kit for early Alzheimer disease diagnosis]]
- In 117 cognitively normal elderly followed five years, the 21 who progressed to mild cognitive impairment only had a metabolic network more modular than stable normals and less so than those who progressed to Alzheimer disease; see [[Brain metabolic network modularity]]. Study: [[Brain metabolism and Alzheimer risk gene expression in normal elderly]]
- In 136 patients (28 progressing during follow-up) the 13-protein universal plasma panel separated progressing from stable patients with a ROC-AUC of 0.81, against 0.62 for the Mini-Mental State Examination and clock drawing test together; a panel tuned only for Alzheimer disease versus controls reached 0.64. Study: [[Plasma protein panel for Alzheimer disease risk by MRM mass spectrometry]]
- Minor haplotypes of the [[NECTIN2]] and [[APOC1]] regions were enriched in mild cognitive impairment (253 patients) as well as in Alzheimer disease, relative to controls. Study: [[Non-coding risk haplotypes at the APOE locus in Alzheimer disease]]
- In the EMIF-AD MBD cohort (198 patients with mild cognitive impairment) CSF [[FABP3]], [[MDH1]] and [[GDI1]] were higher than in cognitively normal people and were higher again in amyloid-positive than amyloid-negative patients, each separating the two groups on its own. Study: [[Microglial activity state marker panel in FTD-GRN and Alzheimer disease CSF]]
- Named, with early Alzheimer disease, as the target population for [[APP-selective BACE1 inhibitors]], aiming for fewer off-target effects than earlier BACE1 inhibitors. Study: [[APP-selective BACE1 inhibitor FAH65 from a phenytoin screening hit]]
- Amyloid PET is reviewed as able to predict progression from mild cognitive impairment to Alzheimer disease (abstract only, no rate given); see [[Amyloid PET]]. Study: [[Amyloid PET imaging in Alzheimer disease review]]

- Amyloid-positive MCI resembled Alzheimer disease in CSF proteomics, with raised glycolytic enzymes ([[ALDOA]], [[LDHA]], [[MDH1]], [[PGAM1]]), while amyloid-negative MCI changed differently and resembled none of the dementias studied. Study: [[CSF proteomics of Alzheimer, Lewy body and frontotemporal dementia]]
- Mild impairment may already be too late for an effective amyloid-targeted intervention, which would make prevention the better but harder-to-trial strategy; the tau antibody [[Gosuranemab]] and [[Levetiracetam]] were tested here. Study: [[Genetics and failed Alzheimer disease therapeutics perspective]]
- In ADNI participants with this diagnosis, [[BACE2 rs9980146]] and [[BACE2 rs2012050]] associated with CSF Aβ1-42 in APOE ε4 non-carriers (uncorrected p = 0.0207 for the lead SNP). Study: [[Common BACE2 polymorphisms and CSF amyloid in APOE ε4 non-carriers]]
- In 62 patients with amnestic mild cognitive impairment and positive CSF or amyloid PET biomarkers, 48.4% met criteria for [[Mild behavioral impairment]], with raised affective dysregulation, [[Apathy]] and impulse dyscontrol versus 50 cognitively normal controls; neither [[APOE]] ε4 nor [[BDNF p.V66M]] Met predicted severity. Study: [[Mild behavioral impairment with APOE and BDNF in early Alzheimer disease]]
- Of 44 MCI patients followed yearly in Taiwan, 28 (63.6%) progressed to Alzheimer disease; pooled with 255 Alzheimer patients they were used to test genetic effects on cognitive decline, and dropping the 16 non-progressors left results unchanged. Study: [[Genetic effects on cognitive decline trajectories in Han Chinese in Taiwan]]
