# Antrocin

Antrocin is a sesquiterpene lactone natural product, studied here as a candidate drug for [[Alzheimer disease]]. It is the main bioactive component of interest from *Antrodia cinnamomea*, a fungus found only in Taiwan, and has known anti-tumour and immune-modulating activity.

## Connections

- Predicted by SVM and LiCABEDS models to cross the blood-brain barrier, and by docking to bind [[CAMK4]], [[TOMM70]] and [[TIPRL]] with high affinity; predicted ADMET and drug-likeness were favourable and Lipinski's rules were met. All evidence was computational. Study: [[Multiomic analysis of Alzheimer brain finds five upregulated genes and antrocin]]
- Methods: [[Molecular docking]], [[Blood-brain barrier permeability prediction]]
- Full-text predictions: blood-brain barrier score 0.038 (threshold 0.02), molecular weight 234.33 Da, no PAINS alerts, not a P-glycoprotein substrate, no CYP1A2, CYP2C19, CYP2D6 or CYP3A4 interaction, bioavailability 0.55. Docking affinities were -6.80 ([[TOMM70]]), -6.70 ([[CAMK4]]), -6.60 ([[TIPRL]]), -5.90 ([[ATP6V1A]]) and -5.80 kcal/mol ([[BNIP3]]). Earlier work by the same group showed antrocin suppressing FAK, PI3K/Akt, MAPK/ERK, mTOR, NF-kB, JAK/STAT3, Notch1 and IGF-1R signalling in cancer settings.
