# Dystrophic neurite

Dystrophic neurites are swollen presynaptic axonal processes with sprouting, packed with organelles and enzymes such as [[BACE1]], that surround amyloid plaques in [[Alzheimer disease]] brain.

## Connections

- In mouse primary neurons, amyloid-beta 42 oligomers produced beads-on-a-string neurites with immobile microtubules and tubulin aggregates; in patients and [[5xFAD mouse]] animals the neurites held disrupted microtubules, mislocalised dynein and kinesin, high BACE1 and amyloid-beta, and defective autophagy and lysosomes. The review infers that oligomers drive their formation, which it flags as untested. Reactive astrocytes clear them ([[Astrocyte activation]]). Study: [[Amyloid-beta and tau interplay in Alzheimer disease review]]
- APP-positive dystrophies beside dense-core plaques co-stained for [[TMCC2]] almost universally in late-onset Alzheimer brain (more in APOE3 than APOE4 homozygotes) but rarely in early-onset familial and Down syndrome brain, where intensely APP-positive dystrophies were common. Study: [[TMCC2 in dense-core plaques and Down syndrome pathology in Alzheimer brain]]
