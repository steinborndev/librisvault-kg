# White matter hyperintensity

White matter hyperintensities are bright areas of cerebral white matter on T2-weighted or FLAIR MRI, linked to vascular risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes and smoking.

## Connections

- Disease: seen less in genetic [[Familial Alzheimer disease]] carriers than in non-carriers of the same cohort, which the authors attribute to younger age
- Study: on a 0 to 3 visual scale and adjusted for age, white matter hyperintensity was higher in Taiwanese early-onset patients with no PSEN1 or APP mutation than in carriers. See [[Familial Alzheimer disease mutations in Han Chinese in Taiwan]]
- Greater volume rises about 6 years before predicted onset in familial Alzheimer carriers and predicts amyloid increase and conversion to Alzheimer disease. Study: [[White matter injury, cholesterol and amyloid in Alzheimer disease review]]; concept: [[White matter injury]]
