# Clusterin and amyloid-beta accumulate in synapses of APOE4 carriers in Alzheimer disease brain

A post-mortem study of superior temporal cortex (Brodmann area 41/42) from people with [[Alzheimer disease]] and non-demented controls, genotyped for [[APOE]]. It tests whether [[CLU]] (clusterin, apolipoprotein J) and ApoE4 act together to damage synapses by concentrating oligomeric [[Amyloid-beta]] inside them. Tissue came from the Edinburgh Brain Bank.

## Design

- APOE genotype was read by PCR of cerebellar DNA followed by HhaI digestion and gel banding.
- Biochemistry: [[Synaptoneurosome isolation]] from fresh-frozen cortex, with western blots for clusterin in crude homogenate and in the synaptic fraction. Enrichment was checked with [[SYP]] (synaptophysin) and [[DLG4]] (PSD95), exclusion of nuclei with histone H3, and post-mortem protein integrity with the NMDAR2B degradation ratio (samples below 1 were excluded).
- Imaging: [[Array tomography]] of 70 nm serial sections, with one round for oligomeric Aβ (antibody 1C22) and a second round, after stripping, for synaptophysin and clusterin. Fields were taken near plaques (under 10 μm from the edge) and far from plaques (over 45 μm). The unit of analysis was the case mean or median, and the experimenter was blind to condition.
- Because little tissue was suitable for array tomography there was no APOE4 control group in that experiment.

## Findings

- Western blots: clusterin was higher in Alzheimer cases than controls in crude homogenate (disease effect P = 0.0003) with no effect of APOE genotype. In synaptoneurosomes the disease effect was larger (P < 0.0001), and Alzheimer APOE4 cases had more synaptic clusterin than Alzheimer APOE3 cases (Tukey P = 0.0413). So APOE4 raises clusterin at the remaining synapses, not across the cortex. In Alzheimer APOE4 cases, more than in any other group, synaptic clusterin exceeded the level in the same case's homogenate.
- Synapse density: pre-synaptic density was lower in Alzheimer cases than controls, lower near than far from plaques, and the loss near plaques was worst in APOE4 cases (APOE4 near versus far P = 0.032; control versus Alzheimer APOE4 near P < 0.0001). This replicates the group's earlier report and fits [[Synapse loss]] as a hallmark.
- Oligomeric Aβ: the share of synapses containing it was higher near plaques than far from them, and higher in APOE4 than APOE3 cases near plaques (P = 0.0061).
- Clusterin in synapses: the share of synapses with clusterin was increased in Alzheimer cases, most near plaques in APOE4 carriers (Kruskal-Wallis P = 0.0003).
- Co-localisation: the share of synapses holding both clusterin and oligomeric Aβ was highest in Alzheimer APOE4 cases near plaques (Kruskal-Wallis P = 0.0002; APOE3 near versus APOE4 near P = 0.0061).

## Interpretation by the authors

- The rise of synaptic clusterin could be a stress response to Aβ, internalisation of clusterin bound to Aβ, or both in a positive feedback loop. They state openly that the data cannot tell whether the accumulation protects (clusterin binds Aβ and prevents fibrillisation) or harms (clusterin knockout protected rat neurons from Aβ toxicity in culture, though at supraphysiological Aβ).
- ApoE and clusterin may compensate for each other: Apoe knockout raises clusterin mRNA, and plaque deposition in PDAPP mice changed most when both were deleted.
- Clusterin may also act through partners such as [[TREM2]], and a shift in its subcellular position could affect the neuro-glial-vascular unit; loss of clusterin in APP/PS1 mice moves amyloid to vessels.

## Why This Source Matters

Clusterin and APOE are two of the strongest genetic risk factors for late-onset Alzheimer disease, and both are lipoproteins that handle Aβ. This study puts the two together in human tissue at synapse resolution: the APOE4 genotype concentrates clusterin and oligomeric Aβ in the same synapses, particularly near plaques, where synapse loss is also worst. That gives a concrete, testable site of interaction between two risk genes and supports a synapse-level route from genetic risk to cognitive decline.

## Assessment

- Small human cohorts, with case numbers given only in the paper's Table 1; array tomography had no APOE4 control group, so the APOE4-by-disease interaction there cannot be separated from the effect of the allele alone.
- Post-mortem, end-stage tissue from one cortical area; cross-sectional data cannot show whether clusterin accumulation precedes or follows Aβ at the synapse.
- Co-localisation within a synaptophysin punctum on 70 nm sections shows that two signals lie in the same terminal, not that they bind each other.
- Western blots compare a synaptically enriched fraction; the enrichment markers confirmed enrichment per case, but a mixed (pre- and post-synaptic, glial) content of synaptoneurosomes is not resolved.
- The introduction's figures on APOE4 (12-fold risk and onset at 68 rather than 84 years for two copies versus two APOE3 copies) are background from earlier literature, not results of this study.

## Open Questions

- Does clusterin accumulation at synapses precede oligomeric amyloid-beta accumulation in the same terminals (only end-stage post-mortem tissue was examined)?
- Is synaptic clusterin protective or harmful for synapse survival in APOE4 carriers (the data are correlative and no manipulation was done)?
- Is the extra synaptic clusterin of APOE4 carriers internalised from the extracellular space bound to Aβ, or made inside the neuron in response to Aβ?
- Is the clusterin increase at synapses also seen in APOE4 carriers without Alzheimer disease (no APOE4 control group was available for array tomography)?
