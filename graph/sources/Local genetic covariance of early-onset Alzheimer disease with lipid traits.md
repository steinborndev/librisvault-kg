# Local genetic covariance of early-onset Alzheimer disease with lipid traits

A PLOS Genetics study that asks whether the common-variant risk of [[Early-onset Alzheimer disease]] (EOAD) overlaps with the genetics of blood lipids. It works only from published summary statistics: the Alzheimer's Disease Genetics Consortium EOAD [[Genome-wide association study]] (6,282 cases with diagnosis at or before age 70 and 13,386 cognitively normal controls over 70, all of European ancestry; n = 19,668) and the Global Lipids Genetics Consortium meta-analysis of five traits (European-ancestry subset, n = 1,320,016): [[Cholesterol]] (total), [[LDL cholesterol]], [[HDL cholesterol]], [[Non-HDL cholesterol]] and [[Triglycerides]]. The motivation is that about 90% of EOAD is not explained by [[APP]], [[PSEN1]] and [[PSEN2]], that midlife cholesterol and statin use track Alzheimer risk, and that a plasma study of 2,125 EOAD cases and controls reported higher LDL-C independent of [[APOE]] and rare coding variants in APOB.

## Findings

- **Direct overlap of GWAS hits.** Taking each genome-wide significant EOAD locus with a 1 Mb window, five loci were also significant for at least one lipid trait: the [[APOE]] region on 19q13.32 (EOAD top SNP [[APOE rs59007384]], P = 5.73e-311; lipid top SNPs include [[APOE rs429358]] for HDL and [[APOE rs7412]] for LDL, nonHDL and total cholesterol), the [[TREM2]] region on 6p21.1 ([[TREM2 rs75932628]], P = 1.66e-15; triglycerides P = 2.68e-10), a chromosome 11q12.2 locus ([[MS4A4E rs11824734]], P = 2.88e-8; total cholesterol P = 4.64e-9), [[LILRA5]] on 19q13.42 ([[LILRA5 rs2781753]], P = 3.47e-10; HDL, total cholesterol and triglycerides) and [[LRRC25]] on 19p13.11 ([[LRRC25 rs7258465]], P = 8.91e-9; HDL P = 1.73e-8). At the 11q12.2 locus the nearest gene is MS4A6E but the gene prioritised in the EOAD GWAS is [[MS4A4E]].
- **Local genetic covariance.** With [[Local genetic covariance analysis]] (SUPERGNOVA) over 2,353 approximately independent regions, three regions passed the Bonferroni threshold of P < 2.12e-5: 5q13.3 (73.51 to 75.24 Mb, GRCh37; total cholesterol P = 5.5e-10, LDL-C 8.17e-9, nonHDL-C 1.30e-10), 10q26.13 (123.86 to 124.89 Mb; total cholesterol 3.78e-9, LDL-C 1.26e-8, nonHDL-C 2.90e-9) and 11q12.1-q12.2 (59.62 to 61.87 Mb; nonHDL-C only, P = 1.60e-6). No covariance reached significance with HDL-C or triglycerides. Together the regions hold 26 genes under the peak of the EOAD top hit.
- **Gene prioritisation.** Each of the 26 genes received a [[Composite gene prioritization score]] summing z-scores for MAGMA gene-based association, Agora Alzheimer risk scores, MetaBrain cortex and hippocampus cis-[[Expression quantitative trait locus analysis]], [[Colocalization analysis]] with 61 eQTL datasets, ROSMAP prefrontal [[DNA methylation]] against amyloid and tau load, and [[Single-cell RNA sequencing]] differential expression in human Alzheimer brain (4 cases, 4 controls, 44,132 cells) and an Aβ42-injected [[Danio rerio]] model. The top gene in each region was [[ANKDD1B]] (5q13.3), [[CUZD1]] (10q26.13) and [[MS4A6A]] (11q12.1-q12.2).
- **Evidence for the three genes.**
  - ANKDD1B: gene-based P = 2.9e-4; cortex eQTL P = 3.18e-110; colocalization H4 = 0.82; Agora target risk 1.62.
  - CUZD1: gene-based P = 0.01; cortex eQTL P = 1.05e-91; H4 = 0.84; Agora 1.48.
  - MS4A6A: gene-based P = 4.07e-10; H4 = 0.96; Agora 3.49; methylation sites linked to both amyloid (P = 0.02) and tau (P = 0.005); differentially expressed between human cases and controls (P = 3.74e-20).
- **Context drawn from the literature.** ANKDD1B is tied to LDL-C, [[Type 2 diabetes mellitus]] and a severe-hypercholesterolaemia polygenic score; CUZD1 sits in secretory granules and the zymogen activation pathway and its protein level tracks [[Migraine]] risk; [[MS4A6A rs610932]] is associated with lower Alzheimer risk and MS4A6A is linked to atherosclerosis and to lipid differences between cases and controls.

## Assessment

- Everything is correlational and works from summary statistics; the authors state functional follow-up is needed to show causality. Only genes under the EOAD top-hit peak were scored, so causal genes elsewhere in a region, or in regions missed for lack of power (especially in the EOAD set) or without sex-stratified data, would be overlooked.
- The composite score is an unweighted sum of z-scores, and some genes lack data in some resources, so their rank depends on which resources cover them.
- The abstract names "MS4A64", but the figure legend, results and discussion all give MS4A6A; this is read as a typographical slip. One triglyceride P-value for the LILRA5 locus is garbled in the text ("6.61x3.63x10-10") and is not reproduced here.
- Controls were required to be older than 70 while cases could be diagnosed up to age 70, which makes this a case definition of onset at or before 70 rather than the usual under-65 cut-off used in the introduction. The methods say the EOAD data were aligned to GRCh38 while all results are in GRCh37.
- The human single-cell comparison uses 4 cases and 4 controls; the zebrafish arm is an Aβ42 injection model.

## Why This Source Matters

By the authors' account it is the first test of shared common-variant architecture between early-onset Alzheimer disease and blood lipids, and it points to non-APOE loci (5q13.3, 10q26.13 and the MS4A cluster) where the lipid link is specific to cholesterol fractions that contain LDL. It also shows a multi-evidence, z-score-sum recipe for ranking genes at a covariance region that others can reuse.

## Open Questions

- Does experimental knockdown or overexpression of ANKDD1B or CUZD1 in neurons or microglia change amyloid or tau pathology (the study is correlational and has no functional follow-up)?
- Does the 5q13.3 and 10q26.13 covariance with LDL-C hold in a larger EOAD GWAS with more statistical power (the EOAD sample was 6,282 cases and the authors name power as a limit)?
- Are the same regions seen when late-onset Alzheimer disease summary statistics are used in the same covariance analysis (only EOAD was tested)?
- Is the lipid covariance different in men and women (sex-stratified data were not available)?
