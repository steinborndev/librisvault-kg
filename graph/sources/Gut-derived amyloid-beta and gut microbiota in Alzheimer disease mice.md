# Gut-derived amyloid-beta and gut microbiota in Alzheimer disease mice

A 2023 mouse and human-tissue study asking whether the gut is a source of brain [[Amyloid-beta]] and whether the [[Gut microbiota]] drives that source. It profiles [[APP]] processing genes along the gut, follows colonic Aβ42 with age in wild-type and [[APP-PS1 mouse]] animals, characterises the faecal microbiome, transplants microbiota from old APP/PS1 donors into young wild-type recipients, and traces labelled human Aβ42 from gut to brain with and without vagotomy.

## Design

- Gut and brain mRNA of App, Adam10, Bace1, Psen1, Psen2, Ncstn, Aph1a, Aph1b and Mapt in 3-, 6- and 12-month-old mice (qPCR, n = 3 or 4); Aβ by Western blot in the same tissues.
- Human tissue: postmortem brain (n = 7) and gut tissue adjacent to cancer (n = 9) by electrochemiluminescence immunoassay for Aβ40/42; Aβ42 immunohistochemistry in colon.
- 16S rRNA (V3-V4) sequencing of faeces from 3- and 12-month-old wild-type and APP/PS1 mice (n = 6 to 8 per group); [[Fecal microbiota transplantation]] of 12-month-old APP/PS1 faecal supernatant into just-weaned wild-type mice, three gavages a week, with self-gavaged littermates as control.
- Tracer experiments in 10-month-old mice: 125-iodine and FITC-labelled human Aβ42, and oligomeric human Aβ42 (50 μg injected into the colon serosa), with gastric vagotomy in 5- and 10-month-old mice, read out 3 months later.
- Behaviour (Y-maze, novel object recognition, Morris water maze) two months after transplantation.

## Findings

- **Gut as a source.** App mRNA was higher in all gut segments than in brain (2 to 6 times higher in the discussion), while Bace1 and Mapt were lower; Adam10, Psen1, Psen2, [[NCSTN]], [[APH1A]] and [[APH1B]] did not clearly differ. Aβ was present throughout the gut, in some segments close to brain level. In human tissue the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio was significantly higher in gut than brain (P < 0.0001), and colonic epithelial cells stained strongly for Aβ42.
- **Ageing.** Colonic Aβ42 was higher at 12 than 3 months in wild-type mice (P < 0.05) and in APP/PS1 mice (P < 0.01 by Western blot, confirmed by ELISA). APP and BACE1 trended up; PS1 was lower in 12-month-old APP/PS1 colon.
- **Microbiome.** Alpha-diversity did not differ, but beta-diversity separated 3- from 12-month-old animals and APP/PS1 from wild-type at each age. Bacteroidales, Bacteroidia, Bacteroidetes, Prevotellaceae and Prevotella rose in 12-month-old APP/PS1 mice. Gut permeability (FITC-dextran) was highest in these mice, with more pro-inflammatory genera (Bacteroides, Prevotella, Ruminococcus) and fewer Akkermansia, Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus.
- **Transplantation.** Recipients of old APP/PS1 microbiota had higher colonic BACE1 and Aβ42 (both P < 0.05); APP rose but not significantly (P = 0.1221). Little Aβ was present in the faecal supernatant itself.
- **Gut to brain.** Radiolabelled Aβ42 reached blood and brain within 1.5 to 2 hours, and FITC-Aβ42 was seen in brain parenchyma by two-photon imaging. Three months after colonic injection of oligomers, human Aβ42 sat in cortex and hippocampus, mostly inside microglia, with microglial activation. Vagotomy removed the hippocampal signal and microglial activation in 5-month-old mice but not in 10-month-old mice, which also had more plasma IgG leaking into cortex, so the blood route dominates in age when the [[Blood-brain barrier dysfunction]] is greater.
- **Transplant phenotype.** FMT-AD mice scored lower on Y-maze and novel object recognition but not on the water maze. No plaques and no change in brain APP or BACE1, yet higher [[AIF1]] (Iba1) and [[NOS2]] (iNOS) in cortex and hippocampus and more Aβ42 inside microglia (cortex P < 0.01, hippocampus P < 0.05), read as early [[Neuroinflammation]] and [[Microglial cell activation]].

## Why This Source Matters

It gives an experimental route, in mice, from an aged and altered gut flora to higher colonic BACE1 and Aβ42, then through the blood to microglia, with a memory phenotype before any plaque. That puts the periphery, and the [[Gut-brain axis]], into the amyloid story alongside brain-intrinsic production, and it suggests microbiota or diet as a handle on Aβ metabolism. The age dependence of the vagal versus blood route is a concrete refinement of an earlier controversy.

## Assessment

- Mouse work dominates; human data are one Aβ40/42 measurement and one immunostain, from different donors for brain and gut (the authors say so), and the gut tissue was adjacent to cancer.
- Group sizes are small (often n = 3 to 9) and several APP and BACE1 changes are trends (P = 0.19, P = 0.1221).
- Transplant recipients were weanlings gavaged with whole faecal supernatant, so other microbial products, not Aβ made in the gut, could drive the microglial and behavioural change; the study does not separate gut Aβ from other microbiota effects.
- The tracers are human Aβ42 injected as a single bolus into the colon wall, which bypasses normal gut production.
- Water maze performance was unchanged; no plaques were seen, and no longer follow-up of deposition was done.

## Open Questions

- Does lowering colonic BACE1 or Aβ42 (for example by diet, probiotics or a gut-restricted inhibitor) prevent the microglial and memory changes seen after transplantation of old APP/PS1 microbiota?
- Which bacterial taxa or products raise colonic BACE1 in recipients of old APP/PS1 faeces (only composition shifts were reported, with no causal taxon tested)?
- Is the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio higher in gut than brain within the same individuals (brain and gut samples came from different donors)?
