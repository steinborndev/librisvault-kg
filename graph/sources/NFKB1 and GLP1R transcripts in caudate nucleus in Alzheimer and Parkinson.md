# NFKB1 and GLP1R transcripts in caudate nucleus in Alzheimer and Parkinson

## Design

- Whole-transcriptome bulk RNA-seq of postmortem [[Caudate nucleus]] from the NIH NeuroBioBank: 5 unaffected controls, 6 [[Alzheimer disease]] and 3 [[Parkinson disease]] cases (14 donors, 4 male and 10 female, median age 75, all white; postmortem interval 4 to 25 h). RNA was extracted with a QIAGEN lipid-tissue kit and sequenced in two sessions covering 62,704 gene loci, of which 2,144 (3.4%) had p below 0.05.
- Analyses: principal component analysis in GraphPad Prism on FPKM values, hierarchical clustering (Instant Clue), gene set enrichment (GSEA 4.3.3, g:Profiler), and Welch ANOVA or t-tests per gene. Alzheimer and Parkinson cases were first pooled, then split.
- A side analysis looked up transcript length, telomere proximity (within 50 Mb) and A+T content (above 59%) of neurotransmitter receptor genes in mouse, rat, chimpanzee and human genomes as proxies for mutability.

## Findings

- Principal component 1 explained 86.6% of variance; MT-ATP6P1 and [[FKBP5]] loaded most strongly, and [[MT-CYB]] appeared in both separate Alzheimer and Parkinson analyses, which the authors take as mitochondrial involvement. One control (Control 3) clustered with the disease samples, so one in three controls looked disease-like, and hierarchical clustering over 41,971 transcripts did not separate samples strictly by diagnosis.
- TNF-alpha signalling via NF-κB ranked first or second among enriched gene sets in both diseases. Of eleven transcription factors checked, [[NFKB1]] and [[NFE2L2]] were higher in both Alzheimer and Parkinson caudate nucleus; [[TIPE2]] (TNFAIP8L2) was higher in Alzheimer alone.
- [[GLP1R]], [[SLC25A6]] and [[SLC9A9]] changed in both diseases and [[SLC37A1]] in Alzheimer alone; [[HSPA2]] did not reach significance in separate comparisons.
- [[HBA1]] RNA fell about 10-fold in Alzheimer (305 vs 35 FPKM) and 7-fold in Parkinson (305 vs 46 FPKM) cases; hemoglobin genes were the top hits overall (p about 1e-10).
- Of ten Alzheimer risk genes, only [[PLCG2]] (both diseases) and [[SORL1]] (Alzheimer alone) differed from controls; [[APP]], [[CD33]], [[CASS4]], [[TREM2]], [[INPP5D]] and PILRA did not when diseases were split. Among stress genes, [[SQSTM1]] rose in both diseases and FKBP5, [[ZBTB16]] and [[CALCOCO2]] in Alzheimer alone.
- Of nine other neurotransmitter receptor genes (DRD1, DRD2, GHSR, OPRM1, OPRD1, OPRK1, HTR1A, HTR2A, GLP2R), none changed as clearly as GLP1R. GLP1R lies near a telomere in rat, chimpanzee and human and has a longer transcript in chimpanzee; OPRM1 has an unusually long human transcript; OPRK1 and HTR1A looked genomically stable.
- The authors read the pattern as a compensatory but insufficient response to oxidative stress and mitochondrial impairment, with lower GLP1R signalling weakening SLC25A6 and driving SLC9A9, and suggest GLP-1 receptor agonists (liraglutide, exenatide, lixisenatide trials are cited) could act through mitochondrial restoration.

## Assessment

- Strengths: human postmortem tissue from a region (caudate nucleus) rarely sampled, two diseases compared in one design, and the authors state plainly that the work is exploratory.
- Sample size is 5, 6 and 3 donors, with two sequencing batches (the preliminary screen had 3 per group). Only 12 of 52 genes ranked in the top 100 in the first session stayed significant after the second, and one control resembled disease samples.
- No multiple-testing correction is described for 62,704 loci; the methods say non-parametric tests were chosen but then describe t-tests and Welch ANOVA, and one figure legend names the Mann-Whitney test. Direction of change for GLP1R, SLC25A6 and SLC9A9 is stated inconsistently between sections, and several figure panel references in the discussion do not match the legends.
- PLCG2 is read as microglial activation, and the introduction frames a bacterial infection hypothesis, yet bulk tissue cannot assign cell type and no microbial data were collected. Alzheimer and Parkinson groups also differ in size, age and sex balance were not modelled, and the 14 samples cannot support biomarker claims without the planned qPCR and protein validation.
- The telomere-proximity and transcript-length analysis is a descriptive proxy for mutability, not a measured mutation rate.

## Open Questions

- Do the higher NFKB1 and NFE2L2 transcript levels in caudate nucleus replicate in a larger cohort of Alzheimer and Parkinson postmortem brains (the study has 14 donors and an admitted batch effect)?
- Is the roughly 10-fold lower HBA1 RNA in Alzheimer caudate nucleus a neuronal signal or a consequence of blood content and postmortem interval (not tested in the study)?
- Does lower GLP1R transcript in caudate nucleus correspond to lower receptor protein or signalling (protein assays are described only as planned work)?
- Which cell types carry the higher PLCG2 and NFKB1 signal (bulk RNA-seq cannot resolve it; single-nucleus data from caudate nucleus would be needed)?

## Why This Source Matters

It is a small exploratory transcriptome of caudate nucleus tissue that puts Alzheimer and Parkinson disease side by side and points to a shared TNF and NF-κB inflammatory signal with mitochondrial and hemoglobin gene changes. It supplies caudate-specific expression data for NFKB1 and GLP1R, a region that most Alzheimer expression studies cover with cortex only, and a hypothesis for why GLP-1 receptor agonists may help both diseases.
