# Amyloid-beta and tau interplay in Alzheimer disease review

A narrative review of what is known about [[Amyloid-beta]] and tau pathology in [[Alzheimer disease]], and of the evidence that the two act together. The authors end on the position that the two pathologies are equally important and synergistic, acting in parallel through routes beyond the [[Amyloid cascade hypothesis]], which puts amyloid upstream of tau, while still calling amyloid the primary driver.

## Amyloid-beta

- Processing: sequential cleavage of [[APP]] by [[BACE1]] and then gamma-secretase gives Aβ40 or the more aggregation-prone Aβ42, plus sAPPβ and the APP intracellular domain; [[ADAM10]] cleavage inside the Aβ sequence gives sAPPα and C83 and is neuroprotective. Soluble monomers form fibrils, protofibrils and oligomers; oligomers (AβOs) are held to be the main neurotoxic species, extracellular ones disrupting synapses early and intraneuronal ones adding endoplasmic reticulum stress.
- Blood: circulating AβO levels correlated with reduced brain volume, including white matter, in a cited study, with no causal link shown; the authors speculate that blood oligomers may enter the brain.
- [[Dystrophic neurite]]: swollen presynaptic processes round plaques, rich in BACE1. AβO exposure of mouse primary neurons produced beads-on-a-string neurites, and neurites in patients and [[5xFAD mouse]] animals held disrupted microtubules, mislocalised dynein and kinesin, and defective autophagy and lysosomes. The authors infer that oligomers drive their formation and mark this as untested.
- Astrocytes: reactive astrocytes clear oligomers and neurites ([[Astrocyte activation]]), but in APP/PS1 hippocampus amyloid-beta lowered their phagocytic receptors [[MEGF10]] and [[MERTK]], leaving more oligomers in a proposed vicious cycle.
- Synaptic binding and resilience: hippocampal proteomics of [[Non-demented with Alzheimer neuropathology]] cases found a distinct post-synaptic proteome shaped by miR-149, miR-485 and miR-4723, which reduced Aβ binding to synaptic terminals in mice. In temporal cortex AβOs have candidate postsynaptic receptors in [[TMEM97]], PrPc ([[PRNP]]) and PSD-95 ([[DLG4]]), and binding to TMEM97 altered gene transcription in APP/PS1 mice and stem cell-derived human neurons.

## Tau

- Tau stabilises microtubules and axonal transport; [[Tau hyperphosphorylation]] detaches it, and it assembles into filaments and [[Neurofibrillary tangle]]. Early post-mortem work found tau phosphorylation three to fourfold higher in Alzheimer brain.
- Kinases: [[PRKACB]] (repressed by miR-200a-3p, [[MIR200A]], which is lowered in Alzheimer models and patient blood); [[FYN]], which phosphorylates Tyr18 and amplifies [[Excitotoxicity]] through NMDA receptor calcium influx; [[GSK3B]], restrained when soluble [[TREM2]] binds [[TAGLN2]] and inactivates [[RHOA]]-ROCK, with soluble TREM2 or TREM2 overexpression protective in tau P301S mice; and [[CDK5]], driven in mice by a high-salt diet through reduced endothelial nitric oxide, calpain-2 and p35.
- Phosphatases: [[PPM1B]] dephosphorylates Ser258 of [[DYRK1A]] and so blocks tau phosphorylation at Thr212 (DYRK1A sits on chromosome 21, relevant to [[Down syndrome]]); [[Protein phosphatase 2A]] activity falls by half in Alzheimer brain, and [[Prostaglandin A1]] activated it by a covalent bond at Cys377 of subunit A, lowering tau phosphorylation in P301S mice and, at 2.5 mg/kg/day for 6 months, averting cognitive decline.

## Amyloid-beta and tau together

- In APP/PS1+tau mice there were 1,531 differentially expressed transcripts, more than in the single-pathology lines: upregulated glial neuroinflammation genes were driven largely by either pathology alone, while downregulated synaptic genes needed both, and lowering tau halted the changes and the hyperactive phenotype. The authors judge the model weak because it overexpresses tau without showing phosphorylation or aggregation.
- Crossing APP/PS1 with rTg4510 mice: amyloid alone gave cortical hyperactivity at 6 to 12 months, tau alone silenced neurons (3.6 to 5.8-fold more silent cells, though only 1.21% of layer 2/3 neurons held tangles), and the cross showed profound silencing, with tau predominating. Lowering tau did not rescue silencing in the presence of amyloid.
- Aβ42 injected into P301L mice raised tangles fivefold with phosphorylation at Ser212, Thr214 and Ser422. Human tau paired helical filaments injected into [[5xFAD mouse]] cortex caused a working-memory deficit and oligomerisation of endogenous 4R tau in dystrophic neurites close to plaques, with higher CDK5 activity.
- In human imaging, tangles start in the entorhinal cortex and [[Tau propagation]] accelerates at the prodromal stage with the inferior temporal gyrus as hub; a remote mode (across synapses, lateral entorhinal cortex) and a local mode (co-mingling, inferior temporal gyrus) of amyloid-tau interaction were proposed. Depolarised synaptosomes released exosome-encapsulated oligomeric tau that seeded a FRET biosensor, with oligomeric Aβ42 correlating ([[Extracellular vesicle]]); the authors' exosome model of amyloid deposition and tau spread is a hypothesis.

## Immunotherapy

- [[AADvac1]], a tau vaccine, in a post hoc analysis of the phase 2 ADAMANT trial (119 of 196 patients): MRI machine-learning models predicted amyloid-positive and tau-positive status with positive predictive values of 97.7% and 96.2%, plasma [[NEFL]] was lower (p = 0.0139), and antibody response was associated with reduced atrophy and with CDR-SB and activities-of-daily-living scores.
- [[CAD106]], an amyloid vaccine, and the BACE1 inhibitor [[Umibecestat]] in Generation Study 1 (60 to 75-year-olds at genetic risk, stopped early): CAD106 raised amyloid-beta IgG in 41 of 42, lowered the plaque formation rate (P < 0.001) and three cases of [[Amyloid-related imaging abnormalities]] occurred. See [[Anti-amyloid antibody therapy]].

## Why This Source Matters

It collects in one frame the molecular routes to tau phosphorylation, the postsynaptic biology of amyloid oligomers and the mouse and human evidence that amyloid and tau pathology amplify each other, and it states the case that the two run in parallel as well as in sequence. The kinase and phosphatase sections name specific pairs (a kinase and the factor that restrains it) that can be followed to primary studies, and the closing trial summaries show where tau and amyloid vaccines stood.

## Assessment

- A narrative review with no stated search strategy or inclusion criteria; most claims rest on one cited study each, largely in mice or cultured cells, and the review repeatedly marks mechanisms as unknown.
- The text mixes reported results with the authors' own postulates (flagged by "we postulate"), for example the circulating-oligomer entry route, the exosome model and the oligomer origin of dystrophic neurites.
- The conclusion that amyloid and tau are equally important and parallel sits beside the statement that amyloid remains the primary driver; an earlier passage says tangle pathology plays a more significant role than plaques on the basis of "mounting evidence" not itemised there.
- The ADAMANT result is a post hoc subset (119 of 196) and the direction of the CDR-SB and activities-of-daily-living associations is not stated clearly; Generation Study 1 was terminated early. Both are small, hypothesis-generating signals.
- The text is written in the review's own words; the review describes the Aβ and tau interaction as speculative on mechanism throughout.

## Open Questions

- What links amyloid-beta plaques to tau phosphorylation and spread at the molecular level (the review states the mechanisms are largely unknown and that the cited work was observational or transcriptomic)?
- Does amyloid-beta in the blood form oligomers that enter the brain and seed plaques (the cited study found a correlation with brain volume and no causal evidence)?
- Which upstream factors lower miR-200a-3p in Alzheimer disease and so release the tau kinase PRKACB (not explored in the cited work)?
- How does a high-salt diet reduce nitric oxide production by cerebral endothelial cells and so activate the calpain-2 and CDK5 route to tau phosphorylation (the review calls the mechanism unknown)?
- Do exosomes carrying tau and amyloid-beta account for the spread of tau between cortical neurons in living brain (the exosome model is the authors' hypothesis built on synaptosome experiments)?
