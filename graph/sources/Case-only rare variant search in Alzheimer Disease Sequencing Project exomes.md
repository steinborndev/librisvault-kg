# Case-only rare variant search in Alzheimer Disease Sequencing Project exomes

A genetic association study of whole-exome data from the Alzheimer's Disease Sequencing Project: 5,617 people with [[Alzheimer disease]] (57.0% women, mean age 76.4 years) and 4,594 cognitively normal controls (59.0% women, mean age 86.5 years), all of non-Hispanic European ancestry. Instead of testing association, the authors listed moderate- or high-impact rare variants present in cases but absent in controls, then looked for support in Utah pedigrees and in whole-genome data.

## Why This Source Matters

It shows that a case-only filter can surface rare coding variants that conventional association tests cannot score, and that two of them lie in genes known for other dementias: [[NOTCH3]] ([[CADASIL]]) and [[TREM2]] ([[Nasu-Hakola disease]]). The authors read this as evidence that different mutations in one gene, or different doses of one mutation, can yield different dementias, and propose the NOTCH3 and TREM2 variants as possible fully penetrant causes of late-onset disease.

## Methods

- Exomes from the [[Alzheimer's Disease Sequencing Project]]; participants were selected by a risk score from age, sex and APOE ε4 status. Two analyses: all genes, and 95 genes previously tied to Alzheimer disease, related traits or other adult-onset dementias.
- Independent exomes of 19 affected first- or second-cousin pairs from high-risk Utah pedigrees, filtered to 130 variants; whole-genome data from 1,432 cases and 1,660 controls (ADSP Extension), 550 cases and 283 controls (family study) and 809 Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative participants.
- Haplotypes with PLINK and Haploview, identity-by-descent sharing, homology modelling of NOTCH3 (SWISS-MODEL, Maestro), a STRING interaction network for NOTCH3 and [[JAG1]] with PANTHER enrichment, and a gene-set burden test by Combined and Multivariate Collapsing with covariates sex, age, sequencing centre and ancestry components. See [[Whole-exome sequencing]] and [[Case-only rare variant analysis]].

## Findings

- Across the genome, 24 moderate- or high-impact variants in 19 genes occurred in 10 or more cases and no controls.
- NOTCH3: [[NOTCH3 p.A284T]] (rs149307620, allele frequency 0.0005) was in 10 cases and no controls, verified by Sanger sequencing in 8. Carriers shared a 5-SNP haplotype (15% in cases, 14% in controls), so the mutation likely arose in a distant common ancestor; 8 of 10 clustered in a minor ancestry cluster with mitochondrial haplogroups K1a1b1a or K1a9, common in Ashkenazi Jewish people (8 of 358 cases against 0 of 337 controls, P = .006), and the allele is about 25 times more frequent in Ashkenazi Jewish people (0.0046) than in other Europeans (0.00019). Whole-genome data added one case (onset 89) and one person with [[Mild cognitive impairment]] (onset 76), no controls.
- The carriers looked like Alzheimer disease, not CADASIL: mean onset 80.5 years (similar to the 80.9 of all ADSP cases, against usually below 50 in CADASIL), prominent memory loss, no clinical strokes; the one autopsy showed Alzheimer pathology (Braak stage VI) with moderate atherosclerosis but no arteriosclerosis, lacunes or microinfarcts.
- Four more NOTCH3 variants: [[NOTCH3 p.P2074Q]] (4 cases; common, probably benign); [[NOTCH3 p.G248A]] and [[NOTCH3 p.A198E]] (both in an affected half-first-cousin pair in Utah, frequency 0.0002, not found in whole-genome data); [[NOTCH3 p.H1133Q]] (an affected cousin pair in a second family; frequency 0.010, seen in controls of other data sets).
- Modelling put p.A284T in the EGF calcium-binding region near the JAG1 binding site and predicted extra hydrogen bonding and stronger ligand contact, whereas the CADASIL change [[NOTCH3 p.R332C]] adds a cysteine and disrupts disulfide bonding. The 30-gene NOTCH3/JAG1 network contains [[APP]], [[BACE1]], [[PSEN1]] and [[PSEN2]] and was enriched for [[Notch signaling]] (P = 6.48 x 10^-49), angiogenesis (1.61 x 10^-12), secretase-mediated APP cleavage (3.50 x 10^-16) and the presenilin gamma-secretase complex (5.78 x 10^-26).
- [[TREM2 p.Q33X]] (rs104894002) was heterozygous in 4 of 5,617 cases (0.071%) and no controls; none had bone cysts or unusual behaviour. Homozygotes have Nasu-Hakola disease, and both obligate heterozygous parents of one patient showed amyloid deposition on CSF and PET, so the authors favour an intermediate inheritance model.
- Previously reported AD variants recovered among 32 variants in 24 known genes (each in 4 or more cases, no controls): [[PSEN1 p.A75V]] (n = 7), [[PSEN1 p.R35Q]] (n = 4), [[SORL1 p.Y391C]] (n = 5), [[MAPT p.R741W]] (n = 4) and [[ABCA7 p.E1679X]] (n = 4).
- Novel candidate genes: 10 cases carried three [[ABCD4]] missense variants ([[ABCD4 p.G59R]], [[ABCD4 p.V172I]] and a third) on one rare 8-SNP haplotype of 12.9 kb (0.3% of cases, 0% of controls); 10 cases carried two [[CELSR1]] variants ([[CELSR1 p.P2983A]] and one more), 8 of them also two [[GTSE1]] variants ([[GTSE1 p.A219V]] and a synonymous one) on a 77.6 kb haplotype, with one person homozygous for all four. Identity-by-descent sharing in these groups was no higher than in the sample overall. [[LAMC3]] had three variants in the same 7 cases and [[TTN]] six distinct case-only variants; 9 genes not previously linked to the disease had a high-impact variant in at least 7 cases and no controls.
- Burden: cases carried significantly more moderate- and high-impact rare variants in dementia-associated genes than controls (P = .006).

## Assessment

- Case-only counts cannot reach significance; the authors say so, were unable to replicate in the Alzheimer's Disease Genetics Consortium GWAS because rare-variant imputation was poor, and warn that cutoffs were arbitrary and some calls were not Sanger-verified.
- Controls were about 10 years older than cases, which makes absence in controls more informative, but the selection by APOE ε4 status and a risk score shapes who is a case and who a control.
- The burden result is printed as "2314 vs 3354 cumulative variants, respectively", which, read in the order cases then controls, would put the larger count in controls; the direction of the sentence and the counts do not obviously agree.
- Typos in the text: rs11219217 for rs112197217 (H1133Q); the ABCD4 list includes "p.G248" and "p.P2983A", the latter being the CELSR1 change; the CELSR1 variant appears as "p.2983A" and "p.2703M". The 8 versus 10 NOTCH3 carriers (Sanger versus exome) and the total of 11 cases after adding whole-genome data are consistent once the data sets are separated.
- The claim that p.A284T and TREM2 p.Q33X may be fully penetrant causes of late-onset disease is called speculative by the authors, resting on very few documented carriers; the structural prediction is a homology model with no functional assay.

## Open Questions

- Does NOTCH3 p.A284T raise Notch signalling or amyloid precursor protein processing in a cell model (only homology modelling was done)?
- Is NOTCH3 p.A284T enriched in Alzheimer cases of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry in an independent cohort (the signal came from 8 carriers in one ancestry cluster)?
- Do ABCD4, CELSR1 and GTSE1 variants raise Alzheimer risk when tested in a larger exome data set (the haplotypes were found by a case-only screen with no significance test)?
- Do heterozygous TREM2 p.Q33X carriers develop biomarker evidence of amyloid deposition earlier than non-carriers (the evidence so far is two parents of one Nasu-Hakola patient)?
- How is the burden of rare deleterious variants in dementia genes distributed between cases and controls (the printed counts of 2314 and 3354 do not clearly state which group is which)?
