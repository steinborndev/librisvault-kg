# White matter injury, cholesterol and amyloid in Alzheimer disease review

## Scope

A narrative hypothesis-and-review paper (Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience, 2023). It proposes that [[White matter injury]], [[Cholesterol dysmetabolism]] and [[Amyloid-beta]] dysmetabolism form a three-way vicious cycle that produces or worsens [[Alzheimer disease]] neuropathology. It is explicitly "not an exhaustive review" and reports no new experiments; the one first-hand observation is the authors' own LPS-hypoxia-ischemia rat model.

## The model

- Age is named the main initiator of white matter injury, joined by [[APOE]] genotype, systemic factors and AD risk genes. Injured myelin releases cholesterol and degraded [[MBP]] (dMBP).
- Raised cellular cholesterol lowers [[LRP1]] and raises [[AGER]] (RAGE) at the blood-brain barrier, so amyloid-beta leaves the brain less and enters it more. Cholesterol also binds [[APP]], aggregates amyloid-beta, and with dMBP seeds plaques; dMBP competes with amyloid-beta for LRP1-mediated removal.
- Amyloid-beta in turn kills mature oligodendrocytes and blocks myelin sheet formation, closing the loop. Cholesterol and amyloid-beta also feed [[Tau hyperphosphorylation]] and vascular injury.
- Three entry routes are proposed: white matter injury (age, systemic factors), cholesterol dysmetabolism (APOE ε4, diet) and amyloid-beta dysmetabolism (familial genes). The authors argue the failure of amyloid-lowering and tau-lowering trials motivates looking upstream.

## Evidence assembled

- Human imaging: [[Diffusion tensor imaging]] shows myelin change from about age 31, worse in [[APOE]] ε4 carriers; fractional anisotropy falls from [[Mild cognitive impairment]] to Alzheimer disease (a 26-study, 1,021-patient meta-analysis); abnormal white matter precedes plaques and tangles in [[Familial Alzheimer disease]] carriers (greater [[White matter hyperintensity]] volume about 6 years before predicted onset, parietal and occipital change up to 22 years before) and in cognitively normal APOE ε4 carriers and people with a parental history. [[Down syndrome]] shows early change in late-myelinating tracts.
- Neuropathology and lipids: sulfatide depleted by up to 93% in grey matter and 58% in white matter in mild dementia, ceramide up three-fold in white matter, decreased [[CERS2]] activity at Braak stage I/II, and cholesterol down over 70% in Alzheimer white matter.
- Rodents: myelin abnormalities precede plaques in [[3xTg-AD mouse]], [[5xFAD mouse]], APP/PS1 and Tg2576 mice; a LINGO-1 antibody, M1 receptor deletion in oligodendroglia and [[Clemastine]] restored myelin and memory in APP/PS1 or 5xFAD mice without lowering plaque load.
- Cholesterol handling: hypercholesterolemia lowers LRP1 and raises RAGE via Wnt/β-catenin signalling at the promoters; brain export through [[CYP46A1]], [[ABCA1]] and [[ABCG1]] is reduced in Alzheimer disease (−73% and −33% for ABCA1 and ABCG1 transport to CSF); blood-derived [[27-Hydroxycholesterol]] enters the brain and promotes amyloid accumulation while [[24S-Hydroxycholesterol]] exits it; ACAT/[[SOAT1]] inhibition and [[CYP27A1]] shifts in oligodendrocytes and around plaques are described.
- Mechanisms of white matter injury: age, [[APOE]] ε4 (astrocyte-to-oligodendrocyte cholesterol transfer impaired), amyloid-beta, [[Hypertension]], [[Type 2 diabetes mellitus]], [[Lipopolysaccharide]], pericyte loss with fibrinogen deposition, oxidative stress (myelin loss in Nrf2 knockout mice, see [[NFE2L2]]) and an oligodendrocyte Drp1-HK1-NLRP3 inflammatory pathway ([[DNM1L]], [[HK1]], [[NLRP3]]).
- Intact MBP is said to bind and degrade amyloid-beta, so plaques form only where myelin is degraded; MBP-null shiverer mice make more amyloid-beta but almost no plaques.

## Assessment

- The causal direction, white matter injury before plaques, is best supported in autosomal dominant familial disease and in rodents; the authors themselves call the evidence in late-onset disease "less convincing", and cannot exclude that white matter change follows grey matter or axonal injury.
- Much of the cholesterol mechanism rests on mouse and cell-culture work, often one group's; LRP1 and RAGE changes in endothelial cells are extrapolated to human brain, and the quantitative figures are quoted from other papers without appraisal.
- Contradictions are acknowledged but not resolved: total brain cholesterol falls in Alzheimer white matter while "free" cholesterol bound to APOE is postulated to rise (not measured); statins lower incidence but not course of disease; NSAIDs likewise.
- Narrative selection of literature, no search method, and an author-group model (LPS-ischemia rat) used as supporting evidence. The paper is a hypothesis, not a test of one.

## Open Questions

- Does lowering white matter injury (for example with a pro-myelinating drug such as clemastine) delay plaque and tau pathology in people with a high familial or APOE ε4 risk (only rodent data cited; no human trial of this design)?
- Is "free" cholesterol bound to APOE actually raised in Alzheimer white matter while total cholesterol falls (postulated, not measured in the cited studies)?
- How do microglia handle cholesterol released from damaged myelin, and does APOE ε4 change that handling in human brain (mostly mouse and iPSC evidence so far)?
- Does preventing myelin injury prevent Alzheimer disease (listed by the authors as unanswered)?

## Why This Source Matters

It supplies a single organising hypothesis that ties together cholesterol transport genes ([[APOE]], [[LRP1]], [[ABCA1]], [[ABCG1]], [[CYP46A1]]), amyloid-beta transport receptors, myelin biology and systemic risk factors such as hypertension and diabetes, and gives a reason why myelin-directed and cholesterol-directed treatments are credible alternatives to amyloid removal. It is a useful map of what is known about white matter in Alzheimer disease and where it is thin.
