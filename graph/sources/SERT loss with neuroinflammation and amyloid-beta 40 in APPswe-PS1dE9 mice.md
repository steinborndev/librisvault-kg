# SERT loss with neuroinflammation and amyloid-beta 40 in APPswe-PS1dE9 mice

Trials and animal work give conflicting answers on whether [[Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors]] help or harm people with [[Alzheimer disease]]. The authors ask whether the target, the serotonin transporter encoded by [[SLC6A4]] (SERT), changes in density and activity as amyloid builds up, and whether neuroinflammation or amyloid-beta itself is responsible. They followed 113 [[APPswe-PS1dE9 mouse]] animals and wild-type littermates (109 females) from 3 to 24 months and measured SERT density, SERT-mediated serotonin uptake, [[TSPO]] binding as a microglial marker, and soluble Aβ and cytokines in cortex.

## Design

- Animals: APPswe/PS1dE9 transgenic and wild-type littermates on a C57BL/6J background, killed at 3, 6, 12, 18 and 24 months (autoradiography, 6 per group) or 3, 6, 12 and 20 months (uptake, cytokines and Aβ).
- SERT density by [3H]DASB autoradiography; TSPO density by [3H]PK11195 autoradiography on adjacent sections; Slc6a4 mRNA by RT-qPCR in brainstem, normalised to Hprt. See [[SERT and TSPO radioligand autoradiography and uptake assays]].
- SERT activity: saturation kinetics (Vmax, Km) of [3H]serotonin uptake in freshly prepared cortical membranes; the same supernatant gave soluble Aβ40, Aβ42, [[IL1B]], [[IL6]] and [[TNF]] by Meso Scale Discovery immunoassay.
- Direct test: 100 nM synthetic human Aβ40 or Aβ42 (a concentration chosen from the 20-month transgenic cortex) added to forebrain membranes of 3-month-old wild-type mice, plus [3H]DASB displacement by Aβ over 0.01 pM to 10 μM.
- Statistics: three-way and two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post hoc tests, Pearson correlation between TSPO and SERT changes, t tests for the peptide experiments.

## Findings

- **SERT density.** Cortical [3H]DASB binding was lower in transgenic mice, in frontal cortex at 24 months and in parietal cortex at 18 and 24 months; occipital and temporal cortex did not differ. Slc6a4 mRNA in brainstem did not differ by genotype or age, which the authors read as loss of serotonergic terminals and not reduced transporter expression.
- **SERT activity.** Vmax of serotonin uptake was lower in 20-month-old transgenic than wild-type cortex with no change in Km; the Vmax/Km efficiency drifted down with age in transgenic mice but not significantly between single groups.
- **Neuroinflammation.** [3H]PK11195 binding rose with age in both genotypes and more in transgenic mice (frontal at 12 and 24 months, parietal at 18 and 24 months). IL-1β protein was higher in transgenic cortex from 12 months and rose most at 20 months; IL-6 and TNF rose with age irrespective of genotype. TSPO binding was inversely correlated with SERT density in parietal cortex only. These increases came before the SERT decline.
- **Soluble Aβ.** Soluble Aβ40 and Aβ42 rose with age only in transgenic mice, and the Aβ40 share of the Aβ40:Aβ42 ratio increased at 20 months, the age at which uptake fell.
- **Aβ40 inhibits uptake.** Aβ40 lowered baseline Vmax from 1832 ± 79 to 1490 ± 91 fmol/min/mg (about 20%) without changing Km; Aβ42 had no effect. Neither peptide displaced [3H]DASB, so the authors favour non-competitive inhibition of transport and not competition at the ligand site.
- **No cytokine effect on SERT.** The authors found no sign that the cytokines altered SERT binding, and note their cortical levels were at least tenfold below those used in earlier in vitro studies that did.

## Assessment

- Almost all animals were female and each readout rests on 5 to 7 animals per group, so sex effects and small group sizes limit the age-by-genotype contrasts.
- SERT density and uptake are measured in cortex only; Slc6a4 mRNA was measured in whole brainstem, so a raphe-subregion change in expression cannot be ruled out.
- The Aβ40 effect was shown on wild-type membranes with synthetic peptide at one concentration; the preparation conditions for the peptide (aggregation state) were not characterised, and no in vivo test (for example microdialysis or SSRI response) followed.
- Uptake and Aβ were measured at 20 months, while autoradiography ran to 24 months, so the two SERT readouts do not cover the same ages.
- The suggestion that anti-amyloid therapy would work better than SSRIs in late-stage disease is an inference, not tested.

## Why This Source Matters

It offers one concrete reason for the mixed SSRI results in Alzheimer disease: in a familial-type amyloid mouse, the transporter itself shrinks in the cortex late in disease, and soluble Aβ40 directly lowers its uptake capacity, so SSRI response may depend on the amyloid stage. It also shows microglial TSPO signal and IL-1β rising before serotonergic markers fall.

## Open Questions

- Does Aβ40 inhibit serotonin transporter uptake in living brain tissue of APPswe-PS1dE9 mice (only shown in isolated membranes from wild-type mice with synthetic peptide)?
- Does the response of APPswe-PS1dE9 mice to chronic SSRI treatment differ between early amyloidosis, when SERT density is high, and late amyloidosis, when it has fallen?
- Which step of the serotonin transporter cycle does Aβ40 block, given that it did not displace the [3H]DASB ligand?
- Is the early rise in TSPO binding in parietal cortex a useful predictor of later serotonergic terminal loss in people (the data are from mice only)?
