# TNF-alpha neuronal necroptosis via p62-RIPK1 and UVRAG in Alzheimer models

A Theranostics paper that asks what switches on [[Necroptosis]] in neurons of [[Alzheimer disease]]. Using human cortex, [[APP-PS1 mouse]] and [[5xFAD mouse]] brain, three neuronal culture systems and viral gene manipulation in the mouse hippocampus, it assembles a chain: [[TNF]] acting on [[TNFRSF1A]] (TNFR1), loss of NF-κB-driven [[UVRAG]] transcription, stalled [[Autophagy]] with [[SQSTM1]] (p62) build-up, p62 binding to [[RIPK1]], and the [[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]] cascade.

## Why This Source Matters

Necroptosis markers were already known to be raised in Alzheimer brain, but nothing explained what starts it. This work names an upstream trigger (TNF-α, elevated in cerebrospinal fluid and plasma of patients) and links it to a second, independent hit, failing autophagic flux, so that cell death, inflammation and protein clearance become one pathway rather than separate hallmarks. It also offers two intervention points in an APP/PS1 mouse (p62 knockdown, UVRAG overexpression) and reports that UVRAG overexpression improved water-maze performance. The causal chain is built mostly from a drug-induced culture model rather than from Alzheimer tissue (see Assessment).

## Design

- Human tissue: paraffin cortex sections from the NIH NeuroBioBank, 8 Alzheimer patients (Braak VI) and 7 age-matched controls, stained for MLKL, phospho-MLKL and p62.
- Mice: 10-month-old APP/PS1 and 5×FAD animals against wild-type littermates; stereotaxic injection of 5 μg recombinant murine TNF-α into the lateral ventricle, and intrahippocampal CA1 injection of anti-TNFR1 neutralizing antibody or AAV carrying TNFR1 shRNA, p62 shRNA or UVRAG (read out 3 weeks later, n = 3 to 5 mice per group).
- Cells: human [[SH-SY5Y]], rat [[PC12]] and primary mouse cortical neurons. Necroptosis was induced with TNF-α (40 ng/ml) plus [[Smac mimetic]] and [[Z-VAD-FMK]] (the "TSZ" combination), with [[Necrostatin-1]] and [[Chloroquine]] as inhibitors and lentiviral, adenoviral or AAV vectors for knockdown and overexpression.
- Readouts: cell viability (CCK-8), propidium iodide and acridine orange staining, immunoblotting of soluble and urea-soluble fractions, co-immunoprecipitation, GFP-mCherry-[[MAP1LC3B]] flux reporter, RT-qPCR, promoter analysis (ConTra v2) with RelA ChIP-qPCR, and the [[Morris water maze]]. Statistics were t tests and one- or two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post-tests.

## Findings

- Human and mouse brain: MLKL and phospho-MLKL were higher in Alzheimer cortex than in controls, and phospho-MLKL was greatly raised in CA1 of APP/PS1 and 5×FAD mice (stronger in 5×FAD), with total MLKL only mildly raised.
- TNF-α is sufficient and needed in vivo: 5 μg TNF-α raised phospho-MLKL about 3-fold in wild-type CA1 (only about 1.47-fold in APP/PS1, already high) and caused about 13.75% neuronal loss in 10-month wild-type mice. In APP/PS1 mice TNFR1 shRNA lowered phospho-MLKL by 55.5% and the neutralizing antibody by 45.5%.
- Culture model: TNF-α alone lowered viability by 16.4% (SH-SY5Y), 21.5% (PC12) and 22.4% (primary neurons) and mildly raised phospho-MLKL; the TSZ combination gave necrosis in about 51 to 54% of cells, with propidium iodide uptake, cell swelling and a sharp rise in phospho-MLKL. TNF-α with Smac mimetic activated [[CASP8]] and [[CASP3]] (apoptosis), and Z-VAD-FMK blocked this and pushed cells to necroptosis.
- RIPK1 dependence: TSZ raised phospho-RIPK1 (Ser166) and phospho-RIPK3, shifted RIPK1 into the urea-insoluble (aggregated) fraction, and Necrostatin-1 or RIPK1 shRNA blocked RIPK3/MLKL phosphorylation and cell death.
- p62 as scaffold: p62 co-precipitated with RIPK1, RIPK3 and MLKL under TSZ; p62 overexpression raised RIPK1 and phospho-RIPK1/MLKL, p62 knockdown reversed it, and a p62 lacking the ZZ domain (residues 122 to 167) could not rescue necroptosis in p62-knockdown cells. p62 was higher in Alzheimer cortex and APP/PS1 CA1, and p62 shRNA lowered phospho-MLKL by 47% in APP/PS1 CA1.
- Autophagic flux: TNF-α and more so TSZ raised LC3-II and p62 (including p62 in the insoluble fraction), cut red-only autolysosome puncta and raised yellow autophagosome puncta; chloroquine amplified p62, LC3-II, RIPK1/MLKL activation and cell death.
- UVRAG: among autophagosome-lysosome fusion and recognition genes, UVRAG mRNA fell the most with TSZ. A stable UVRAG-overexpressing SH-SY5Y line prevented the LC3-II and p62 rise, RIPK1/MLKL phosphorylation and cell death.
- Mechanism of UVRAG loss: TSZ lowered nuclear RelA (p65, see [[RELA]]) and its binding to a predicted NF-κB site in the Uvrag promoter by ChIP-qPCR, so Uvrag appears to be a direct target of TNF-α/TNFR1/[[NF-kappaB signaling]] that Smac mimetic (a cIAP1/2 inhibitor) switches off.
- Rescue in vivo: AAV-UVRAG in APP/PS1 CA1 lowered p62 by 34.5% and phospho-MLKL by 36.5% in transduced neurons, and improved water-maze learning and memory (groups of 6 to 8 mice) ([[Memory impairment]]).

## Assessment

- Most mechanistic steps (p62-RIPK1 binding, flux block, UVRAG loss, NF-κB inhibition) come from cultured cells under TSZ, which blocks caspases and cIAPs and is not a physiological stimulus. In Alzheimer tissue only MLKL, phospho-MLKL and p62 levels, and the effect of TNFR1, p62 and UVRAG manipulation on phospho-MLKL and p62, were measured; UVRAG levels in patient or mouse brain, p62-RIPK1 complexes in vivo and RIPK1 inhibition in mice were not shown.
- The TNF-α sufficiency test uses 5 μg intraventricularly, probably far above brain concentrations in disease, and the antibody and TNFR1 knockdown removed only about half of the phospho-MLKL signal, so TNF-α/TNFR1 is not the only input.
- The human data are immunohistochemistry on 8 versus 7 late-stage (Braak VI) cases, with no stated quantification or covariate adjustment, and the authors acknowledge conflicting literature that reports lower p62 in Alzheimer brain.
- Cell-level quantification is based on at least 15 to 20 labeled cells per group, with n = 3 to 5 mice or experiments; pooled cells risk inflating significance, and only 10-month-old animals and one behavioural test were used.
- Reporting slips: the Methods list the same Jackson stock number (034848) for APP/PS1 and 5×FAD; the legend of the Morris water maze panel lists "APP/PS1 group (injected with AAV-GFP)" twice, so the UVRAG group is not labelled; the abbreviation list gives "Necrostain-1".
- The authors view neuronal necroptosis as a later, secondary event after Aβ-driven neuroinflammation, consistent with limited early-stage efficacy of Nec-1, but timing was not tested.

## Open Questions

- Is UVRAG protein or mRNA reduced in post-mortem Alzheimer cortex and in APP/PS1 or 5×FAD hippocampus, as the TSZ culture model predicts (it was measured only in cultured cells)?
- Does the RIPK1 inhibitor Necrostatin-1, or a brain-penetrant RIPK1 inhibitor, lower phospho-MLKL and improve cognition in aged APP/PS1 mice (RIPK1 was manipulated only in cultured neurons)?
- Which inputs other than TNF-α/TNFR1 drive the remaining phospho-MLKL signal in APP/PS1 CA1 after TNFR1 knockdown or antibody blockade?
- Do p62 levels in Alzheimer brain rise or fall across disease stages, given that the Braak VI result here conflicts with several earlier reports (only end-stage cortex was stained)?
