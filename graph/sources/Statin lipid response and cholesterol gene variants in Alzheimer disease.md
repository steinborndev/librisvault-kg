# Statin lipid response and cholesterol gene variants in Alzheimer disease

A single-centre prospective pharmacogenetic cohort from the Universidade Federal de São Paulo (Brazil) that followed outpatients with late-onset [[Alzheimer disease]] dementia for one year on [[Statin]] therapy and asked whether variants in cholesterol-metabolism genes change the change in their lipid profile. See [[Pharmacogenetics]] for the study type.

## Design

- 217 consecutive outpatients with Alzheimer disease dementia (National Institute on Aging-Alzheimer's Association criteria) recruited October 2010 to May 2014; 14 died, 7 were lost to follow-up and 7 had incomplete data, leaving 189. Almost two-thirds were women, most had vascular risk factors, and most statin users took the lipophilic statins [[Simvastatin]] or [[Atorvastatin]]. Only two took rosuvastatin, so it was not analysed alone.
- Only the first and last of at least three visits were analysed. Statins were started if lifestyle measures failed after three months or if 10-year coronary risk exceeded 10%; many patients were already on treatment at entry.
- Genotyping by TaqMan real-time PCR for [[HMGCR rs3846662]], [[NR1H2 rs2695121]], [[LDLR rs11669576]], [[LDLR rs5930]], [[CETP rs5882]], [[CETP rs708272]], [[APOE rs7412]] and [[APOE rs429358]]; the [[ACE Alu insertion-deletion]] by conventional PCR. All variants were in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.
- Outcomes were one-year changes in triglycerides, total, LDL and HDL cholesterol (log-transformed). A general linear model with sex, baseline age and change in body mass index tested genotype by statin interactions; Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction was applied and results are shown with and without it. APOE was analysed as the number of ε4 alleles. The data set is public at Mendeley Data (doi 10.17632/6n7z6hytrj.1).

## Findings

- Across all patients body mass index, total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol were lower after a year of statin therapy; triglycerides, VLDL and HDL cholesterol did not change significantly. In the model, statin use lowered total and LDL cholesterol and raised HDL cholesterol, but the HDL rise did not survive false discovery rate correction.
- Simvastatin lowered total and LDL cholesterol and raised HDL cholesterol; atorvastatin lowered total, LDL and HDL cholesterol and produced smaller triglyceride variations than simvastatin.
- [[ACE]]: carriers of the Alu insertion allele had lower total and LDL cholesterol regardless of statin use (corrected P = 0.006 and 0.003), the only genotype effect independent of statin. By drug, insertion carriers had lower total and LDL cholesterol on simvastatin than atorvastatin and lower triglycerides on atorvastatin, whereas D/D carriers had lower triglycerides only on simvastatin.
- [[APOE]]: ε4 carriers (53.4% of the cohort) showed a larger HDL cholesterol rise on atorvastatin than non-carriers (corrected P = 0.011).
- [[LDLR]] rs11669576: G-allele carriers had lower total and LDL cholesterol on simvastatin, and lower total cholesterol and triglycerides on atorvastatin. The low variability of this SNP left some LDLR haplotypes under-represented, so only CETP haplotypes were analysed.
- [[CETP]]: rs5882 GG carriers had lower LDL cholesterol on atorvastatin than AA carriers, and both AA and GG had lower triglycerides on atorvastatin than simvastatin, more so for GG. rs708272 A-allele carriers had larger HDL increases on atorvastatin, while GG carriers had lower HDL. In haplotypes, those carrying A at both SNPs gained most from statins (lower total cholesterol, higher HDL) and from atorvastatin on triglycerides, whereas atorvastatin's lowering of total and LDL cholesterol was strongest for rs5882 GG with rs708272 GG.
- No lipid effect was reported for [[NR1H2]] rs2695121 or LDLR rs5930, and [[HMGCR]] rs3846662 could not be tested because all 189 patients were AA.

## Why This Source Matters

It is a rare pharmacogenetic look at lipid-lowering in people who already have Alzheimer disease, a group usually excluded from statin trials, and it points to the Alu insertion in ACE, APOE ε4 status and CETP haplotypes as candidate modifiers of which statin works best on which lipid fraction. It also supports stratifying by APOE ε4 status in dementia trials of lipid-modifying drugs.

## Assessment

- Uncontrolled, non-randomised convenience sample of 189 with no dietary data, no protein measures and many patients already on statins at entry, so baseline lipid status confounds the one-year changes.
- Many comparisons were made across variants, statins and lipid fractions; many footnoted P values are uncorrected (0.01 to 0.05), and the authors call the work preliminary.
- The text is not fully consistent: the results summary says atorvastatin lowered HDL cholesterol while the discussion says simvastatin was better at raising it, and the discussion reports no NR1H2 differences although table footnotes flag corrected within-genotype statin comparisons for that gene.
- No dosage, adherence or treatment duration before entry is analysed, and two patients on rosuvastatin were left out of drug-specific tests.
- The study was funded by CAPES and FAPESP, which had no role in the design or reporting.

## Open Questions

- Does the lower total and LDL cholesterol seen in carriers of the ACE Alu insertion allele replicate in a larger cohort of people with Alzheimer disease (the original had 189 patients and no control group without dementia)?
- Do APOE ε4 carriers with Alzheimer disease gain more HDL cholesterol on atorvastatin than non-carriers in a randomised comparison (the original was observational and many patients were already treated)?
- Does the HMGCR rs3846662 genotype modify statin response in a population where the variant is not monomorphic (all 189 patients were AA)?
- Do CETP rs5882 and rs708272 haplotypes predict triglyceride and LDL cholesterol response to atorvastatin independent of baseline lipid levels?
