# Sex-specific hippocampal transcriptomes in Alzheimer disease from GEO datasets

A secondary analysis of three public microarray datasets of postmortem human hippocampus ([[Alzheimer disease]] cases and aged controls) that compares men and women separately: whole hippocampus (GSE48350, GSE5281) and the CA1 and CA3 subfields (GSE29378). It asks which genes and pathways change in Alzheimer disease in one sex but not the other, and which Alzheimer susceptibility genes sit next to the estrogen receptors in a protein interaction network. See [[Sex differences in Alzheimer disease]].

## Design

- Datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus, chosen for hippocampal tissue and more than four female cases. GSE29378: CA1 with 8 female cases, 5 female controls, 11 male controls, 8 male cases; CA3 with 7, 5, 11 and 5. GSE5281 hippocampus: 4 female and 6 male cases, 3 female and 10 male controls. GSE48350 hippocampus: 10 female and 9 male cases, 12 female and 14 male aged controls (young controls dropped).
- Four contrasts per dataset: male controls vs female controls ("normomics"), female cases vs female controls, male cases vs male controls, and male cases vs female cases. Differential expression with [[Limma differential expression analysis]], keeping the top 200 up and top 200 down genes at an uncorrected p below 0.05 (few genes survived adjusted p in GSE29378); "opposite" genes were ranked by the absolute difference in log fold change between two contrasts.
- Enrichment of the 400-gene sets (and of up and down halves separately, to read direction) against KEGG, with cancer pathways dropped, and GO biological process, in Metascape.
- Network: 766 Alzheimer susceptibility genes from DisGeNET, 518 of which mapped into a [[STRING protein interaction network analysis]] (confidence 0.7; 518 nodes, 1119 edges); first neighbours of [[ESR1]], [[ESR2]] and [[GPER1]], the targets of [[Menopausal hormone therapy]], were extracted.

## Findings

- Baseline sex difference: in healthy controls of both subfields the neurodegeneration-disease KEGG cluster (Alzheimer, Parkinson, Huntington, ALS) was upregulated in men versus women, and [[BACE1]] was among the top upregulated genes in CA1 and CA3 (as was [[APP]] in CA3). The authors read this as a possibly higher baseline amyloid production in men and flag the unbalanced sex ratio.
- Direction of change in cases: the [[Estrogen signaling pathway]] was enriched and upregulated in male cases only (CA1 and CA3), read as a possible compensatory response to neurodegeneration (it held with the top 100 genes). The GABAergic synapse ([[GABA signaling pathway]]) and insulin secretion were downregulated in male cases only. In female cases the [[Dopamine receptor signaling pathway]] (dopaminergic synapse) was downregulated in both subfields, and [[Calcium signalling]] was up in CA1 but down in CA3; HIF-1 signaling and Epstein-Barr virus infection were female-specific in CA1.
- Shared by both sexes: the [[Interleukin-17 signaling pathway]] was upregulated in both subfields, MAPK signaling was enriched, glutamatergic synapse and oxytocin signaling were down in CA1, and TNF signaling and apoptosis were up in CA3.
- Genes: 16 genes were in the top 200 up genes in both subfields and both sexes, with [[RGS1]] the largest fold change and also [[CD163]] and [[CD44]] among them; [[EFHD2]] was the only shared top downregulated gene. [[SLC1A7]] was up in male cases and down in female cases in both subfields; TMEM10, MOG and MAL were up in female and down in male cases in CA3.
- Direct male versus female case contrast: [[ESR1]] was slightly higher in male CA1 (logFC +0.13, nominal p = 0.047), not in CA3; pathogen pathways (COVID-19, Legionellosis, Salmonella infection) and antigen processing and TNF signaling were lower in male than female cases in CA1.
- Whole hippocampus: results differed by dataset. In GSE5281 antigen processing and presentation was downregulated in female cases (read as impaired microglial surveillance) and circadian entrainment, axon guidance and sphingolipid signaling were down in male cases. In GSE48350 GABAergic and glutamatergic synapse, synaptic vesicle cycle and long-term depression were upregulated in female cases, and TGF-beta signaling was down in them; no top genes were shared across the four case groups of the two datasets.
- Interaction network: ESR1 neighbours included UBE2I, [[TP53]], SETD7, RPS6KB2, PPARG, PARP1, NTRK1, NOS3, MMP9, [[MAPK1]], [[IGF1]], ESR2, [[EGFR]], DROSHA, CTSD, [[CD44]], BRCA2, BCL2 and [[AKT1]]; ESR2 neighbours were CACNA1G, EGFR, ESR1, IGF1, MAPK1, RPS6KB2 and TP53; GPER1 touched ESR1, ESR2 and EGFR. The authors stress an estrogen, IGF1, AKT and MAPK neuroprotective axis as a route for hormone therapy.

## Why This Source Matters

It is one of few studies to split human hippocampal Alzheimer expression data by sex and by subfield, and it generates concrete hypotheses (male-specific estrogen-pathway upregulation with GABAergic loss, female-specific dopaminergic and calcium changes, a shared IL-17 signal) for the roughly two-to-one female excess of the disease. It also lists estrogen-receptor neighbours among known susceptibility genes as candidates for sex-aware pharmacogenetics of hormone therapy.

## Assessment

- Group sizes are small (as few as 3 to 5 samples in some groups), sex ratios are unbalanced, and gene lists use uncorrected p below 0.05 with only the top 400 genes enriched, so pathway calls are hypothesis-generating.
- Hormone therapy status of the women and Braak stage of all samples were unavailable, so estrogen-pathway findings cannot be tied to treatment or disease stage.
- The female and male case-versus-control DEG counts in GSE29378 are given as identical (2247 in CA1, 2790 in CA3), which looks like a reporting slip; the whole-hippocampus datasets disagree with each other on direction of GABAergic and other synaptic pathways.
- The ESR1 male-versus-female difference is small and nominal; the BACE1 baseline difference is expression only, with no protein or amyloid data.
- Bulk tissue does not separate cell types, so microglial interpretations (CD163, antigen presentation) are inferred.

## Open Questions

- Does the male-specific upregulation of the estrogen signaling pathway in hippocampal Alzheimer tissue replicate in an independent, larger cohort with balanced sexes and Braak staging (the three datasets here have as few as 3 to 5 samples per group)?
- Is the higher BACE1 expression in healthy male hippocampus reflected in BACE1 protein or activity (only mRNA was compared)?
- Which cell types carry the sex-specific GABAergic, dopaminergic and IL-17 pathway changes (the data are bulk microarray, not single-cell)?
