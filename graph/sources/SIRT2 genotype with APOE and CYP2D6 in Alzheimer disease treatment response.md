# SIRT2 genotype with APOE and CYP2D6 in Alzheimer disease treatment response

A review of the seven sirtuins in [[Alzheimer disease]] combined with an original genotype-phenotype study of 1086 patients with the disease. The study asks whether the [[SIRT2 rs10410544]] variant, alone and paired with [[APOE]] and [[CYP2D6]] status, shapes age at onset, blood and cardiovascular measures, baseline cognition and the cognitive response to a one-year multifactorial treatment.

## Design

- 1086 patients (625 women, 461 men; mean age about 71 years, range 50 to 98). SIRT2 rs10410544 (C/C, C/T, T/T), APOE genotype and CYP2D6 metabolizer class (extensive, intermediate, poor, ultra-rapid) were determined for each, then combined into bigenic clusters.
- Treatment given for one year: CDP-choline ([[Citicoline]]), piracetam, a lipid-lowering nutraceutical, an iron, [[Folic acid]] and vitamin B12 complex, plus [[Atorvastatin]], enalapril or metformin where hypertension, hypercholesterolemia or diabetes were present; psychotropic drugs were avoided. Cognition was scored by the Mini-Mental State Examination at baseline and after 1, 3, 6, 9 and 12 months.
- Tests: t-test, analysis of variance, chi-squared and Fisher exact tests, rank-sum tests, regression and correlation.

## Findings

- Genotype frequencies in these patients: SIRT2 C/C 34.72%, C/T 50.92%, T/T 14.36%. APOE: 3/3 56.35%, 3/4 29.38%, 2/3 7.64%, 4/4 4.61%, 2/4 1.84%, 2/2 0.18%. Combined, they give 18 bigenic genotypes; the commonest are 33CT (27.81%), 33CC (21.36%), 34CT (15.29%), 34CC (9.76%) and 33TT (7.18%).
- APOE 3/4 and 4/4 carriers tended to accumulate in T/T more than C/T more than C/C (not significant), and T/T and C/T carriers accumulated among APOE 4/4 patients.
- SIRT2 variants did not alter age at onset except for a tendency to earlier onset in T/T men; APOE ε4 carriers, especially 2/4 and 4/4, had earlier onset, and some bigenic genotypes differed significantly (for example 33CC against 44CT, p = 0.009).
- Few biochemical differences by SIRT2 genotype: higher total cholesterol in C/T than C/C (p = 0.05), and differences in calcium, liver enzymes, bilirubin, sodium, chloride, ferritin, folate and in leukocytes, neutrophils, lymphocytes and monocytes (mostly T/T against the others). Blood pressure and body mass index did not differ; abnormal electrocardiogram rates were 47%, 48% and 52% in C/C, C/T and T/T.
- Baseline Mini-Mental State Examination did not differ by SIRT2 genotype (about 20 in each group) but did by APOE genotype, and across many bigenic clusters.
- Cognition improved for the first 9 months of treatment, then declined. APOE 3/3 carriers responded best and ε4 carriers worst. By SIRT2 genotype, C/T carriers were the best responders (the only group to stay above baseline for a year), T/T intermediate and C/C worst. In bigenic clusters 33CC responded better than 33TT and 34CT, and 24CC and 44CC responded worst.
- CYP2D6 classes: extensive 59.46%, intermediate 20.06%, poor 5.36%, ultra-rapid 6.12%. Poor metabolizers had the lowest baseline scores and were the worst responders, extensive metabolizers the best, and ultra-rapid metabolizers tended to do better than intermediate ones. APOE 3/4 and 4/4 carriers were over-represented among poor metabolizers. Across the 12 SIRT2-CYP2D6 combinations, C/T extensive metabolizers responded best and CCIM, CCPM, CTPM and TTPM worst.
- From earlier work cited by the authors: [[TOMM40 rs10524523]] short poly-T carriers (S/S) responded best, L/L worst, and L/L occurred only with APOE 4/4.

## Review content

- SIRT1, SIRT3 and SIRT6 are described as neuroprotective and reduced in Alzheimer brain: [[SIRT1]] steers [[APP]] processing toward [[ADAM10]] and away from [[BACE1]], and its levels fall with disease stage and with APOE4; [[SIRT3]] protects mitochondria against p53-driven damage; [[SIRT6]] is reduced in Alzheimer brain, and Aβ42 lowers it.
- [[SIRT2]] is described as more ambiguous: raised in Alzheimer disease and linked to tubulin deacetylation and impaired autophagy, yet protective in some cell stress models. The T allele of rs10410544 is cited from earlier work as a 1.709-fold risk for late-onset disease in APOE ε4-negative people.
- A long list of [[Sirtuin modulators]] is surveyed, including [[Resveratrol]], [[Curcumin]], [[Honokiol]], [[Nicotinamide riboside]], [[Cilostazol]], [[Citicoline]] and [[Donepezil]], with mostly cell and mouse evidence. The authors note that no sirtuin drug has produced a breakthrough in Alzheimer disease.

## Why This Source Matters

It is one of few attempts to place a sirtuin variant beside [[APOE]] and [[CYP2D6]] when asking who benefits from standard Alzheimer treatment, and its message is modest: SIRT2 alone has a very mild effect, and the interactions matter more. It also gathers, in one place, the evidence connecting sirtuins to amyloid processing, tau, mitochondria and autophagy. See [[Pharmacogenetics]] for the wider design.

## Assessment

- The treated cohort has no placebo or untreated arm and no healthy control group, so responder rankings cannot be separated from natural course; a multi-drug regimen cannot tell which component drives any response.
- The "association" of rs10410544 with disease in the abstract rests on earlier case-control work; here only genotype frequencies in patients are given, and the accumulation of APOE ε4 carriers in T/T was not significant.
- Many bigenic comparisons are run on small cells (some clusters under 1% of 1086 patients) with p values near 0.05 and no stated multiple-testing correction.
- Descriptive numbers contain inconsistencies: the T/T male and female shares (40.08% and 51.92%) do not sum to 100%, an age range of 51 to 73 is given beside a mean of 71.55 with older patients elsewhere, and a genotype is written "CTPN" where CTPM is meant. The treatment-response figures are described in text without effect sizes.
- Most of the sirtuin modulator evidence is from cell lines or rodents; the one human trial mentioned (resveratrol, phase II) is reported only for crossing the blood-brain barrier and modulating the immune response.

## Open Questions

- Does the better response of SIRT2 C/T carriers to multifactorial treatment replicate in an independent cohort with an untreated comparison group (the study had no control arm)?
- Is the interaction between SIRT2 rs10410544 and APOE ε4 status on Alzheimer disease risk reproduced in other populations (the risk allele effect was reported only in ε4-negative people, and the T/T enrichment among ε4/ε4 patients was not significant)?
- Does CYP2D6 poor-metabolizer status explain part of the weak drug response of APOE ε4/ε4 patients (the paper shows co-occurrence but no mediation analysis)?
- Does inhibiting SIRT2 or activating SIRT1 change amyloid or tau outcomes in humans with Alzheimer disease (support so far comes from cell and rodent models, and the one resveratrol phase II trial mentioned is reported only for blood-brain barrier passage and immune modulation)?
