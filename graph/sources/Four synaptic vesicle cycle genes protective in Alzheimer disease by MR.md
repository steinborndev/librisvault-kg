# Four synaptic vesicle cycle genes protective in Alzheimer disease by MR

A secondary analysis of public data that asks which genes of the KEGG synaptic vesicle cycle pathway (79 genes, hsa04721) are both changed in [[Alzheimer disease]] brain and causally linked to its risk. Bulk cortex microarray data gave the differentially expressed genes, [[Mendelian randomization]] with blood expression quantitative trait loci tested which of them change risk, and a single-cell cortex dataset placed the survivors in cell types. Four genes passed every filter: [[ATP6V1D]], [[ATP6V1G2]], [[CLTB]] and [[NSF]], all lower in Alzheimer brain and all with an odds ratio below one.

## Why This Source Matters

It links a cellular process often described only as an early Alzheimer lesion, the [[Synaptic vesicle cycle]], to a genetic causal test, and finds that higher genetically predicted expression of four of its genes goes with lower risk. It also gives a worked example of a pipeline (differential expression, then MR, then nomogram, immune profiling and single-cell localisation) that can be repeated on other pathway gene sets. The causal and diagnostic claims are modest (see Assessment), so the four genes are best read as a candidate list for experiments, not as validated biomarkers.

## Design

- Expression data: GSE132903 (97 Alzheimer, 98 control samples) as training set; GSE118553 (167 vs 100) and GSE125583 (219 vs 70) as validation sets; GSE157827 (10X, 12 Alzheimer and 9 control samples) for single-cell analysis.
- Differential expression with [[Limma differential expression analysis]] (absolute log2 fold change above 1.2, adjusted P below 0.05) gave 2,149 genes (1,094 down, 1,055 up); intersected with the 79 pathway genes this left 25. A [[STRING protein interaction network analysis]] (score 0.4) of the 25 gave 87 pairs, with SNAP25, NSF, STXBP1, VAMP2 and CLTB the best connected.
- Mendelian randomization: eQTLGen cis-eQTLs as exposure; outcome the FinnGen-derived IEU OpenGWAS set finn-b-G6_ALZHEIMER (3,899 cases, 214,893 controls, European). Instruments at P below 5e-8, clumped at r2 0.001 over 100 kb, F statistic above 10, SNPs tied to educational attainment or brain structure removed (P below 1e-5). Inverse variance weighting was the main estimator, with MR-Egger, weighted median, simple and weighted mode, Cochran's Q, an MR-Egger intercept test and leave-one-out checks. A gene counted as a biomarker only if its direction of expression change agreed with its causal direction.
- Downstream: a [[Diagnostic nomogram]], [[Gene set enrichment analysis]] per gene, ssGSEA [[Immune infiltration analysis]] of 28 immune cell types, a miRNA-lncRNA regulatory network, and Comparative Toxicogenomics Database disease associations.
- Single-cell analysis with Seurat 5.1.0 (173,281 cells, 22 clusters, annotated with SingleR and Sc-Type), then [[Pseudotime trajectory analysis]] of excitatory neurons and CellChat ligand-receptor communication. See [[Single-cell RNA sequencing]].

## Findings

- Of the 25 genes, 15 had usable instruments (126 SNPs after filtering). Four had an inverse-variance-weighted odds ratio below one: ATP6V1D 0.926 (0.894 to 0.959, P below 0.0001), ATP6V1G2 0.932 (0.877 to 0.991, P = 0.0243), CLTB 0.934 (0.883 to 0.989, P = 0.0189), NSF 0.897 (0.831 to 0.968, P = 0.0055). Heterogeneity and pleiotropy tests were not significant and leave-one-out did not change the picture.
- All four were lower in Alzheimer samples in the training set and in both validation sets, and were positively correlated with each other. They sit on chromosomes 14 (ATP6V1D), 6 (ATP6V1G2), 5 (CLTB) and 17 (NSF).
- The nomogram built from the four genes reached an area under the ROC curve of 0.799 in the training set, with a decision curve showing net benefit over single genes.
- Gene set enrichment placed all four in oxidative phosphorylation, interferon gamma response, inflammatory response and TNF-alpha signalling via NF-kappaB (see [[NF-kappaB signaling]], [[IFNG]], [[TNF]]); the authors read these as mitochondrial dysfunction, chronic inflammation, immune dysregulation and neuronal damage.
- Immune infiltration: 19 of 28 cell types differed. Effector memory CD4 T cells and eosinophils were lower in Alzheimer samples; 17 types, including activated and central memory CD8 T cells, were higher. All four genes correlated positively with the two reduced types and negatively with the 17 raised types, strongest for ATP6V1D with effector memory CD4 T cells (r = 0.66) and NSF with CD56dim natural killer cells (r = -0.86).
- Regulatory network: miRTarBase returned 18 miRNAs for CLTB and NSF; starBase added 75 lncRNAs from 16 of those miRNAs, giving 176 interaction pairs, for example XIST to NSF through hsa-miR-193b-3p (see [[MIR193B]]). hsa-miR-34a-5p ([[MIR34A]]) and hsa-miR-16-5p are already proposed Alzheimer markers. CTD linked the genes to memory disorders, learning disabilities, brain diseases, neural tube defects and neurotoxicity syndromes.
- Single-cell: seven cell types (oligodendrocytes, excitatory and inhibitory neurons, astrocytes, microglia, endothelial cells, oligodendrocyte precursor cells). Excitatory neurons were the largest group in both Alzheimer (40.7%) and control (42.9%) samples. NSF was densest in excitatory and inhibitory neurons and differed between groups in every cell type; ATP6V1D was spread evenly with lower levels in Alzheimer excitatory neurons; CLTB peaked in excitatory neurons and fell in Alzheimer; ATP6V1G2 was barely detected. Along the excitatory neuron trajectory ATP6V1D and CLTB were steady while NSF was higher early and mid-way. Communication from astrocytes to endothelial cells and excitatory neurons, and from oligodendrocyte precursors to microglia, was weaker in Alzheimer samples.
- The authors cite earlier reports that NSF phosphorylation falls in an Alzheimer mouse model, that the NSF rs199533 A allele is protective and delays onset, and that low NSF impairs autophagy and AMPA receptor trafficking; they also note ATP6V1D falls with disease stage, possibly through lysosomal dysfunction.

## Assessment

- The exposure is blood eQTL data, whereas the downregulation filter and the cell-type story come from brain tissue; that blood-derived genetic effects on expression carry over to brain neurons is assumed. No colocalisation test was run.
- Fifteen genes were tested with no stated multiple-testing correction. Only ATP6V1D would clearly survive a Bonferroni threshold of about 0.003; NSF is borderline, and ATP6V1G2 and CLTB (P about 0.02) would not.
- The outcome set has only 3,899 cases, much smaller than the large Alzheimer GWAS meta-analyses, so instruments are weakly powered, and effect sizes are modest (odds ratios 0.90 to 0.93 per unit of expression).
- The nomogram's AUC of 0.799 comes from the training set alone, with no AUC reported in the two validation sets, so it is an optimistic apparent figure. The training and validation sets are bulk microarray or RNA data with unstated brain regions in the text, and the immune scores are ssGSEA estimates, not measured cell counts.
- The abstract says excitatory neurons fell significantly in Alzheimer, but the reported proportions (40.7% vs 42.9%) differ little and no test is shown in the text; the claim of neuronal loss from this is weak. Pseudotime "development" in postmortem adult cortex is a computed ordering, not a lineage.
- The pleiotropy check is described as "MR-PRESSO's MR-Egger regression", which blends two different methods, and no per-gene SNP counts, heterogeneity or intercept statistics are given in the main text (they are in supplementary tables).
- Downregulation in disease does not by itself show that raising the genes would help; the paper's therapeutic suggestions (genetic or pharmacological upregulation) have no experimental support here. The authors state that no animal or cell experiments were done.

## Open Questions

- Do ATP6V1D, ATP6V1G2, CLTB and NSF keep protective causal estimates when brain-derived eQTL data replace blood eQTLs (the study used eQTLGen blood data only)?
- Does a colocalisation analysis support a shared causal variant between expression of each of the four genes and Alzheimer risk (not run in the paper)?
- Does the four-gene nomogram keep an area under the curve near 0.8 in the validation cohorts GSE118553 and GSE125583 (only the training set value was reported)?
- Does restoring NSF or CLTB expression in Alzheimer mouse neurons rescue synaptic vesicle recycling or memory (no animal or cell experiment was done)?
- Why do V-ATPase subunits move in opposite directions in different Alzheimer expression studies, with ATP6V1D and ATP6V1G2 reported lower here and ATP6V1A reported higher elsewhere?
