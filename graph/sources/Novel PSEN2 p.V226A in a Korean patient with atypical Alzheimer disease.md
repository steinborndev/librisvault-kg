# Novel PSEN2 p.V226A in a Korean patient with atypical Alzheimer disease

A single-patient case report with structure modelling. A 59-year-old Korean woman presented with six months of rapidly progressive memory loss and visual hallucinations. Whole-exome sequencing found a heterozygous, previously unreported [[PSEN2 p.V226A]] in the fifth transmembrane helix of [[PSEN2]], confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The authors model that variant and ten other changes in the same helix and propose that the helix matters for gamma-secretase function, but they provide no functional data and no segregation.

## Case

- Onset with forgetting of routine tasks, then disorientation at home, failure to recognise her granddaughter and husband, and night-time hallucinations (talking to someone, seeing animals that were not there). She retired because of it.
- Mini-Mental State Examination 15/30, Global Deterioration Scale 4, Clinical Dementia Rating 1; neurological examination otherwise normal. [[APOE]] genotype e3/e3.
- MRI: mild diffuse atrophy. [[Amyloid PET]] (a fluorine-18 tracer): abnormal grey-matter deposition throughout the brain, most in the left temporal lobe. The [[Multimer detection system oligomeric amyloid-beta assay]] scored 1.05 (cut-off 0.90). Complement [[C3]] was 202 (normal 90 to 180) with C4 normal at 24, read as neuroinflammation specific to Alzheimer disease rather than acute inflammation.
- Her mother died of dementia at 71 without work-up; other relatives declined genetic testing or information. The authors classify the case as atypical because of the hallucinations and diagnose [[Early-onset Alzheimer disease]].

## Methods

- DNA from whole blood; exome sequencing on an Illumina platform, variants viewed in IGV and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The panel also covered genes for Parkinson disease, frontotemporal dementia, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and vascular disease. Study approved by the Soonchunhyang University College of Medicine Cheonan Hospital review board.
- Pathogenicity tools: PolyPhen-2, CADD, MutationTaster, FATHMM and M-CAP; ExPASy for bulkiness, polarity and Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy; ColabFold (AlphaFold2) models of mutant and wild-type PSEN2 inspected in Discovery Studio; STRING and ClueGO for gene networks. See [[In silico missense variant effect prediction]] and [[Whole-exome sequencing]].

## Findings

- PSEN2 Val226Ala has a gnomAD frequency of about 1.6 per million and is absent from 1000 Genomes. Valine 226 is conserved across several mammals; glutamine or arginine sits there in birds and fish, and alanine in no species examined.
- Prediction scores were weak or mixed: PolyPhen-2 0.34 (weakly pathogenic), CADD 17.8 (below 20), FATHMM -6.08 (damaging), M-CAP 0.216 (possibly pathogenic), MutationTaster predicted disturbance of the helical transmembrane feature. The residue lies near a histone 3 lysine 36 trimethylation site, which the authors raise as a possible epigenetic link.
- ExPASy: alanine is slightly more polar, less hydrophobic and less bulky than valine. The AlphaFold2 models differed little overall, but alanine 226 loses a hydrogen bond to glutamine 229 and weaker van der Waals contacts are predicted, suggesting a more flexible helix and a slight disturbance of hydrophobic loop 4.
- Two further rare variants were found, [[NOTCH3 p.R75L]] (cysteine-sparing, thought benign) and [[GBA1 p.I20V]] (probable benign). STRING showed PSEN2 linked to [[NOTCH3]] by experiment and curated databases and to [[GBA1]] by text mining and co-expression; ClueGO found no link among the three. The authors speculate that such partners could promote hallucinations.
- Ten other missense changes lie in the same helix, and all were modelled: [[PSEN2 p.L225P]], [[PSEN2 p.Q228L]], [[PSEN2 p.Y231C]], [[PSEN2 p.I235F]], [[PSEN2 p.A237V]], [[PSEN2 p.L238F]], [[PSEN2 p.L238P]], [[PSEN2 p.M239T]], [[PSEN2 p.M239V]] and [[PSEN2 p.M239I]]. Each was predicted to disturb the helix; the leucine to proline change at 238 is expected to kink it. Published case data, reviewed in the text, range from typical early-onset Alzheimer disease (M239T, M239I) through late-onset (A237V) to frontotemporal dementia (Y231C). Cell data show a roughly doubled amyloid beta 42 to 40 ratio for I235F and L238F and a raised ratio for M239I.
- Hallucinations are rare in PSEN2 disease; N141I, S130L, V191E and A415S carriers have been described with them. The authors call for transfected-cell work on Val226Ala.

## Assessment

- One patient, no segregation (relatives declined testing) and no cell or animal functional data, so causality of the variant is not shown; the authors themselves say a direct or indirect role cannot be ruled out. All structural claims come from AlphaFold2 predictions in which wild-type and mutant models look similar.
- In silico scores are borderline (CADD under 20, PolyPhen-2 0.34), and the clinical picture includes hallucinations that are plausibly explained by the two other rare variants or by Lewy-type pathology not tested for here.
- The text is inconsistent in places: the conclusion cites positive FDG-PET although the case describes only an amyloid PET; the structure section calls the alanine to valine change at 237 "Met237Val"; and the figure legend calls Gln228Leu "Glu228Leu".
- The claim that every TM5 mutation shows extensive Alzheimer pathology is stated without the table that supports it being included in the text read.

## Open Questions

- Does PSEN2 p.V226A change amyloid beta 42 to 40 ratios or gamma-secretase processing of the amyloid precursor protein and Notch in transfected cells (no functional study was done)?
- Does PSEN2 p.V226A segregate with dementia in the patient's family (her relatives declined genetic testing and her mother died at 71 without a diagnosis)?
- Do the NOTCH3 p.R75L and GBA1 p.I20V variants contribute to hallucinations in carriers of PSEN2 variants (only one patient was examined and the link rests on a network database search)?
- Is the amyloid beta 42 to 40 ratio raised for the PSEN2 transmembrane domain 5 variants that have no cell data, such as Q228L, Y231C and A237V (only I235F, L238F and M239I were measured)?
- Is reduced access of the amyloid precursor protein substrate to the gamma-secretase active site the mechanism of transmembrane domain 5 variants (the proposal rests on structure predictions)?

## Why This Source Matters

It extends the vault's presenilin material from the transmembrane domain III change [[PSEN2 p.H169N]] to a second East Asian case in a different helix, and collects the eleven known PSEN2 changes in the fifth transmembrane helix into one place with a shared structural argument. It also adds hallucinations as a phenotype to weigh when reading PSEN2 variants, and shows how weak in silico scores (CADD, PolyPhen-2) still leave a novel variant unresolved without functional work, the same limit seen in [[Double APP and PSEN2 mutations in a Korean patient with early-onset Alzheimer disease]].
