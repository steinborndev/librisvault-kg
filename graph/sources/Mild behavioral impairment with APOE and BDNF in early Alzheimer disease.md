# Mild behavioral impairment with APOE and BDNF in early Alzheimer disease

A cross-sectional memory-clinic study from the [[Czech Brain Aging Study]] of 62 people with amnestic [[Mild cognitive impairment]] and biomarker evidence of Alzheimer disease (aMCI-AD) and 50 cognitively normal older adults. It asks how often [[Mild behavioral impairment]] occurs at this stage, which neuropsychiatric symptoms it consists of, and whether the [[APOE]] ε4 allele or the [[BDNF p.V66M]] Met allele is associated with its severity.

## Design

- Participants: 112 people recruited from memory clinics in Prague (n = 89) and Brno (n = 23); all Caucasian. Mild cognitive impairment followed NIA-AA 2011 criteria, and only the amnestic type was kept (16 single-domain, 46 multiple-domain). Each patient had a positive Alzheimer biomarker result from CSF β-amyloid 1-42 (ELISA, n = 21), [[Flutemetamol]] [[Amyloid PET]] read visually (n = 25) or both (n = 16), plus medial temporal lobe atrophy on MRI. One CSF/PET disagreement was resolved by PET. Controls came from the University of the Third Age or patients' relatives and had normal neuropsychology and no significant atrophy or vascular change (Fazekas ≤ 2); their amyloid status was not known.
- Behaviour was rated by a close informant with the [[Mild Behavioral Impairment Checklist]] (total 0-102, five domains), and the participants themselves completed the Geriatric Depression Scale-15 and the Beck Anxiety Inventory. Mild behavioral impairment was defined by a total score of 7 or more in the aMCI-AD group and 9 or more for subjective cognitive decline, with 7 and an experimental 6 tried for both groups.
- Genotyping: DNA from blood; APOE by high-resolution melting analysis (Luna Probes assay) and BDNF rs6265 (G196A) by a purpose-built melting assay. APOE was available for 61 and BDNF for 52 patients. Patients were split into ε4 carriers (37 heterozygotes and 8 homozygotes, n = 45) and non-carriers (n = 16), and into Met carriers (18 heterozygotes and 2 homozygotes, n = 20) and non-carriers (n = 32). One ε2 carrier was left out.
- Analysis: multiple linear regression of each outcome on cognitive status adjusted for age and sex; within aMCI-AD, outcomes on ε4 status and Met status adjusted for age, sex and Mini-Mental State Examination score, then with an APOE-by-BDNF interaction term. No correction for multiple comparisons.

## Findings

- Mild behavioral impairment was present in 48.4% (31 of 62) of the aMCI-AD group and in none of the controls at the validated cutoffs. With the lower cutoff of 6 applied to both groups, 53.2% of patients and 6% (3 of 50) of controls qualified. A checklist total above zero was seen in 82% of patients and 28% of controls.
- Compared with controls, aMCI-AD was associated with 3.33 points higher total severity (B = 3.33, 95% CI 1.96 to 4.71, p < 0.001) and with higher scores in affective dysregulation (B = 1.13), [[Apathy]] (decreased motivation, B = 0.88) and impulse dyscontrol (B = 1.13), all p < 0.001, but not social inappropriateness or psychotic symptoms, whose mean scores were very low.
- Self-report showed 0.61 points more [[Depression]] on the Geriatric Depression Scale-15 (p = 0.005), mostly below threshold (only two patients scored above 5), but no difference in [[Anxiety]] on the Beck Anxiety Inventory.
- Within aMCI-AD, neither ε4 carriage (B = 0.52, p = 0.846) nor Met carriage (B = -1.29, p = 0.646) was related to total checklist severity, singly or together, and the APOE-by-BDNF interaction was null (B = -0.40, p = 0.948); domain scores and depression scores were also null.
- For anxiety, ε4 (p = 0.108), Met (p = 0.107) and the interaction (B = -9.29, p = 0.055) were individually non-significant, but after adjusting for ε4 and the interaction, Met carriers scored higher on the Beck Anxiety Inventory than ε4-negative, Met-negative patients (B = 10.73, 95% CI 2.51 to 18.94, p = 0.012); this model fitted best.
- ε4 was common in the patients (72.6% carriers against 25.8% non-carriers), and 24% were taking SSRI antidepressants and 21% cognitive enhancers.

## Why This Source Matters

It is one of the few studies to characterise informant-rated behavioural change with the [[Mild Behavioral Impairment Checklist]] in memory-clinic patients whose Alzheimer pathology was confirmed by biomarkers, so the roughly 50% prevalence and the apathy, mood and impulse-control profile can be attributed to prodromal Alzheimer disease and not to mixed causes of cognitive impairment. Its null result for ε4 and Met alone, together with a suggestive combined effect on anxiety, argues that earlier positive APOE findings in unselected non-demented samples may partly reflect hidden preclinical Alzheimer disease, and that gene-gene effects are worth testing.

## Assessment

- Small sample (62 patients, 20 Met carriers, 16 ε4 non-carriers, eight ε4 and two Met homozygotes) leaves low power for genetic effects, and no dose-dependent analysis was possible.
- No multiple-comparison correction; the one positive genetic result (Met and anxiety, p = 0.012) came from a model chosen after the interaction was borderline, rests on a reference group of ε4-negative, Met-negative patients, and has a very wide confidence interval.
- Cross-sectional design cannot capture how symptoms change over time or genotype acting on trajectory.
- Controls had no biomarker data, so some may have had preclinical Alzheimer disease; the groups also differed in age and sex.
- Memory-clinic recruitment inflates symptom prevalence relative to community samples. Genetic effect models explained little variance (modest R2 throughout).
- Detailed genetic regression tables are in supplementary material that was not part of the text read.

## Open Questions

- Does the association of the BDNF Met allele with self-reported anxiety replicate in a larger biomarker-defined amnestic mild cognitive impairment sample (here 20 Met carriers; no correction for multiple tests)?
- Do APOE ε4 and BDNF Met alter the trajectory of mild behavioral impairment symptoms over time (the design was cross-sectional)?
- How does the profile of mild behavioral impairment differ between amyloid-positive cognitively normal older adults and people with mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer disease (the control group had no biomarker data)?
- Do sex-specific associations between APOE, BDNF and neuropsychiatric symptoms exist in aMCI-AD (sex was only a covariate)?
