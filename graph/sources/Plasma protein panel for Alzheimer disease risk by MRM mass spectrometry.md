# Plasma protein panel for Alzheimer disease risk by MRM mass spectrometry

A joint analysis of 331 non-depleted plasma samples from two Moscow clinics, quantified for 125 proteins by [[Multiple reaction monitoring mass spectrometry]], to confirm candidate plasma biomarkers of [[Alzheimer disease]] and to build a protein panel that separates patients from controls and predicts which patients with [[Mild cognitive impairment]] progress.

## Design

- Two cohorts: N.A. Alekseev Mental-Health Clinic No. 1 (49 Alzheimer disease, 78 mild cognitive impairment, 49 controls) and the Mental Health Research Center (46, 58 and 51; an expansion of the authors' earlier study). Total 95 patients with Alzheimer disease, 136 with mild cognitive impairment and 100 controls; 28 of the 136 progressed during follow-up. Only the Mini-Mental State Examination and clock drawing test were common to both clinics.
- A commercial 125-protein kit with stable isotope-labelled peptide standards, 10 microlitres of plasma, a triple-quadrupole instrument; 82 proteins were quantified consistently in both cohorts. Welch t-tests with Benjamini-Hochberg correction, empirical-Bayes batch correction, STRING network and Gene Ontology enrichment.
- Classifiers: L1-regularized logistic regression repeated 1000 times to find "stable" proteins, backward elimination, then class-weighted L2 logistic regression with nested cross-validation.
- Genotyping on a microarray of 21 Alzheimer risk SNPs plus the [[APOE]] alleles, regressed against plasma protein levels.

## Findings

- 41 proteins had q below 0.05 and 43 passed a 10% false discovery rate in the combined Alzheimer versus control comparison; 37 of the 43 were already reported as candidate biomarkers, 19 of them in three or more earlier cohorts. Within a single cohort, 28 proteins reached q below 0.05 in the first clinic but only five ([[AFM]], [[PON1]], FGB, [[FN1]], FGG) in the second. Fifteen proteins moved in opposite directions between cohorts, none of them significant in the second.
- The 43 proteins clustered into lipoprotein remodeling and cholesterol efflux, acute-phase and complement, and coagulation and fibrinolysis groups.
- Sixteen proteins were selected in every L1 run for Alzheimer disease versus controls: [[APOA4]], [[ORM1]], [[AFM]], [[LBP]], [[HP]], [[C5]], [[CLU]], [[FN1]], [[CP]], [[ATRN]], [[BCHE]], [[PZP]], [[A1BG]], [[PON1]], [[SERPINF2]] and [[APOE]]. The best 12 reached a cross-validated ROC-AUC of 0.93 for Alzheimer disease versus controls, but a panel of 12 reached only 0.64 for progressing versus stable mild cognitive impairment.
- Adding [[C1S]], [[TF]], [[TTR]], [[F12]] and [[SERPINA7]], then pruning, gave a 13-protein "universal panel" (ORM1, APOA4, LBP, HP, FN1, BCHE, APOE, PZP, A1BG, TF, SERPINA7, TTR, F12): ROC-AUC 0.90 for Alzheimer disease versus controls (sensitivity 0.87, specificity 0.83) and 0.81 for progressing versus stable mild cognitive impairment (sensitivity 0.68, specificity 0.89). The two cognitive tests alone gave 0.62 and combining them with the proteins gave 0.82.
- Cross-center training and testing: trained on the research-center cohort and tested on the clinic cohort, 0.94 (top-12) and 0.91 (universal); the reverse direction gave 0.90 and 0.80.
- Among the tested genetic variants only the [[APOE]] genotype survived correction: the ε2 allele was associated with higher plasma APOE (beta 0.37, q = 6.3 x 10^-3) and ε4 with lower (beta -0.22, q = 6.3 x 10^-3). No other locus kept significance, so genotype was not added to the classifier.

## Why This Source Matters

It tests in two independent cohorts whether a mass spectrometry panel built from earlier candidate biomarkers holds up, and it targets the harder clinical question of which patients with mild cognitive impairment will progress. Its result that a protein panel beat standard cognitive screening tests for predicting progression, while an Alzheimer-versus-control panel did not transfer to that question, shows that the two tasks need different markers.

## Assessment

- Only 28 patients with mild cognitive impairment progressed, and the 0.81 progression AUC carries a wide spread across folds (plus or minus 0.09 to 0.1) from 3-fold cross-validation; there was no separate held-out validation cohort.
- The two cohorts differed: [[APOE]] ε4 frequency separated controls from patients in one cohort but not the other, and the first clinic yielded many more significant proteins than the second. Merging relied on batch correction and on high correlation of group means (R about 0.99).
- Cross-center transfer fell to 0.80 for the universal panel when trained on the first clinic, so performance depends on the training set.
- The panel selection (L1 selection, then backward elimination on the same cross-validation) may be optimistic. Effects are reported mainly through q-values and AUCs; the direction of change for each protein is shown in figures and tables not reproduced in the text read.
- Both cohorts are from Moscow, and the diagnoses used ICD-10 criteria with no biomarker confirmation of amyloid or tau.

## Open Questions

- Does the 13-protein universal panel keep its ROC-AUC of about 0.81 for mild cognitive impairment progression in a third cohort not used for protein selection (the study had two cohorts and no held-out set)?
- How does the panel perform against plasma phosphorylated tau 217 and the amyloid-beta 42/40 ratio in the same individuals (those assays were not measured here)?
- Why do the two Moscow clinics differ so much in the number of significant proteins (28 versus 5) when the group means correlate at R about 0.99?
