# CHRFAM7A proinflammatory switch of alpha7 nAChR in iPSC microglia

A 2020 International Journal of Molecular Sciences study that asks why the alpha7 nicotinic receptor ([[CHRNA7]]) is anti-inflammatory in animal models yet is a long-standing but unsuccessful drug target in [[Alzheimer disease]]. The authors' answer is [[CHRFAM7A]], a fusion gene found only in humans, which is absent from every laboratory animal. They build isogenic human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) lines with and without CHRFAM7A, differentiate them into microglia-like (MGL) cells, and show that the fusion gene lowers [[Amyloid-beta]] uptake yet turns the response to Aβ and to [[Lipopolysaccharide]] (LPS) from restrained to NF-κB-driven inflammation.

## Design

- Lines: UB068 (CHRFAM7A null), UB068_CHRFAM7A (the same line with CHRFAM7A knocked in using TALENs, the isogenic carrier) and UB052 (a carrier with one copy, "nascent"). All derive from skin fibroblasts reprogrammed with episomal vectors.
- Differentiation: yolk-sac embryoid bodies in medium with CSF1 and [[IL34]] (the Muffat protocol), then plated progenitors; characterised by VE-cadherin, c-Kit and [[SPI1]] (PU.1) early, then [[ITGAM]] (CD11b), CD68, [[AIF1]] (Iba-1) and TMEM119, with fluorescent bead [[Phagocytosis]] as the functional check.
- Aβ uptake: fluorescent Aβ1-42 at 1 to 250 nM (dose response, transient CHRFAM7A-mCherry versus empty vector in UB068) and 100 nM (time course in the three lines), with the alpha7 antagonist [[Methyllycaconitine]] (MLA).
- Inflammatory readouts: p65 ([[RELA]]) nuclear translocation scored by the Manders coefficient, phospho-p65 and [[NFKBIA]] immunoblots, RT-qPCR of RELA, IL1B, IL6 and TNF, a human cytokine antibody array on conditioned medium, an NF-κB DNA-binding assay and IL-6 ELISA, with LPS (1 μg/mL) as a non-alpha7 stimulus and [[Nicotine]] (10 μM) or MLA as modulators. See [[NF-kappaB nuclear translocation dynamics]].
- Statistics: triplicate or quadruplicate experiments, two-tailed unpaired t tests, Bonferroni correction for the cytokine array.

## Findings

- Expression: CHRNA7 was expressed in all three MGL lines; CHRFAM7A was abundant only in the two carrier lines, shown by RT-qPCR across the breakpoint and by immunoblot.
- Aβ uptake: internalised Aβ1-42 partly overlapped with alpha7 (Manders 0.575) and the acidic compartment (0.783). CHRFAM7A flattened the dose-response curve, significant at 50 and 100 nM, and both carrier lines took up less Aβ over time than UB068. MLA paradoxically increased Aβ uptake in the null line and barely changed it in carriers.
- Response to Aβ (a damage-associated pattern): 1 μM Aβ1-42 drove p65 into the nucleus in both genotypes, but nuclear p65 and phospho-p65 stayed raised at 4 h only with CHRFAM7A. Aβ raised RELA, IL1B, IL6 and TNF transcripts in carriers and lowered RELA and TNF in the null line. MLA raised these targets in the null line and lowered them in carriers.
- Response to LPS (a pathogen-associated pattern): the isogenic carrier had higher baseline NF-κB-dependent cytokines and NF-κB DNA binding, and shifted toward an NF-κB-dependent cytokine profile after LPS. [[IL6]], [[CXCL8]] and [[CCL2]] emerged as drivers after correction; [[CXCL1]], [[CXCL5]] and [[IL10]] also responded in the carrier, while TNF-α and TNF-β fell, unexpectedly. IL-6 transcript and protein were confirmed by RT-qPCR and ELISA in all three lines; nicotine raised and MLA did not lower the LPS response in carriers, the reverse of the null line.
- Dynamics: after LPS, NF-κB reached a maximum in the nucleus at 45 minutes and left quickly in UB068, but peaked at 2 h and persisted to 6 h with CHRFAM7A, alongside a distinct phospho-p65 to p65 ratio and IκBα degradation pattern.
- Interpretation offered: a CHRFAM7A-containing alpha7 pentamer is a hypomorphic ionotropic receptor with a longer inactive state, which favours metabotropic signalling and releases the tonic brake on NF-κB (see [[Cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway]]). The authors link this to human Alzheimer microglia, whose top upregulated pathways (chemotaxis, IL-6, NF-κB) differ from the [[Disease-associated microglia]] programme of the [[5xFAD mouse]], noting that mice are all CHRFAM7A null while over 99% of people are carriers.

## Why This Source Matters

Alpha7 agonists failed in Alzheimer trials after promising animal work, and the paper proposes a concrete, testable reason: a human-specific gene rewires the receptor's inflammatory output, so the mouse is the wrong sign. It supplies a ready isogenic iPSC pair for screening, and it is a clear case for [[Human-specific gene duplications]] as a source of the translational gap discussed under [[Mouse model validity for Alzheimer disease]]. It also adds a direct human-cell mechanism for how Aβ reaches NF-κB in microglia ([[Neuroinflammation]]).

## Assessment

- The central comparison rests on one isogenic pair plus one independent carrier line, each with a single clone, so line-specific effects (such as the baseline cytokine difference that the authors read as transcriptional priming) are hard to separate from CHRFAM7A itself. The authors say a population-representative iPSC panel is needed.
- The copy number and expression level of the TALEN knock-in compared with natural carriers are not documented in the text, and the legend to the expression figure says CHRFAM7A lowered CHRNA7 transcript while the results text reports CHRNA7 expression in all lines without a magnitude.
- Concentrations are not consistent across the paper: NF-κB assays use 1 μM Aβ1-42 in the results and 500 nM in the methods, uptake incubation is 18 h in the methods and 24 h in a legend, and MLA is 10 μM in the Aβ experiments and 50 μM in the methods for LPS. The argument that carriers respond above 50 nM Aβ is drawn from the uptake range, whereas the inflammatory readouts were at micromolar Aβ.
- Claims that CHRFAM7A acts through a metabotropic switch are inferred from earlier oocyte and neuron work and from the pharmacology; calcium signalling, receptor currents and G-protein or kinase mediators were not measured here. Receptor-blocking conclusions rest on MLA and nicotine alone, with no genetic alpha7 knockout.
- The Aβ preparation is not characterised for oligomeric state in the text, and 2D cultures lack other cell types; the authors themselves state that mouse knock-in models may not recapitulate the findings. Statistics are unpaired t tests without an omnibus test; many readouts have n = 5 in legends against triplicate or quadruplicate designs in the methods.

## Open Questions

- Does a microglial CHRFAM7A genotype (carrier versus null) predict the inflammatory pathway signature in post-mortem Alzheimer brain transcriptomes (proposed by the authors but not performed)?
- Is the CHRFAM7A-driven proinflammatory switch protective or destructive for amyloid clearance and neuronal survival in 3D organoid or co-culture models (only microglia-like cells in 2D were tested)?
- Which upstream step links the hypomorphic alpha7 pentamer to prolonged NF-κB nuclear residence: calcium decoders, calcium store regulation or IκB kinase activity (listed as candidates, none tested)?
- Why do TNF-α and TNF-β fall and IL-10 rise after LPS in CHRFAM7A carrier microglia (the authors raise a possible immune reset but did not test it)?
- Do alpha7 agonist trials in Alzheimer disease show a different response by CHRFAM7A carrier status (genotype of the trial participants was not available to the authors)?
