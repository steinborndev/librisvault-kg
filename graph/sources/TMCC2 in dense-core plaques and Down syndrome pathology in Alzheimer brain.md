# TMCC2 in dense-core plaques and Down syndrome pathology in Alzheimer brain

The first look at [[TMCC2]] protein in human brain. TMCC2 is the human orthologue of the fly gene dementin, whose mutation causes neurodegeneration with Alzheimer-like features, and it forms a conserved complex with [[APP]] and binds apolipoprotein E. The authors stained and blotted post-mortem brain from 19 [[Alzheimer disease]] cases and 5 non-demented controls to ask whether TMCC2 follows APP, varies with [[APOE]] genotype, or sits in Alzheimer lesions.

## Design

- Brain banks in Manchester and London; superior temporal gyrus for staining and blotting, cerebellum for blotting. Groups: five non-demented APOE3/3 controls (mean age 85), five late-onset AD APOE3/3 (Braak IV-V, mean age 77), five late-onset AD APOE4/4 (Braak V-VI, mean age 76), five early-onset AD with [[APP p.V717I]], one with [[APP p.V717G]], and three [[Down syndrome]] cases with AD pathology.
- Two rabbit antibodies to TMCC2 (the better-validated one, antibody 94, and a second, less specific one); dual immunofluorescence with antibodies to the APP C-terminus and ectodomain, to amyloid-beta (4G8) and to phospho-tau (AT8), plus methoxy-X04 as an amyloid dye. Overlap with APP was scored as Mander's coefficient; plaque-associated dystrophies were scored post hoc.
- Efficient TMCC2 extraction needed SDS homogenates; non-ionic detergents (Triton X-100, NP40) recovered it poorly. Western blot band ratios were compared by one-way ANOVA with Tukey's test or Student's t-test.

## Findings

- In non-demented temporal cortex TMCC2 was mostly neuronal and its staining was nearly always coincident with APP, with occasional APP-only signal; both were also seen in blood vessel lumen.
- In late-onset AD (both APOE genotypes) TMCC2 filled the centre of [[Dense-core amyloid plaque]] but not the diffuse amyloid halo around it, nor diffuse plaques, nor phospho-tau neurites. It also co-stained with APP in the [[Dystrophic neurite]] bordering dense cores, almost universally in late-onset cases and more often in APOE3 than APOE4 brains.
- A trend to less TMCC2-APP overlap in AD did not reach significance in the small sample.
- Early-onset AD with APP Val717 mutations: dense-core plaques with TMCC2 and APP positive dystrophies were present, but many plaques had APP-positive dystrophies lacking TMCC2, and TMCC2-APP co-staining of dystrophies was rare compared with late-onset disease.
- Down syndrome AD: the same dense-core pattern, plus, in two of three cases, spicular or thread-like methoxy-X04-positive, TMCC2-positive but APP-negative lesions, morphologically unlike anything seen in the 10 late-onset or 6 familial cases. The authors liken them to the Aβ-negative "bird's nest" or "fibrous" plaques reported by others and have not shown that TMCC2 itself is amyloid.
- Western blots showed three TMCC2 bands, all at or above the predicted 77.5 kDa. Total TMCC2 did not differ by APOE or AD status. The share of lower-migrating forms was higher in cerebellum (57.5 ± 2.7%) than in temporal gyrus (42.5 ± 3%; p = 0.002); the regional difference held in APOE3/3 brains with or without AD but not in APOE4/4 AD. The authors suggest differential post-translational modification, though splice variants are not excluded.

## Why This Source Matters

It moves a Drosophila and rat-brain finding (TMCC2 in complex with APP, modulated by apoE) into human tissue and gives TMCC2 a concrete place in AD neuropathology: dense-core plaque centres and the dystrophic neurites around them, with a distinct form in Down syndrome. It also hints that the TMCC2-APP relationship differs between late-onset disease (where APOE is the main risk) and APP-driven early-onset disease.

## Assessment

- Descriptive and post hoc: no prior quantitative expectation, 19 AD cases, no multiple-testing correction stated, and sex and post-mortem delay could not be stratified.
- APOE4/4 cases were at later Braak stages than APOE3/3, and no age-matched non-demented APOE4/4 brains were available, so APOE and dementia effects on the TMCC2 band pattern cannot be separated.
- Only two of three Down syndrome cases showed the thread-like feature, and the TMCC2 isoform differences in early-onset and Down syndrome samples were not significant, which the authors attribute to low power.
- Colocalisation in dense cores shows spatial association, not that TMCC2 is an amyloid component or that it changes APP processing in human brain.

## Open Questions

- Does TMCC2 itself adopt a beta-sheet amyloid conformation in the thread-like lesions of Down syndrome Alzheimer brain (only antibody and methoxy-X04 co-staining was shown)?
- Which TMCC2 forms (isoforms or post-translational modifications) make up the three western blot bands in human brain, and why do they shift with APOE genotype (no mass spectrometry or phosphatase treatment was done)?
- Is the loss of TMCC2-APP co-staining in dystrophic neurites of early-onset Alzheimer disease a consequence of the APP mutation or of faster disease course (only six APP Val717 cases were examined)?
- Does TMCC2 deficiency alter APP processing or Aβ levels in a mammalian Alzheimer model (mouse TMCC2 knockouts were only cited, not tested with APP)?
