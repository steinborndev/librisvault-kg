# PBMC nicotinic receptor and cytokine expression in DLB and Alzheimer disease

A small case-control study from the Dementia Center of the University of Chieti-Pescara that measured, by qRT-PCR in [[Peripheral blood mononuclear cells]], the mRNA of five nicotinic acetylcholine receptor subunit genes, of acetylcholinesterase and of three pro-inflammatory cytokines. It compared 21 patients with probable [[Dementia with Lewy bodies]], 13 patients with [[Alzheimer disease]] (both naive to cholinesterase inhibitors, matched for age, sex, education, disease duration and cognitive level) and 8 healthy controls recruited among the patients' spouses.

## Design

- Blood drawn once, PBMCs isolated on Ficoll-Paque, RNA extracted, reverse transcribed and quantified by real-time PCR in triplicate; expression is fold change (2^-ΔΔCt) relative to the control group.
- Data were not normally distributed, so the authors used Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn tests for clinical variables, Mann-Whitney for DLB against AD, a sign test for each patient group against controls, and Spearman correlations without multiplicity adjustment.
- Clinical profiling of the DLB group: parkinsonism in 19 (90.5%), fluctuating cognition in 13 (61.9%), visual hallucinations in 16 (76.2%) and REM sleep behaviour disorder in 18 (85.7%). MoCA scores were lower in both dementia groups than in controls (p < 0.001).

## Findings

- **Cytokines.** [[IL1B]], [[TNF]] and [[IL6]] mRNA were higher in both patient groups than in controls. IL1B was higher in DLB than AD (p = 0.008), TNF was lower in DLB than AD (p < 0.0001), and IL6 did not differ between the two (p = 0.785).
- **Nicotinic subunits against controls.** [[CHRNA4]], [[CHRNB2]] and [[CHRNB4]] were lower in both DLB and AD. [[CHRNA3]] was lower in DLB and, per the discussion, higher in AD. [[CHRNA7]] was reported as not different among groups.
- **Nicotinic subunits, DLB against AD.** CHRNA3, CHRNA4, CHRNB2 and CHRNB4 all differed between the two diseases (p ≤ 0.001): CHRNA3 and CHRNB2 were lower in DLB, while CHRNA4 and CHRNB4 were lower in AD (medians of 0.0 against about 0.3-0.4 in DLB).
- **Acetylcholinesterase.** [[ACHE]] mRNA was higher in DLB than AD (median fold change 2.0 against 0.4, p = 0.002); in the sign test it was raised in DLB against controls but not in AD.
- **Correlations between subunits.** In DLB, CHRNB2 with CHRNA3 (rho 0.589) and CHRNA4 with CHRNA3 (rho 0.500) were positive. In AD, CHRNA4 correlated positively with CHRNA3 (rho 0.762) and with CHRNB4 (rho 0.717), while CHRNA7 correlated negatively with both CHRNA3 and CHRNA4 (rho -0.683).
- **No link to cytokines or severity.** No correlation was found between receptor subunits and cytokines, nor between either and MoCA, Clinical Dementia Rating or disease duration. The authors read the systemic signal as a marker of disease rather than of stage.

## Why This Source Matters

It offers a blood-based readout of cholinergic dysfunction that separates two dementias with overlapping cholinergic loss: the receptor pattern, and ACHE in particular, differs between DLB and AD even though the cytokine rise is shared. The authors propose peripheral nicotinic subunits and cytokines as diagnostic biomarker candidates and tie the DLB pattern to the better response of this disease to cholinesterase inhibitors. The cohort is too small to support that, so the value lies in the hypothesis and in the direction of effects.

## Assessment

- Very small groups (8 controls, 13 AD, 21 DLB) and exploratory statistics with no multiplicity correction on the correlations; the AD correlations rest on very few paired observations. The authors say the cohort does not allow assessment of sensitivity and specificity.
- Controls were spouses and are not described beyond age and sex; all patients were at an overt stage of dementia, which the authors suggest may blur cytokine differences.
- The text is not fully consistent: the abstract and the figure legend give a sign-test p of 0.002 for CHRNA7 in DLB against controls and 0.021 in AD, while the results and discussion say CHRNA7 did not differ among groups. The abstract lists CHRNB2 as reduced in both diseases, yet its AD median (0.7) is close to that of controls, and the text also states TNF lower in DLB than AD while the abstract says only that both exceed controls.
- mRNA in blood cells is only a proxy; the link to brain receptor loss is assumed, not measured, and the authors call for CSF and microglia imaging studies.
- Raw data are available from the corresponding authors on request.

## Open Questions

- Do the PBMC receptor and ACHE differences between DLB and AD hold in a larger, independent cohort, including patients at the mild cognitive impairment stage (the present cohort has 34 patients, all with overt dementia)?
- What are the diagnostic sensitivity and specificity of a PBMC panel of CHRNA3, CHRNA4, CHRNB2, CHRNB4 and ACHE for separating DLB from AD (not assessed because the cohort was too small)?
- Does PBMC CHRNA7 expression differ between patients and controls (the abstract and figure legend give sign-test p values of 0.002 and 0.021 that the results text contradicts)?
- Do peripheral nicotinic receptor levels track brain receptor loss measured by imaging or in CSF (no central measure was taken)?
