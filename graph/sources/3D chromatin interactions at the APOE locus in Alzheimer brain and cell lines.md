# 3D chromatin interactions at the APOE locus in Alzheimer brain and cell lines

A chromosome conformation capture study of the TOMM40-APOE-APOC1 gene cluster on chromosome 19. The authors map physical contacts between regulatory elements in four human cell lines and in frozen frontal lobe from 25 postmortem brains (15 [[Alzheimer disease]], 10 controls), measure the contact strength with a new digital PCR assay, and relate it to [[APOC1]] mRNA, [[APOE]] genotype and local DNA methylation.

## Design

- Anchors chosen from public maps (FORGEdb, TADKB): the [[APOE]] promoter and the CpG island in APOE exon 4. Contacts were detected by the [[Chromosome conformation capture assay]] (formaldehyde fixation, restriction digestion, ligation, nested PCR, Sanger sequencing) and quantified with the [[CIS-dPCR chromatin interaction strength assay]], normalised to HBB and a spiked-in GFP DNA.
- Cell lines: [[HepG2]] (hepatocyte-like), [[U-87 MG]] (astrocyte-like), [[HMC3]] (microglia-like) and [[SH-SY5Y]] (neuron-like). APOC1 mRNA was measured by TaqMan RT-qPCR against ACTB.
- Brain samples: APOE genotype by TaqMan for [[APOE rs429358]] and [[APOE rs7412]] (ε3/ε3 n = 10, ε3/ε4 n = 10, ε4/ε4 n = 5), the [[APOC1 rs11568822]] insertion/deletion by HpaI digestion, and methylation of the APOE differentially methylated region I (27 CpG sites) by bisulfite pyrosequencing ([[DNA methylation]]).
- Statistics: t-tests, standardised linear regression with interaction terms, and [[Causal mediation analysis]] in R.

## Findings

- **Two conserved contacts.** The APOE promoter contacted a region spanning [[TOMM40]] introns 2 to 3 in U-87 and HMC3 cells but not in SH-SY5Y, consistent with earlier reports in primary human astrocytes and microglia; that TOMM40 region carries the enhancer mark H3K4me1 and the risk SNP [[TOMM40 rs2075650]]. The APOE exon 4 CpG island contacted the APOC1 promoter in all four cell lines and in every brain sample. Several TOMM40 intron 2 to 6 sites contacted the same APOE promoter region with different restriction enzymes, confirming the reciprocal contact.
- **Strength tracks expression across cell types.** Contact strength between the APOE exon 4 island and the APOC1 promoter differed between the four lines, and the lines with stronger contacts had higher APOC1 mRNA.
- **Alzheimer brain.** Contact strength was higher in Alzheimer than control frontal lobe (p < 0.05) and APOC1 mRNA was higher (p < 0.005). Strength and expression were inversely correlated in controls (r = -0.56, p = 0.09) and positively, but not significantly, in Alzheimer samples (r = 0.39, p = 0.15).
- **APOE genotype.** ε3/ε4 brains had stronger contacts than ε3/ε3 (p < 0.05), with a moderate positive link to APOC1 mRNA (r = 0.61, p = 0.06); ε4/ε4 brains showed little extra looping (standardised beta 0.024) yet the highest APOC1 expression (beta 0.570). The combination of Alzheimer disease and ε3/ε4 gave the largest effects (beta 0.427 for contact strength, 0.651 for APOC1 mRNA); the Alzheimer plus ε4/ε4 estimate could not be fitted. The authors read the ε4/ε4 pattern as a decoupling of chromatin architecture from transcriptional output.
- **APOC1 promoter variant.** Contact strength differed by [[APOC1 rs11568822]] genotype (p < 0.05), highest in heterozygotes; APOC1 mRNA followed the same trend without significance (p > 0.09). The concordance with the ε4 pattern is taken to mean strong linkage between the insertion allele and ε4.
- **Methylation is a minor player.** Methylation of the APOE island did not differ between Alzheimer and control (p = 0.383). It correlated positively with contact strength in controls (r = 0.65, p = 0.04) and negatively in ε3/ε3 carriers (r = -0.69, p = 0.03). Mediation analysis found a minimal methylation-mediated effect on either contact strength or APOC1 mRNA; the total effect on contact strength was significant (p = 0.042) and the direct effect close to it (p = 0.066).
- **Interpretation.** The authors call the hypermethylated APOE exon 4 island a noncanonical enhancer of APOC1 (it lacks H3K4me1, H3K27ac and p300 but shows enhancer activity in their earlier reporter assays), propose that TOMM40, APOE and APOC1 are coregulated through [[Chromatin looping]], and suggest allele-specific DNA mimetics targeting locus enhancers and silencers as a therapeutic idea. No such intervention was tested.

## Why This Source Matters

It moves the question of why the ε4 allele raises Alzheimer risk from the apoE protein to the three-dimensional wiring of the locus, giving a quantitative, brain-level readout of a TOMM40-APOE-APOC1 contact that changes with disease status and genotype. It also supplies a reusable assay for measuring a single chromatin contact in absolute terms, complementary to genome-wide [[Hi-C chromatin interaction analysis]] and to the haplotype and expression findings in [[Non-coding risk haplotypes at the APOE locus in Alzheimer disease]] and [[TOMM40 RNA upregulation in Alzheimer disease brain and mitochondrial dysfunction]].

## Assessment

- Small samples: 15 Alzheimer and 10 control brains, with only 5 ε4/ε4 and no ε2 carriers, so several key results sit at p of 0.05 to 0.15 and the ε4/ε4 conclusions rest on very few samples.
- The Alzheimer and genotype groups overlap (ε4 carriers are mostly cases), so disease and genotype effects are only partly separable despite the interaction models; the regression estimate for Alzheimer plus ε4/ε4 was unavailable.
- Brain tissue is bulk frontal lobe, so the contact and mRNA signal mixes neurons and glia, whereas the cell-line data show cell-type dependence; hepatoma, glioblastoma and neuroblastoma lines are imperfect stand-ins.
- Correlations between contact strength and APOC1 mRNA are mostly non-significant and no perturbation (deleting the island or blocking the loop) was done, so the claim that looping drives expression is inferential. The authors cite earlier reporter assays for enhancer activity.
- The rs11568822 and ε4 linkage is inferred from matching patterns rather than from phased haplotypes. The cited ancestry-specific regulation findings were not tested in this cohort, whose ancestry is not stated in the text.
- Tables of demographics, regression and mediation output and the supplementary figures were not part of the text read, so exact group sizes by sex and age are not recorded here.

## Open Questions

- Does deleting or silencing the APOE exon 4 CpG island change APOC1 expression in microglia or astrocytes (only correlative evidence plus earlier reporter assays so far)?
- Is the weak chromatin remodelling in ε4/ε4 brain, despite high APOC1 mRNA, reproduced in a larger series of ε4 homozygotes (only five were studied)?
- Does the insertion allele of rs11568822 strengthen the APOE-APOC1 contact independently of ε4 (the two were not separated by phasing)?
- Which cell types in Alzheimer frontal lobe carry the stronger APOE-APOC1 contact, and do they match the glia-specific TOMM40 contact seen in cell lines (bulk tissue was analysed)?
- Does the contact between the APOE promoter and TOMM40 intron 2 to 3 depend on the rs2075650 allele (proposed by the authors but not tested)?
