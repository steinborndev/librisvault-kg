# Pharmacogenetics of anxiety and depression in Alzheimer disease patients

A retrospective cohort study of 1006 patients with [[Alzheimer disease]] from one neuroscience and genomic medicine centre. It measures how common and how severe anxiety and depression are, how they differ between women and men, and whether [[APOE]] genotype and the metabolizer class of [[CYP2D6]], [[CYP2C19]] and [[CYP2C9]] predict how much the symptoms improve after one month of a multifactorial treatment.

## Design

- Patients: 591 women and 415 men, mean age 67.7 years (range 50 to 96), diagnosed with Alzheimer-type neurocognitive disorder by DSM-V and NINCDS-ADRDA criteria, recruited from a clinic database between 2000 and 2010 and followed to 2020.
- Treatment, given for a year (this analysis uses baseline and month 1): [[Citicoline]], piracetam, a lipid-lowering nutraceutical, an iron, folate and vitamin B12 complex, and supplements for documented deficiencies; enalapril, [[Atorvastatin]] or metformin for hypertension, hypercholesterolemia or diabetes; about 5% received [[Donepezil]]; about 25% with moderate to severe depression received [[Fluoxetine]], [[Paroxetine]] or [[Sertraline]]; 18% with agitation or moderate to severe anxiety received [[Alprazolam]] or lorazepam for a month; under 3% needed a neuroleptic.
- Outcomes: Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HARS) and Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS) at baseline and after one month; also MMSE, ADAS, blood chemistry, haematology, blood pressure and electrocardiogram.
- Genotyping: TaqMan real-time PCR (single assays and OpenArray chips) of SNPs in 18 Alzheimer-pathogenesis genes and a panel of 113 SNPs and 3 copy number variants in 60 pharmacogenetic genes; the analysed genes were APOE ([[APOE rs7412]], [[APOE rs429358]]), CYP2C19, CYP2C9 and CYP2D6.
- Statistics: t-test, Mann-Whitney, chi-squared, Fisher exact and Pearson correlation in SPSS and SigmaPlot; significance at p < 0.05, no multiple-testing correction described.

## Findings

- Sex: anxiety was present in nearly 60% and depression in almost 70% of patients, and both were more frequent and more severe in women. Mild, moderate and severe anxiety were 48.9%, 12.3% and 1.7% overall; moderate and severe anxiety were about twice as common in women. Baseline HARS was 12.5 in women and 9.9 in men; baseline HDRS 10.9 and 9.1. Women also had lower red blood cell indices and poorer metabolic status; men had more cardiovascular risk factors, higher glucose and more abnormal electrocardiograms (54% versus 43%), but mood disorders were not linked to vascular or glycaemic measures. See [[Sex differences in Alzheimer disease]].
- Response: both scales improved significantly after one month in women and in men (HARS 11.4 to 9.8, HDRS 10.1 to 8.6 overall), with similar response by sex. Cognition rose over the first six months and fell afterwards, as the authors report in earlier work.
- APOE genotype frequencies: 3/3 61.8%, 3/4 24.5%, 2/3 8.5%, 4/4 3.5%, 2/4 1.4%, 2/2 0.2%. Baseline anxiety and depression differed between several genotypes (4/4 carriers and 2/4 carriers stood out). The best responders were 3/3 and 3/4 carriers for both scales, with 2/4 also significant for anxiety. The authors summarise this as APOE-3 carriers responding best and APOE-4 carriers tending to respond worst.
- CYP2D6 classes: normal 57.7%, intermediate 30.6%, poor 5.6%, ultra-rapid 6.1%. Baseline anxiety was higher in poor metabolizers than in normal and intermediate ones. Only normal and intermediate metabolizers showed significant improvement in both scales.
- CYP2C19 classes: normal (extensive) 71.2%, intermediate 25.8%, poor 1.4%, ultra-rapid 1.7%. Only normal and intermediate metabolizers improved significantly in anxiety; for depression, poor metabolizers had no response and ultra-rapid ones minimal improvement.
- CYP2C9 classes: normal 62.3%, intermediate 32.6%, poor 5.2%. Normal and intermediate metabolizers improved significantly (p < 0.001) on both scales; poor metabolizers improved mildly and not significantly.
- Overall conclusion: normal and intermediate metabolizers of the three enzymes were better responders than poor and ultra-rapid ones, and APOE-4 carriers together with CYP-related poor and ultra-rapid metabolizers (over 40% of the patients, by the authors' reckoning) are a vulnerable group. See [[Cytochrome P450 metabolizer phenotypes]].
- Variants defining the classes: [[CYP2C19 rs4244285]] and [[CYP2C19 rs12248560]], [[CYP2C9 rs1799853]] and [[CYP2C9 rs1057910]], [[CYP2D6 rs3892097]], plus CYP2D6 *3, *6, *41 and gene deletion or duplication alleles and CYP2C9 *5.

## Discussion points

- Pharmacogenetics is credited with about 80% of the variability in drug efficacy and safety, and a trigenic CYP2D6, CYP2C9 and CYP2C19 cluster gives 134 genotypes and 33 phenotypes, of which only 23.6% of people are extensive metabolizers for all three.
- Antidepressants are cited as substrates mainly of CYP2D6 (86%), CYP3A4, CYP2C19 and CYP1A2; fluoxetine, paroxetine and sertraline are described enzyme by enzyme. In this cohort over 80% of patients given one of the three improved, but the authors attribute this partly to the complementary treatment of concomitant illness. They note that placebo-controlled meta-analyses find antidepressants largely ineffective for depression in Alzheimer disease.
- Benzodiazepines are mostly CYP3A4 substrates; alprazolam at low dose relieved anxiety without clear cognitive or psychomotor cost in this cohort.
- Genome-wide studies of depression are cited: 31 genes in 19 risk loci, [[ACE]] variants influencing mood in Alzheimer disease, and an HTR1A variant and CYP2C19 rs12248560 associated with depression severity; no new analysis of these is reported.

## Why This Source Matters

It supplies a large single-clinic view of mood symptoms in Alzheimer disease that ties sex, [[APOE]] and drug-metabolizing enzyme status to the same outcome, and it supports using metabolizer class when choosing among SSRIs and benzodiazepines. It extends the same group's treatment-response work on cognition ([[SIRT2 genotype with APOE and CYP2D6 in Alzheimer disease treatment response]]) from memory scores to anxiety and depression. See also [[Neuropsychiatric symptoms of Alzheimer disease]] and [[Pharmacogenetics]].

## Assessment

- There is no placebo or untreated arm, and the regimen bundles psychotropics, supplements and treatment of comorbidities, so improvement at one month cannot be assigned to any drug or genotype-specific drug handling; the authors themselves say improvement cannot be attributed to psychotropics alone.
- "Better responders" is judged from whether each genotype group's own score change reached significance, not from a direct comparison of change between groups; poor and ultra-rapid groups are small (CYP2C19 poor 1.4%, ultra-rapid 1.7%; CYP2C9 poor 5.2%), so lack of significance there may be lack of power.
- The APOE message is mixed: 3/4 carriers were among the significant responders, which sits uneasily with the abstract statement that APOE-4 carriers respond worst; 4/4 carriers were only 3.5% of the sample.
- Many pairwise baseline comparisons are made with p values near 0.05 and no correction; psychotropic doses were not matched to metabolizer status, and drug use was recorded by category only.
- Anxiety and depression were rated with scales designed for non-demented patients, and rater blinding to genotype is not stated; the data sit in an institutional database and are not public.
- Metabolizer class was inferred from a limited allele panel (for example no CYP3A4 or CYP2B6 variants), and the CYP2C19 class list in the text places *2A/*17 genotypes with extensive metabolizers without explanation.
- The report that moderate and severe anxiety are far more common in women comes from percentages within each sex; absolute numbers per category are in tables not reproduced in the text.

## Open Questions

- Does adjusting CYP2D6-dependent antidepressant or benzodiazepine dosing to metabolizer class improve anxiety and depression scores in Alzheimer disease compared with standard dosing (this study has no randomised or untreated comparison arm)?
- Do APOE 3/4 carriers differ from APOE 4/4 carriers in response of mood symptoms to treatment (4/4 carriers were only 3.5% of the sample, and 3/4 carriers responded significantly)?
- Is the higher burden of anxiety and depression in women with Alzheimer disease explained by iron, folate, red blood cell or other metabolic differences, or by hormonal and psychosocial factors (the study reports associations only)?
- Would a randomised trial of one SSRI versus placebo, stratified by CYP2D6 and CYP2C19 class, reproduce the better response of normal and intermediate metabolizers (the cohort was open-label, with multiple concurrent drugs)?
