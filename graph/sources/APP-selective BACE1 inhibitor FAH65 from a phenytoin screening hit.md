# APP-selective BACE1 inhibitor FAH65 from a phenytoin screening hit

A drug-discovery paper on a new class of [[BACE1]] inhibitors that block the cut of [[APP]] but spare other BACE1 substrates, called [[APP-selective BACE1 inhibitors]] (ASBIs). A screen with a maltose-binding-protein-APPC125 substrate flagged the antiepileptic [[Phenytoin]] as a weak hit; converting its hydantoin core to a fluoro aminohydantoin (FAH) and tuning the two aromatic rings gave a series ending in [[FAH65]], whose active (-) enantiomer, FAH65E(-), is the preclinical candidate for [[Alzheimer disease]].

## Design

- Screening and chemistry: MBP-APPC125 BACE1 cleavage screen, then analogs FAH1, FAH3, FAH17 and FAH65 tested in the cell-free fluorogenic P5-P5' BACE1 assay, in cell assays and in selectivity assays. Synthesis and structure-activity detail are deferred to supplementary material and a future paper.
- Selectivity panels: alkaline-phosphatase-tagged [[NRG1]] and [[SELPLG]] fusions in [[HEK293]] cells with BACE1; cell-free [[CTSD]] and [[BACE2]] activity; surface plasmon resonance binding to APP; hERG ([[KCNH2]]) channel binding. Comparators were the research inhibitor BACE-IV, [[Verubecestat]] and [[Lanabecestat]].
- Cells: [[CHO]] cells expressing wild-type human APP (CHO-7W) or Swedish-mutant APP (CHO-7WSwe); HEK293T cells expressing GFP-tagged full-length APP.
- Pharmacokinetics: oral or subcutaneous dosing at 10 or 30 mg/kg in C57Bl6 mice and Sprague-Dawley rats, tissue levels by LC-MS/MS.
- Efficacy: [[B254 mouse]] animals (4-5 months old, both sexes) given 30 mg/kg twice daily by pipette feeding for 10 days (pilot, n = 8) or 26 days (n = 11), then tested in the [[Novel object recognition test]] and novel location recognition; brain biomarkers by ELISA, AlphaLISA and Meso Scale Discovery. A separate pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic study of FAH65E(-) used [[APOE4-TR-5xFAD mouse]] animals (n = 3 per time point).

## Findings

- Potency ladder in the P5-P5' assay: FAH1 about 10 μM, FAH3 about 1 μM, FAH17 about 0.3 μM, FAH65 about 0.01-0.03 μM; the (-) enantiomer FAH65E(-) about 0.005 μM, while the (+) enantiomer is inactive. The active enantiomer is S-configured, from the X-ray structure of the inactive (+) form.
- Substrate selectivity: FAH17 and FAH65 did not inhibit cleavage of NRG1 or PSGL1 (EC50 above 10 μM) against BACE1-APP IC50 values of 0.3 and 0.01 μM. BACE-IV was unselective (about 0.04 μM for APP, about 0.05 μM for NRG1 and PSGL1), and verubecestat and lanabecestat showed no selectivity (NRG1/APP IC50 ratio below 2.5 against more than 200 for FAH65).
- Enzyme selectivity: FAH65 IC50 about 47 μM for cathepsin D, above 1 μM for BACE2 and about 0.01 μM for BACE1. Binding to APP was seen by surface plasmon resonance, offered as a possible basis for substrate selectivity.
- Cells: FAH17 and FAH65 lowered sAPPβ and Aβ1-42 in CHO-7W cells; only FAH65 lowered Aβ1-42 in Swedish-APP cells. FAH65E(-) lowered sAPPβ with an EC50 of about 25 nM and Aβ1-42 from 25 nM, and in HEK cells with GFP-APP it left more full-length APP on the cells at 1 μM.
- Drug-like properties: FAH65 kinetic solubility 88 μM, plasma half-life above 180 min, liver microsome half-life about 38 min, PAMPA permeability predicting brain entry, hERG binding 38.7-41% at 10 μM. FAH65E(-): solubility 79 μM, microsome half-life 49 min, unbound fraction 0.49% in albumin and 5.4% in brain tissue.
- Exposure: oral 30 mg/kg in mice gave plasma Cmax about 50 ng/mL at 2 h and brain Cmax 74 ng/g at 1 h; in rats brain Cmax was 182 ng/g for FAH65 and 157 ng/g for verubecestat. FAH65E(-) reached a brain Cmax of 188 nM in APOE4-TR-5xFAD mice, with brain sAPPβ lower at 2 h.
- Rat pilot: sAPPβ in cerebrospinal fluid and cortex fell, CSF Aβ1-40 was lower with FAH65 than with verubecestat, and cortical sAPPα rose; the authors read effects as greater than verubecestat's.
- B254 mice, 10-day pilot: brain βCTF fell, but novel object performance did not improve significantly and Aβ1-42 and the p-tau/total tau ratio did not differ; brain FAH65 was about 20 ng/g. Neuroinflammation markers [[GFAP]] and [[CXCL10]] (IP-10) were unchanged.
- B254 mice, 26-day study: novelty preference rose in both novel location and novel object tests, brain sAPPα rose and the sAPPβ/sAPPα ratio fell; brain sAPPβ and Aβ1-42 were highly variable and not significantly different; brain FAH65 was about 50 ng/g.
- Dose reasoning: with a 5.4% unbound brain fraction, the unbound brain concentration at Cmax is about 9.6 nM, near the enzyme IC50 (about 5 nM) but below the cell EC50 (25 nM), so the authors expect under 50% BACE1 inhibition and argue that partial inhibition suffices, citing the protective [[APP p.A673T]] variant (about 30% lifelong less BACE cleavage) and mouse data on small Aβ reductions.

## Why This Source Matters

[[BACE1]] inhibitors such as verubecestat and lanabecestat lowered Aβ but failed in the clinic, with worse cognition in some trials, and the failure is commonly put down to blocking non-APP substrates. This paper offers a chemical series built to separate the two, with numbers for the selectivity window against [[NRG1]] and PSGL1, and a mouse readout in which memory improved with only modest brain exposure. It is also a worked example of turning a repurposed-drug screening hit into a lead, and it frames the compound as a possible oral partner or maintenance therapy to [[Anti-amyloid antibody therapy]].

## Assessment

- Efficacy rests on one model ([[B254 mouse]]) with small groups; the 10-day pilot showed target engagement (βCTF) but no memory gain, and in the 26-day study the direct products sAPPβ and Aβ1-42 did not change significantly, so the memory benefit is only indirectly linked to BACE1 inhibition by sAPPα and the sAPPβ/sAPPα ratio.
- The memory result comes from racemic FAH65 at 60 mg/kg/day; the active enantiomer FAH65E(-) was tested only for brain exposure and sAPPβ in a three-per-time-point study without an untreated time zero.
- Brain exposure is low (about 20-50 ng/g in efficacy studies), and by the authors' own estimate unbound drug is below the cell-based efficacious level. The memory effect at such exposure raises the question of whether it is on-target.
- Selectivity is shown for NRG1 and PSGL1 in cell assays and for cathepsin D and BACE2 in enzyme assays; no in vivo measurement of NRG1 or other substrate processing is reported yet, though the authors list it as a next step. The chemistry, binding data and selectivity table are in supplementary material that was not read.
- The comparison with verubecestat in rats is a pilot with missing early cerebrospinal fluid time points, and brain Aβ1-42 varied with both drugs.
- One of the study's tests (Morris water maze) is not used because earlier reports of this mouse line conflict; the authors note this and align with studies that found impairment at 5-7 months.

## Open Questions

- Does FAH65E(-) leave neuregulin-1 and PSGL1 processing intact in mouse brain after chronic oral dosing (selectivity was shown in cell and enzyme assays; in vivo substrate measurements are listed as future work)?
- Does the memory benefit in B254 mice persist with the pure active enantiomer FAH65E(-) at doses that give measurable brain sAPPβ and Aβ reduction (the efficacy studies used the racemate with variable brain Aβ readouts)?
- How does APP binding by the FAH compounds, seen by surface plasmon resonance, translate into selective blocking of APP at the BACE1 active site (only pilot binding data are in the main text; the structural basis is not shown)?
- Is partial BACE1 inhibition of under 50% enough to lower plaque load in a mouse model, and can it be combined with an anti-amyloid antibody (proposed but untested)?
