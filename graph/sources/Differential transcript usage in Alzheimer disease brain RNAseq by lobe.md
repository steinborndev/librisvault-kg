# Differential transcript usage in Alzheimer disease brain RNAseq by lobe

A reanalysis of three public postmortem brain RNAseq resources, the Mayo Clinic, Mount Sinai Brain Bank and ROSMAP cohorts (see [[AMP-AD brain RNAseq cohorts]]), that asks what [[Alzheimer disease]] changes in gene expression when temporal lobe (affected early) and frontal lobe (affected late) are compared, and whether looking at transcript isoforms, not only whole-gene counts, reveals more. Reads were re-pseudo-aligned with Kallisto to one reference, genes were tested for differential expression (DESeq2) and for differential transcript usage ([[Differential transcript usage analysis]], IsoformSwitchAnalyzeR), and the changed genes were assigned to cell types indirectly through single-cell signatures from the middle temporal gyrus ([[Single-cell RNA sequencing]], Allen Brain Atlas data).

## Design

- Cohorts and regions: Mayo temporal cortex (160 subjects, 82 Alzheimer and 78 control); Mount Sinai Brodmann areas 22 (159), 36 (154), 10 (176) and 44 (153); ROSMAP dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (423). Areas 22 and 36 and Mayo were grouped as temporal lobe, areas 10 and 44 and ROSMAP as frontal lobe.
- Cases carried Braak stage above 4 (and a definite clinical or neuropathological diagnosis); RNA integrity number and postmortem interval were covariates.
- Differentially expressed genes: fold change above 1.3 and FDR below 0.01. Differential transcript usage: differential isoform fraction above 0.05 with overall FDR below 0.01 after stageR confirmation. Only genes found in at least two datasets of the same lobe were carried forward (temporal lobe intersection, TLI; frontal lobe intersection, FLI).
- Enrichment with g:Profiler ([[Gene set enrichment analysis]]) under three significance conditions; cell-type assignment of a gene required expression in more than 10% of cells of a type among seven types (astrocytes, endothelial cells, glutamatergic neurons, GABAergic neurons, microglia, oligodendrocytes, oligodendrocyte precursors).
- Western blotting of BIN1 in frontal cortex and hippocampus from three low-pathology controls and six Alzheimer brains (Braak 6), with HEK cells expressing BIN1 isoforms 1 and 9 as band controls.

## Findings

- Expression change is concentrated in the temporal lobe: 3348 differentially expressed genes in Mount Sinai temporal lobe and 2172 in Mayo, with 734 shared (TLI); only 327 in Mount Sinai frontal lobe and 209 in ROSMAP, with 31 shared (FLI). Of the frontal-lobe genes, 62.5% were also changed in the temporal lobe, which the authors read as the same changes arriving later. The gap was not explained by sample number, since frontal groups were larger and the same donors were used in Mount Sinai.
- Shared temporal genes included [[ABCA1]], [[ABCA2]], [[C1R]], [[C1S]], [[REST]] and the GABAergic markers [[GAD1]], [[GAD2]], [[SLC32A1]], [[CALB1]], [[PVALB]], [[SST]] and [[VIP]]; [[VGF]] was down in both lobes.
- Enrichment of the shared differentially expressed genes gave only generic signalling terms in the temporal lobe and nothing in the frontal lobe.
- Differential transcript usage found 2509 (Mayo) and 1843 (Mount Sinai) usage-changed genes in temporal lobe and 59 (ROSMAP) and 855 (Mount Sinai) in frontal lobe; 435 were shared in temporal and 13 in frontal lobe. Only 34 of the 435 shared temporal genes were also differentially expressed, so the two methods saw largely different genes, and usage-changed genes added vesicle-mediated transport and synapse terms that expression change alone did not show. Examples were [[APP]], [[NSG1]], [[RELN]] and [[GABRA1]].
- Switches were most often linked to alternative transcription start site, alternative transcription end site and exon skipping, then alternative 3' and 5' sites, multi-exon skipping and intron retention ([[Alternative splicing]]). The predicted consequences differed between Mayo (mostly loss of coding potential and domains) and Mount Sinai area 36 (balanced), which the authors tie in part to Mayo's higher read depth.
- Using [[Braak staging]] in Mount Sinai (196 low, 133 mid, 308 high samples), most usage-changed genes appeared only when high stage was compared with low or mid. Of 441 splicing-related genes, 79 were differentially expressed at high versus low or mid stage and none at mid versus low, including [[RBFOX1]], [[RBFOX2]], [[ELAVL2]], [[MBNL3]], [[PTBP1]] and [[NOVA2]]. Which splicing genes changed differed between Mayo and Mount Sinai area 36.
- Of 1135 temporal genes that were differentially expressed or usage-changed, 839 were expressed in at least one cell type. Microglia were enriched for inflammation terms, endothelial cells for cell adhesion, and neurons for synaptic signalling, plasticity and the synaptic vesicle cycle, more strongly in GABAergic than glutamatergic neurons. NSG1, CALB1 and RELN were assigned to GABAergic neurons only.
- Among 176 genes at 45 risk loci plus [[PSEN1]], [[PSEN2]] and APP, 116 were expressed in brain cell types and 54 were differentially expressed or usage-changed in at least one dataset; 52 of those in temporal lobe, including [[ADAM10]], [[BIN1]], [[CLU]] and [[TREM2]]. Eight were changed in both temporal datasets.
- BIN1: transcripts for the neuronal clathrin-binding isoform 1 (and isoform 9) fell while isoform 6 and intron-retaining transcripts rose, predicting less neuronal isoform 1; western blots showed lower isoform 1 protein in Alzheimer frontal cortex and hippocampus.
- APP: two isoforms lacking exon 7 (the Kunitz protease inhibitor, KPI, domain) fell and two rose, in the Mayo and Mount Sinai data. The authors infer a neuronal rise in KPI-containing APP and more Aβ1-42, an inference from earlier work rather than something measured here, and propose that lower RBFOX proteins could drive the exon 7 inclusion.

## Assessment

- Bulk tissue with indirect cell-type assignment: gene-to-cell-type calls rest on expression in more than 10% of cells from a different (middle temporal gyrus) single-cell dataset, so cell-type specificity is inferred, not measured, and the cell-type counts in the text (239, 396 and 211) differ from the figure legend (281, 249 and 77) and from the 839 total.
- Neuron loss is argued against rather than excluded; the authors cite near-equal up and down fractions in neurons and unchanged usage in genes with stable totals.
- Case-control comparison with Braak stages mostly above 4 in cases, so the stage analysis in Mount Sinai is confounded with diagnosis (305 of 308 high-Braak samples were clinical Alzheimer cases).
- The BIN1 blot uses three controls and six cases; the APP isoform conclusions are unvalidated at protein level. Several thresholds are printed inconsistently in the text (FDR above versus below 0.01; an area labelled 26 for 36).
- Stringent two-dataset overlap reduced the gene lists and the authors report that enrichment on expression change alone was weaker than in a prior ROSMAP study using looser cutoffs.

## Why This Source Matters

It shows that whole-gene differential expression and isoform-level usage give largely non-overlapping gene sets in Alzheimer brain, that both are much stronger in early-affected temporal cortex than in late-affected frontal cortex, and that isoform switches track high-grade pathology and altered splicing-factor expression. It also gives a specific, testable pair of isoform changes in two central Alzheimer genes: loss of neuronal BIN1 isoform 1 and a shift in the KPI-bearing APP isoforms.

## Open Questions

- Is the shift toward Kunitz protease inhibitor-containing APP isoforms in Alzheimer neurons seen at the protein level in human brain (the isoform change was predicted from transcript data and prior literature only)?
- Does restoring RBFOX1 or RBFOX2 expression change APP exon 7 inclusion and amyloid-beta production in human neurons (proposed as a therapeutic idea but untested)?
- Do the temporal and frontal lobe differences in isoform switching hold in single-nucleus data from the same donors (cell types were assigned indirectly from a separate middle temporal gyrus dataset)?
- Why do different splicing-factor genes change in the Mayo and Mount Sinai area 36 datasets (read depth and cohort differences were suggested but not tested)?
