# Multiomic analysis of Alzheimer brain finds five upregulated genes and antrocin

An in-silico study (Pharmaceutics, 2021) that pooled differentially expressed genes from public brain microarray datasets of [[Alzheimer disease]] patients and controls, looked for genes raised in every dataset, and then screened one natural compound, [[Antrocin]] (a sesquiterpene lactone from the Taiwanese fungus *Antrodia cinnamomea*), against the encoded proteins by [[Molecular docking]] and pharmacokinetic prediction. No wet-lab work is reported.

## Design

- Data: five Gene Expression Omnibus microarray sets (accessed 21 September 2021): GSE26927 (central nervous system tissue, 63 Alzheimer and 54 control samples), GSE160208 (frontal cortex and cerebellum from 27 [[Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease]] cases and 20 controls, included because the authors regard both diseases as conformational disorders with shared pathology), GSE5281 (161 samples: 87 Alzheimer, 74 non-demented), GSE36980 (frontal and temporal cortex and hippocampus from 33 Alzheimer cases and 47 controls) and GSE39420 (14 cases, 7 controls). Control subjects were cognitively normal.
- Differential expression: [[Limma differential expression analysis]] with Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate below 0.05; overlaps shown with the Multiple List Comparator.
- Downstream: brain-specific co-expression and miRNA networks (NetworkAnalyst, TarBase, miRTarBase, miRanda, TargetScan, miEAA), a STRING protein interaction network (confidence 0.70), KEGG and Gene Ontology enrichment (Enrichr), gene-disease associations from the Open Targets platform (score 0.15), and protein localisation and regional brain expression from the Human Protein Atlas.
- Antrocin: SwissADME, PASS, a support vector machine and LiCABEDS blood-brain barrier server (threshold 0.02, trained on 1,593 compounds), the BOILED-Egg model, and GUSAR QSAR models of rat acute toxicity; see [[Blood-brain barrier permeability prediction]]. Docking used AutoDock Vina 0.8 (exhaustiveness 8, 40 x 40 x 40 Å box, rigid receptor) against crystal structures of CAMK4 (2W4O), ATP6V1A (6XBY), TIPRL (5WOW), BNIP3 (2J5D) and TOMM70 (3FP3).

## Findings

- Five genes were overexpressed in Alzheimer samples in every dataset compared: [[ATP6V1A]], [[BNIP3]], [[CAMK4]], [[TIPRL]] and [[TOMM70]].
- Localisation (from Human Protein Atlas immunofluorescence in A-431 and A549 cells): ATP6V1A in cytosol and nucleoplasm, BNIP3 in cytosol, CAMK4 in nucleoplasm, TIPRL in vesicles and cytosol, TOMM70 in mitochondria. Mean expression was significantly higher in frontal cortex (Brodmann area 9) and lowest in hippocampus.
- Co-expression: a brain-specific network of 986 nodes and 1,212 edges, with the strongest clustering to OPA1, ITFG1, OXCT1, ATP2A2, [[MAPK1]], CDK14, MAP2K4, YWHAB, [[PRKN]] (PARK2), CMAS, HSPA12A and RGS17. The protein interaction network had 45 nodes and 435 edges; TOMM70 and ATP6V1A clustered tightly while BNIP3, CAMK4 and TIPRL formed a minimal cluster.
- miRNA targets of the five genes were enriched for central nervous system diseases including [[Parkinson disease]], [[Down syndrome]], inflammation and cerebral infarction.
- Enrichment: KEGG terms for fatty acid biosynthesis, [[Ferroptosis]], long-term potentiation, amphetamine addiction, adipocytokine and PPAR signalling, synaptic vesicle cycle and peroxisome; molecular functions of ATPase and calcium-dependent protein kinase activity; processes including myeloid leukocyte differentiation, dendritic cell cytokine production, cellular response to oxygen levels and mitochondrial protein catabolism. Open Targets linked ATP6V1A/BNIP3 and CAMK4/TIPRL/TOMM70 to developmental disability, schizophrenia, intellectual disability and abnormal cerebral white matter. The authors read this as evidence for [[Mitochondrial dysfunction]], inflammation and Alzheimer pathways.
- Antrocin: predicted blood-brain barrier score 0.038 against a threshold of 0.02; molecular weight 234.33 Da; no PAINS alerts; not a P-glycoprotein substrate; not a substrate or inhibitor of CYP1A2, CYP2C19, CYP2D6 or CYP3A4; bioavailability score 0.55; predicted high gastrointestinal absorption. Predicted rat LD50 values were 618 (intraperitoneal), 26 (intravenous), 804.3 (oral inhalation) and 517.3 (subcutaneous), reported as g/kg, from which the authors infer a high safety margin.
- Docking affinities (kcal/mol): TOMM70 -6.80, CAMK4 -6.70, TIPRL -6.60, ATP6V1A -5.90, BNIP3 -5.80. Antrocin made three hydrogen bonds with CAMK4 (THR291, HIS156, PRO220) and three with TIPRL (THR208, SER173, PHE254), one with TOMM70 (SER271) and one with ATP6V1A (HIS96), and none with BNIP3, which the authors link to its weakest score.
- The discussion cites an earlier clinical report of two patients with de novo TOM70 C-terminal changes (T607I, I554F) who had white matter abnormalities, hypotonia, dystonia and cognitive deficits, and an earlier report of ATP6V1A and OXCT1 upregulation in Alzheimer cohorts.

## Why This Source Matters

It offers a small, testable gene signature (a mitochondrial and kinase-heavy set) drawn from several independent brain expression datasets, and pairs it with a natural-product candidate whose binding and brain penetration are so far only predicted. It is also a clear example of how far a purely computational pipeline can be pushed with free web tools, and where it stops: docking scores of about -6 to -7 kcal/mol are modest and carry no information on whether binding would raise or lower the target's activity.

## Assessment

- All drug evidence is computational. The authors acknowledge there is no RT-PCR confirmation of the five genes and say animal-model and clinical-sample validation are ongoing.
- The study design is inconsistent: the abstract says six datasets and that the genes were common to five, the methods describe five datasets, and the results say the five genes were found in all five. One of the five sets (GSE160208) is a prion disease cohort run on a targeted NanoString myeloid innate immunity panel, which makes an overlap with whole-genome arrays narrow by construction.
- The differential expression cut-off is stated as a log fold-change threshold with no value given; the datasets mix brain regions and platforms, and Table 1 details (ages, sexes, regions) were not available in the text read.
- Pooled lists of "upregulated" genes do not show that the genes cause disease; the enrichment terms (for example amphetamine addiction) are generic, and the inference to mitochondrial dysfunction rests on ontology terms for myeloid and dendritic cell processes, which is an indirect argument.
- The rat acute toxicity units are given as g/kg body weight, which is implausibly large (the underlying model works in log mmol/kg), and the oral-inhalation route is labelled "o.p". The "high safety" conclusion should be read with that in mind.
- Docking ran a rigid receptor with default settings and no redocking, no known reference ligand, and no check that the grid box covered a functional pocket. The text calls -5.8 to -6.8 kcal/mol "strong".
- "Parthenogenesis" in the abstract and conclusion is evidently a slip for pathogenesis, and the abstract writes TIPRL as "T1PRL".

## Open Questions

- Does antrocin bind CAMK4, TOMM70 or TIPRL in a biochemical assay, and does it change their activity (the study reports docking predictions only)?
- Are ATP6V1A, BNIP3, CAMK4, TIPRL and TOMM70 raised at protein level in Alzheimer brain (the study used microarray mRNA data only, and the authors' own RT-PCR validation is described as ongoing)?
- Is the upregulation of these five genes specific to Alzheimer disease or shared with other neurodegenerative dementias (one prion disease cohort was pooled in, but its separate result is not given)?
- Why is expression of the signature highest in frontal cortex and lowest in hippocampus, a region affected early in Alzheimer disease (the regional data come from the Human Protein Atlas, not from Alzheimer tissue, and the text gives no explanation)?
- Would the five genes still overlap if the whole-genome microarray datasets were analysed without the targeted-panel prion disease cohort (the overlap analysis as described includes it)?
