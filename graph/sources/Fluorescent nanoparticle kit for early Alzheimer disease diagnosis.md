# Fluorescent nanoparticle kit for early Alzheimer disease diagnosis

A proof-of-concept study of a blood-based diagnostic kit for [[Alzheimer disease]]. Quantum-dot nanoparticle probes (a [[Molecular beacon nanoparticle sensor]]) detect eight microRNAs and a panel of protein markers by turning on fluorescence when the target binds.

## Design

- Marker selection: microRNA levels were screened by microarray in [[HT22]] mouse hippocampal cells with or without amyloid beta 1-42 oligomer, and protein levels were screened in plasma of non-transgenic mice (6) and an APP/PS/ApoE knockdown Alzheimer model (6).
- Selected microRNAs: [[MIR106B]], [[MIR146B]], [[MIR181A1]], [[MIR200A]], [[MIR34A]], [[MIR124-1]] (called miR-124b), [[MIR153-1]] and [[MIR155]].
- Selected proteins: amyloid beta 1-42 monomer and oligomer ([[APP]]), [[MAPT]] (tau), [[UCHL1]], [[NLRP3]], [[STAT3]], [[SORL1]], [[CLU]], [[APOE]] (APOE3 and APOE4 forms), [[RTN4]] (Nogo-A), [[IL13]], [[NAMPT]] (visfatin) and NF-kB.
- Validation: HT22 cells (5 μM oligomer for 2 hours, confocal imaging), brain sections of control, ApoE knockdown, APP/PS knockdown and APP/PS/ApoE knockdown mice, and plasma from human volunteers. Clinical status was set by neurologists at Bundang Seoul National University Hospital using the CERAD-K assessment and PET imaging. Six plasma samples (one healthy, five patients; all women aged 70-75) were obtained, with four patient samples profiled in full.

## Findings

- In HT22 cells, the sensors for miR-155, miR-181c, miR-9 and miR-200a gave fluorescence, and miR-106b, miR-153 and miR-155 changed most after oligomer treatment, matching the microarray screen.
- In the mouse brain, amyloid beta 1-42 and NLRP3 stained strongly and STAT3 and tau stained clearly in the APP/PS/ApoE knockdown model, with no signal in controls; amyloid beta 1-42 and Nogo-A were co-localised.
- In plasma of four patients, tau and amyloid beta 1-42 oligomer rose with severity. The authors read patient P1 as severe (high tau and oligomer), P2 and P3 as early (low tau, high oligomer) and P4 as probably [[Mild cognitive impairment]] (slightly high tau, very low oligomer), and state that this matched the clinical diagnoses.
- miR-106b and miR-153 followed disease stage and are proposed as early markers; miR-124b, miR-34a and miR-153 were lower in the third patient; miR-155 showed no clear pattern despite being a known Alzheimer-related microRNA.

## Why This Source Matters

It demonstrates a design in which one probe chemistry can be retargeted to any microRNA or antigen, and combines microRNA and protein markers of amyloid, tau and inflammation in a cheap blood test aimed at the preclinical stage of Alzheimer disease. The marker list and the staging logic are hypotheses for later validation rather than established diagnostics.

## Assessment

- The human evidence is four patient plasma samples, with no sensitivity, specificity or statistical test reported, and the staging is judged against clinical diagnosis by the same authors' reading of the signals.
- Controls in the human work are one healthy volunteer (the main text mentions two normal plasma samples in one figure), so separation of patients from healthy people is barely tested.
- The mouse model is described inconsistently (called both knockdown and knockout), and the early-stage claim rests on markers in advanced-disease mice and patients already diagnosed, not on people before diagnosis.
- Nanoparticle cores contain cadmium, and quantitative fluorescence data were in figures and tables not available in the text read.
- The kit is positioned against earlier patented diagnostic indices (glutamine synthetase, DNA methylation) and against ELISA and FISH, but no head-to-head comparison is reported.

## Open Questions

- Do the plasma microRNA patterns of miR-106b and miR-153 separate people with preclinical Alzheimer disease from healthy controls in a cohort of hundreds of participants (only four patients were profiled)?
- How does the fluorescent nanoparticle read-out compare in sensitivity and specificity with plasma ELISA or amyloid PET for staging Alzheimer disease (no head-to-head comparison was reported)?
- Why does miR-155, a microRNA known to regulate Alzheimer-related inflammation, show no clear pattern in patient plasma when it responds to amyloid beta oligomer in HT22 cells?
