# Plasma lipidome of Alzheimer disease and associations with risk gene SNPs

A 2021 Translational Psychiatry study that profiles 778 plasma lipid species in 82 older Australians (40 with [[Alzheimer disease]], 42 cognitively normal), tests whether lipid profiles separate the two groups, checks whether [[Amyloid-beta]] oligomers change the lipid profile of cultured astroglioma cells in the same direction, and asks whether the lipids associate differently with Alzheimer risk variants in cases and controls.

## Design

- Cohort: a subsample of the Sydney Memory and Ageing Study (started 2005, community-dwelling adults aged 70 to 90 at entry), with Alzheimer cases diagnosed by NINCDS-ADRDA criteria and controls cognitively normal; samples from Waves 2 and 4, age range 75 to 97, 51.2% male. Cases were older than controls and had lower Mini-Mental State Examination scores; education, hypertension, diabetes and smoking did not differ. [[APOE]] ε4 carriage was 37.5% in cases versus 19% in controls (p = 0.063).
- Lipid measurement: untargeted [[Lipidomics]] of 10 µL plasma by reversed-phase liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry on an Orbitrap instrument, lipids assigned with LipidSearch 4.2 and normalised to internal standards. Nine classes were quantified: [[Ceramide]] (43 species), [[Sphingomyelin]] (90), [[Phosphatidylcholine]] (120), [[Lysophosphatidylcholine]] (24), [[Phosphatidylethanolamine]] (38), [[Phosphatidylinositol]] (17), [[Cholesteryl ester]] (14), [[Diacylglycerol]] (50) and [[Triglycerides]] (382).
- Group comparisons: inverse-normal-transformed residuals after regressing out age, sex, BMI, diabetes, hypertension, antihypertensive and lipid-lowering medication, APOE ε4, education and smoking; t tests with false discovery rate correction at 0.05; fold change as the ratio of mean abundance in cases and controls.
- Cell model: [[U251]] human astroglioma cells treated for 24 h with 5 µM recombinant oligomeric Aβ42 (oligomers matured 6 weeks at 4 °C) against untreated cells, six wells each, same lipid platform plus lyso-phosphatidylethanolamine.
- Classification: elastic-net logistic regression (glmnet, caret) on a random 70/30 train/test split with repeated cross-validation, repeated over 10 splits, using individual species, the nine class sums, or one class at a time; sensitivity, specificity and AUC on the test data.
- Genetics: 925 participants genotyped on an array and imputed to 1000 Genomes; a polygenic risk score built with PRSice at a GWAS p threshold of 5e-5 ([[Polygenic risk score]]) and 33 Alzheimer-associated SNPs (minor allele frequency above 0.05) mapped to 19 genes. For each lipid, a regression with a case-control by SNP (or by score) interaction term tested whether the association differs between cases and controls.

## Findings

- Individual lipids: 420 of the 778 species differed between cases and controls after FDR correction. Almost all ceramides differed, but in both directions by structure: dihydroxy ceramides with 18-carbon acyl chains and 0 or 2 double bonds, and all with 19 carbons, were higher in cases; those with 16 carbons or an 18-carbon chain with one double bond, and most monohydroxy and trihydroxy ceramides, were lower. Most sphingomyelins and most phosphatidylethanolamines (about 24% of species lower, roughly twice the number that were higher) were lower in cases; most phosphatidylinositols and diacylglycerols were higher; phosphatidylcholines were mostly higher for short-chain species; about 30% of triglycerides were higher and about 18% lower, the lower ones largely long-chain polyunsaturated. No lysophosphatidylcholine differed.
- Lipid classes: summed over the class, only diacylglycerol was higher in cases (p = 0.009); all other class sums were unchanged after adjustment.
- Cell lipidome: Aβ42 oligomers raised sphingomyelin (p = 0.0015), phosphatidylinositol (p = 0.041) and triglyceride (p = 0.005) totals and the lyso-phosphatidylethanolamine to phosphatidylethanolamine ratio; other classes did not differ. The authors take this as a contributory, possibly causal, role for oligomeric Aβ42 in the peripheral lipid changes.
- Classification: models built on cholesteryl esters, sphingomyelins or triglycerides reached more than 80% AUC on held-out data; class sums and the lysophosphatidylcholine and phosphatidylethanolamine models were less accurate.
- Polygenic score: no lipid associated with the score after FDR correction; the score by diagnosis interaction was nominally significant (p ≤ 0.05) for 57 lipids, mainly phosphatidylcholines, sphingomyelins and triglycerides. All 33 SNPs showed nominal differential associations with some lipids, none surviving correction; 43 lipids had differential associations with both the score and gene SNPs.
- Gene by class pattern: [[FERMT2]] and [[MS4A6A]] had differential associations with lipids in every class. [[ABCA7]] was linked to 52.6% of diacylglycerols and 57.1% of phosphatidylinositols; [[CLU]] to 43.4% of sphingomyelins and, with [[CR1]], more than 30% of phosphatidylethanolamines; [[PICALM]], [[SLC24A4]] and [[SORL1]] each to more than 30% of cholesteryl esters; [[BIN1]] to more than 30% of triglycerides.
- Mechanistic reading offered: FERMT2 may alter amyloid precursor protein handling through lipid-raft composition; ABCA7 through diacylglycerol metabolism and cholesterol efflux; lipid changes are tied to cholesterol and sphingolipid metabolism, lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase activity and phosphatidylinositol-mediated calcium signalling. These are interpretations drawn from prior literature, not tested here.

## Why This Source Matters

It is a compact worked example of using untargeted lipidomics both as a diagnostic signal and as a read-out for what Alzheimer risk genes might do. The class-level pattern (sphingomyelin, cholesteryl ester and triglyceride separating cases at over 80% AUC; diacylglycerol the only class sum shifted) gives candidate lipid arms of the three GWAS pathways of lipid metabolism, immune function and endosomal trafficking, and the gene-by-class table gives specific lipid classes to test for [[ABCA7]], [[CLU]] and [[FERMT2]]. The astroglioma experiment is a small step toward asking whether the periphery reflects amyloid exposure rather than only peripheral disease.

## Assessment

- Sample size is small (about 40 per group), the authors concede it is adequate for nine class-level tests but weak for 778 species, and no replication cohort exists. The classification AUCs come from random splits of the same 82 samples with no external validation, so they are likely optimistic.
- Genetic associations do not survive multiple-testing correction at any level; gene-level statements ("FERMT2 associated with lipids in all classes") rest on nominal interaction p values across many tests and 33 SNPs. Because the phenotype is the interaction term, they test whether SNP effects differ by diagnosis, not whether the SNP causes the lipid change.
- The plasma and cell results are not a clean match. Most sphingomyelin species were lower in cases while the sphingomyelin total rose in Aβ-treated cells, and the class sums for sphingomyelin, phosphatidylinositol and triglyceride were not different in plasma. U251 is a glioblastoma-derived line, here a stand-in for astrocytes, with 5 µM oligomers for 24 h (the discussion calls this both pathophysiological and subpathological) and six wells per arm.
- Internal inconsistencies: the abstract lists phosphatidylethanolamines as "PIs"; the discussion attributes 57.14% of phosphatidylinositols to MS4A6A while the results give 57.14% to ABCA7; the LPE/PE p value is missing from the text; the abstract and discussion say ceramide class sums did not differ although "almost all" ceramides did. Plasma oligomeric Aβ is described as higher in cases and "associated with increased MMSE", which reads as an error for decreased.
- Cases were older, APOE ε4 was balanced only by regression, only 10 of 40 cases had MRI so vascular co-pathology is unknown, fasting status and lipid-lowering drug details are not given, and peripheral lipids reflect liver and other organs as well as brain.

## Open Questions

- Do the sphingomyelin, cholesteryl ester and triglyceride classifiers keep an AUC above 80% in an independent cohort (the study used random splits of one cohort of 82 samples)?
- Which individual sphingomyelin species rise in Aβ-treated astrocytes, and do they match the species that are lower in Alzheimer plasma (the cell result was reported only for the class total)?
- Do the nominal gene-by-lipid interactions for ABCA7 with diacylglycerol and for FERMT2 with phosphatidylinositol replicate in a larger cohort (none survived multiple-testing correction here)?
- Do cerebrospinal fluid or post-mortem brain lipidomes show the same ceramide chain-length pattern as plasma (the authors name this as the needed validation, but only plasma and one cell line were measured)?
- Is the raised diacylglycerol class sum in plasma explained by ABCA7 genotype or by cholesterol esterification (not tested directly)?
