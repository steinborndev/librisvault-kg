# Fifty-gene neurodegeneration panel in eight Thai early-onset Alzheimer patients

Eight Thai patients with clinically diagnosed [[Early-onset Alzheimer disease]] (four women, four men; onset 41 to 60 years, mean 55) were sequenced across a panel of 50 genes linked to Alzheimer disease, [[Frontotemporal dementia]], [[Parkinson disease]], amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, Huntington disease and prion disease. Only two patients carried a classical early-onset mutation, but most carried missense variants in genes of other neurodegenerative diseases, which the authors read as evidence that these disorders sit on one genetic spectrum.

## Methods

- Patients came from a neurodegenerative disease clinic in Thailand and met NINCDS-ADRDA and CERAD criteria; all were typed for APOE by direct PCR.
- Genes were chosen from the Alzheimer and frontotemporal dementia and Parkinson disease mutation databases (accessed January 2019) plus literature for other disorders. See [[Targeted gene panel sequencing]]: Ion Torrent PGM, Sanger confirmation, novelty checked in ExAC, 1000 Genomes and the Korea Centers for Disease Control database (622 whole-genome-sequenced controls).
- Effects were predicted with PolyPhen-2, SIFT and PROVEAN ([[In silico missense variant effect prediction]]), prion protein structures were modelled with RaptorX, and the genes were mapped with [[ClueGO functional network analysis]].

## Findings

- 206 variants and 18 indels were found; after filtering, 63 missense variants in 23 of the 50 genes. The abstract instead says 61 missense variants in 21 genes. Variant burden tended to be higher in patients with a strong family history.
- Known Alzheimer mutation: [[PSEN1 p.E184G]] in a 41-year-old woman (MMSE 29/30, MoCA 25/30) with a four-generation family history, absent from the Asian-relevant databases checked. Possible novel: [[APP p.V604M]] in a patient with progressive non-fluent aphasia from 55 and a CSF amyloid beta 42 of 555 pg/mL; it lies outside the amyloid region, so the authors suggest a mechanism other than faster fibril formation. The few APP, PSEN1 and PSEN2 hits left about 75% of the cohort unexplained by these genes.
- Frontotemporal dementia gene: [[MAPT p.P513A]] in a man (#2) with onset at 51 and logopenic aphasia (see [[Progressive non-fluent aphasia]]), who also carried [[TREM2 p.L211P]].
- Parkinson disease genes: 12 variants in 8 carriers, all heterozygous: [[PINK1 p.Y253D]], [[PINK1 p.A340P]] and [[PINK1 p.N521T]] (absent from the databases), two [[PRKN]] changes (p.S167N, p.V380L) and seven [[LRRK2]] changes including [[LRRK2 p.R1628P]], [[LRRK2 p.N551K]] and [[LRRK2 p.R1398Q]].
- Other dementia genes: 30 non-synonymous variants in Alzheimer risk genes (CR1, TREM2, CTNNA3, DNMBP, SORL1, BACE1, LRP6, ABCA7, CD33); [[PRNP p.M129V]] and [[PRNP p.E219K]], known as [[Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease]] risk variants, appeared in patients (the patient with PSEN1 p.E184G was homozygous for M129V); and [[CSF1R p.P54Q]] and [[CSF1R p.L536V]], novel and outside the kinase domain, so not read as [[Hereditary diffuse leukoencephalopathy with spheroids]].
- ClueGO grouped the genes into amyloid metabolism, tyrosine kinase regulation, receptor recycling and synaptic transmission or oxidative stress.

## Assessment

- Eight patients, no segregation analysis (relatives declined testing) and no functional work: pathogenicity calls rest on database absence and predictors, and the authors admit the study is underpowered.
- "Compelling evidence" that Alzheimer and other neurodegenerative diseases are one spectrum goes beyond the data; heterozygous variants of uncertain significance in many genes are expected in any exome-sized cohort, and the controls were Korean rather than Thai.
- Inconsistencies: 61 variants in 21 genes (abstract) against 63 in 23 (results); patient #2 onset is given as 51 and as 55; the abstract lists CSF1R p.P54Q among probably or possibly pathogenic variants although the text calls it benign and tolerated; the discussion writes the TREM2 change as P211L. The text also states the patients were neuropathologically confirmed while the methods describe clinical diagnosis. E219K is described as a risk factor for sporadic Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease, whereas it is usually reported as protective; the source claim is reported here as written.
- Copy number variants (for example APP) and the C9orf72 repeat expansion were not screened.

## Open Questions

- Do the Thai variants [[APP p.V604M]] and [[CSF1R p.L536V]] alter amyloid beta production or microglial signalling in cell models (no in vitro or in vivo work was done)?
- Do relatives of the Thai patients who carry [[MAPT p.P513A]] or [[PSEN1 p.E184G]] share the variant and the disease (all living relatives refused testing)?
- How often are the panel variants found in Thai or other Southeast Asian people without dementia (the controls were 622 Korean individuals)?
- Does PRNP codon 129 genotype modify onset in carriers of [[PSEN1 p.E184G]] (a single homozygous patient was seen)?

## Why This Source Matters

It is a Southeast Asian early-onset cohort screened beyond the three classical genes, and it shows how panel sequencing turns up variants of uncertain significance in Parkinson, frontotemporal and prion genes. It supplies concrete candidates ([[APP p.V604M]], [[CSF1R p.L536V]], [[MAPT p.P513A]]) for functional follow-up and a warning about over-reading small cohorts.
