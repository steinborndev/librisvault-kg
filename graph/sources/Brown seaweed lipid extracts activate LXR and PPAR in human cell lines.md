# Brown seaweed lipid extracts activate LXR and PPAR in human cell lines

An in vitro screen of lipid extracts from six European brown seaweeds against the Asian seaweed [[Sargassum fusiforme]], which had earlier prevented disease progression in an Alzheimer mouse model. The question was which European species can activate liver X receptors ([[NR1H3]], [[NR1H2]]) and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors ([[PPARA]], [[PPARG]]) as well, and so modulate lipid metabolism, without the arsenic burden and import limits of the Asian species. See [[Brown seaweed lipid extracts]] for the material.

## Design

- Species: Himanthalia elongata and Alaria esculenta (Ireland); Sargassum muticum, Ascophyllum nodosum, Fucus vesiculosus and Saccharina latissima (Netherlands); Sargassum fusiforme (Japan) as reference. Species were chosen for European origin and [[Saringosterol]] content.
- Lipid extraction with chloroform-methanol under UVC light. [[Saringosterol]] and [[Fucosterol]] were measured by GC-MS, phospholipids, glycolipids, glycerides and fatty acids by UPLC-MS/MS, and arsenic and cadmium by ICP-OES.
- Receptor activation with a [[Dual luciferase nuclear receptor reporter assay]] using murine LXRα, LXRβ, PPARα and PPARγ cDNAs with [[RXRA]], in [[HEK293]], [[CCF-STTG1]], [[SH-SY5Y]] and [[CHME3]] cells. Doses were scaled to saringosterol content; controls were [[T0901317]] (LXR), [[Pirinixic acid]] (PPARα) and [[Pioglitazone]] (PPARγ). Three experiments in triplicate, Kruskal-Wallis test.
- Target gene expression by qPCR in CCF-STTG1 astrocytoma cells (three experiments), normalised to five reference genes, also with the RXR agonist [[Bexarotene]].

## Findings

- **Composition:** crude F. vesiculosus, S. muticum and H. elongata had saringosterol comparable to S. fusiforme, and the H. elongata extract matched S. fusiforme extract. Dominant fatty acids included myristic, palmitic, palmitoleic, oleic and linoleic acids, arachidonic acid and EPA.
- **Arsenic:** total arsenic was highest in S. fusiforme (67.26 mg/kg dry weight), then S. muticum (28.95), S. latissima (24.62), A. esculenta (16.79), H. elongata (13.58), F. vesiculosus (10.37) and A. nodosum (7.64). Cadmium was found only in S. fusiforme (1.03 mg/kg). See [[Arsenic]].
- **LXR:** all extracts activated both receptors, cell-type dependently. [[Himanthalia elongata]] was strongest, comparable to S. fusiforme, followed by S. muticum and S. latissima; F. vesiculosus, A. esculenta and A. nodosum activated LXR only in HEK293 and CHME3 cells.
- **PPAR:** PPARα was activated most by A. esculenta, F. vesiculosus and S. latissima; PPARγ most by A. esculenta, A. nodosum, F. vesiculosus and S. muticum, overall less than PPARα. The relative increase was greatest in SH-SY5Y cells. A. esculenta had the highest share of polyunsaturated fatty acids.
- **Gene expression in CCF-STTG1:** every extract raised the cholesterol efflux genes [[ABCG1]], [[ABCA1]] and [[APOE]] (see [[Cholesterol efflux]]), and lowered the cholesterol synthesis genes [[DHCR7]], [[DHCR24]], [[HMGCR]] and [[SREBF2]] and the fatty acid synthesis genes [[SREBF1]], [[ACACA]], [[SCD]] and [[FASN]]. [[T0901317]] and bexarotene lowered DHCR24 and HMGCR but raised the fatty acid synthesis genes, which the extracts did not.
- **Astrogliosis marker:** the extracts lowered [[GFAP]] expression, while T0901317, WY-14643 and pioglitazone raised it, which the authors read as a possible reduction of [[Astrocyte activation]], not tested further.
- The authors propose Himanthalia elongata as a European alternative to S. fusiforme for preventing Alzheimer pathology and cannot rule out a contribution of RXR homodimers, as bexarotene acted similarly.

## Why This Source Matters

It maps which edible European seaweeds carry sterol and fatty acid ligands that switch on both LXR and PPAR in brain-derived cells, and shows that these extracts push astrocyte-like cells towards cholesterol export through [[APOE]] and away from lipid synthesis, the opposite of the lipogenic drift of synthetic LXR agonists. It gives a shortlist for animal studies in [[Alzheimer disease]], and for cardiometabolic and inflammatory conditions.

## Assessment

- Cell-culture work only: whole extracts were applied to cells, though only some sterols and fatty acids cross the blood-brain barrier, and no animal or human data are given.
- Doses were scaled to saringosterol, so dilutions are comparable but not identical and the authors state that relative efficacy of species cannot be concluded.
- Receptor cDNAs were murine in human cells, expression data come from one cell line with n = 3, and the plotted statistics are described only as Kruskal-Wallis comparisons with the solvent control.
- Harvest season was not controlled and only total arsenic was measured, not the toxic inorganic form. Anti-inflammatory effects were not examined. The data are available on request only.
- The identity of PPAR-activating ligands is inferred from literature on fatty acids found in the extracts, not tested individually.

## Open Questions

- Does a Himanthalia elongata lipid extract prevent cognitive decline and amyloid deposition in an Alzheimer mouse model (only cell lines were tested)?
- What is the inorganic arsenic content of Himanthalia elongata and the other European brown seaweeds (only total arsenic was measured)?
- Which individual fatty acids or other lipids in the Alaria esculenta extract drive PPARα and PPARγ activation (ligands were inferred from the literature, not tested)?
- Do the seaweed extracts lower inflammatory signalling in astrocytes and microglia through LXR and PPAR transrepression (anti-inflammatory effects were not investigated)?
- Why do some extracts activate liver X receptors only in HEK293 and CHME3 cells and not in CCF-STTG1 or SH-SY5Y cells (cofactor differences are suggested but not tested)?
