# DAPG lowers beta-amyloid secretion in Alzheimer disease models

A preclinical study of whether [[2,4-Diacetylphloroglucinol]] (DAPG), a small acetylated phloroglucinol made by Pseudomonas bacteria, lowers amyloid beta (Aβ) in cell and mouse models of [[Alzheimer disease]], and by what mechanism.

## Design

- Screen: commercially available low-molecular-weight phloroglucinol derivatives (including phloroglucinol, phloroacetophenone, triacetylphloroglucinol, scyllo-inositol, phloretin, flopropione and maclurin) were applied for 8 hours to [[HEK293]] cells stably expressing Swedish-mutant [[APP]] (293sw). The highest non-toxic dose of each (MTT assay) was used, and secreted Aβ1-40 and Aβ1-42 were read by ELISA.
- Models: 293sw cells; primary neurons and astrocytes from APPsw/PS1dE9 double-transgenic mouse embryos or pups; and 8-month-old APPsw/PS1dE9 mice (n = 7) given 20 nmol DAPG into one hippocampus and vehicle into the other, analysed two days later. A separate cohort received 20 mg/kg intraperitoneally for 9 days.
- Mechanism probes: immunoblots of APP fragments (sAPPα, sAPPs, C83, C99, C89, AICD), organelle fractionation, exogenous Aβ clearance and degradation assays, an α-secretase activity assay, the [[ADAM10]] inhibitor [[GI254023X]], the [[ADAM17]] inhibitor TAPI, and trafficking inhibitors ([[Brefeldin A]], [[Golgicide A]], [[ZCL-278]], CI-976, vacuolin-1, EHNA, Retro-2).
- Statistics: Student's t-test, paired t-test for the two hippocampi, and one-way ANOVA with Tukey's test for multiple comparisons.

## Findings

- Only DAPG significantly lowered both secreted Aβ1-40 and Aβ1-42 in 293sw cells; triacetylphloroglucinol lowered Aβ1-40 only (at 50 μM or more), and phloroglucinol and the other derivatives did nothing. DAPG did not raise LDH release; 100 μM lowered MTT reduction, read by the authors as growth arrest rather than cell death.
- DAPG did not change degradation or clearance of added Aβ, so the authors conclude it lowers production or secretion. It raised intracellular and secreted sAPPα (the α-secretase product), raised the sAPPα to total sAPP ratio, and lowered total secreted sAPP in 293sw cells. The extra sAPPα accumulated in the endoplasmic reticulum, lysosome and Golgi fractions but not in endosomes, and not at the plasma membrane. Total α-secretase activity was unchanged.
- In primary astrocytes DAPG (25-100 μM) lowered secreted Aβ and raised sAPPα and C83, with sAPPs unchanged. In primary neurons 100 μM lowered Aβ but 50-100 μM raised LDH release 2-4 fold, so toxicity limited that model.
- Intrahippocampal DAPG lowered Triton-soluble Aβ1-40 significantly and Aβ1-42 only at a trend (p = 0.064), left the insoluble (GuHCl) fraction unchanged, and raised sAPPα and C83. Intraperitoneal DAPG did not change spatial memory in the Morris water maze or APP processing. A computational model predicted that DAPG does not cross the blood-brain barrier.
- ADAM10 inhibition by GI254023X blocked the DAPG-driven sAPPα rise in both cell types, but ADAM17 inhibition with TAPI did not, and neither β- nor γ-secretase inhibitors altered it. Neither inhibitor prevented the DAPG-driven fall in secreted Aβ. With GI254023X plus DAPG, intracellular Aβ was higher than with DAPG alone, so ADAM10 explains part of the Aβ effect inside the cell but not the secretion effect.
- Brefeldin A alone prevented the DAPG-driven fall in secreted Aβ in 293sw cells, while in astrocytes both Brefeldin A and Golgicide A did, and their effects on sAPPα resembled ADAM10 inhibition. ZCL-278 mimicked ADAM10 inhibition on sAPPα in 293sw cells yet left the Aβ reduction intact. The authors conclude that sAPPα and Aβ are handled by partly independent trafficking routes that differ by cell type.

## Why This Source Matters

It describes a route to lower Aβ that is neither β- nor γ-secretase inhibition: a small commercial compound that shifts [[APP]] toward α-secretase cleavage through [[ADAM10]] and also alters how Aβ and sAPPα are secreted through the Golgi. It also separates the two effects experimentally, which is useful to anyone modelling [[Amyloid-beta formation]]. The compound is a lead for medicinal chemistry rather than a drug, mainly because it does not reach the brain when given systemically.

## Assessment

- Cell experiments use n = 3 per condition and mostly Aβ1-40; Aβ1-42 effects are weaker or non-significant in several panels.
- The in vivo benefit rests on an injection into one hippocampus per animal (n = 7), two days after dosing, with the contralateral hippocampus as control and no behavioural readout; systemic dosing failed.
- The exact molecular target of DAPG is not identified; DAPG did not change ADAM10 level, maturation or total α-secretase activity, so ADAM10 involvement is inferred from an inhibitor and proposed to act through changes in localisation or binding partners.
- Blood-brain barrier impermeability is a computer prediction, not a measured result. Inhibitor concentrations and specificity (Brefeldin A hits several ARF exchange factors) limit how precisely the trafficking step can be assigned.
- The inhibitor names appear with inconsistent codes in the text (GI254023X, GI294023X, GI293023X, GI243023X); the compound is read as the ADAM10 inhibitor GI254023X.
- Possible inhibition of Aβ aggregation by polyhydroxyl phenols is discussed from earlier reports and was not tested here.

## Open Questions

- What is the direct molecular target of 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol that links it to ADAM10 localisation and Golgi trafficking (not identified; ADAM10 level, maturation and total activity were unchanged)?
- Can a chemical analogue of 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol cross the blood-brain barrier and lower Aβ in the brain after systemic dosing (only a computer prediction of impermeability and one failed intraperitoneal experiment were reported)?
- Does 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol also inhibit Aβ oligomerisation and fibril formation, as reported for other polyhydroxyl phenolic compounds (suggested in the discussion but not tested)?
- Which ARF guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GBF1, BIG1 or BIG2) mediates the Brefeldin A effect on Aβ secretion in 293sw cells but not the Golgicide A effect (inferred from inhibitor selectivity only)?
