# Alzheimer disease genes in small vessel ischemic disease and mouse hypoperfusion

A genetic and expression study asking whether the known Alzheimer disease risk factors also act in [[Small vessel ischemic disease]] (SVID), the vessel disease that so often accompanies [[Alzheimer disease]] as mixed dementia. The authors exome-sequenced 96 early-onset SVID patients and compared coding variants in 31 amyloid precursor protein (APP) and amyloid beta metabolism genes and nine late-onset Alzheimer disease GWAS loci against 368 elderly neuropathologically confirmed controls from the Healthy Exomes database. They then measured the same genes' mouse transcripts by RNA sequencing in the [[BCCAS mouse model]] of cerebral hypoperfusion and stained its hippocampus for amyloid beta oligomers.

## Design

- Patients: 96 non-Hispanic Caucasian early-onset (mean 51.5 years; two familial cases aged 68 and 71) SVID cases from the NINDS repository, 82.3% male, 44.8% with a family history, screened negative for HTRA1, NOTCH3, ACTA2 and COL4A1 mutations. Hypertension was present in 60.4% and type 2 diabetes in 30.2%.
- [[APOE]] genotyping by TaqMan (rs429358 and rs7412) and whole exome sequencing on Illumina HiSeq, aligned to GRCh37 and called with GATK; single-variant Fisher's exact tests with false discovery rate correction, powered at 80% only for common variants with odds ratio below 0.6 or above 2.
- Mouse: 8 BCCAS, 8 sham and 4 naive C57BL/6J males; prefrontal cortex, striatum and hippocampus sequenced at 2 and 7 days; the contralateral hemisphere stained for amyloid beta oligomers and glial markers.

## Findings

- **APOE.** Only 29% of patients carried ε4 (ε3/ε4 27%, ε4/ε4 1%), close to published Caucasian controls (21.3% and 1.8%) and well below late-onset Alzheimer disease patients (41.1% and 14.8%). ε2 was present in 13 of 96.
- **Mendelian Alzheimer genes.** Five rare coding variants in [[APP]], [[PSEN1]] and [[PSEN2]] were all outside the pathogenic regions or already known to be benign, so these genes are not common causes of SVID.
- **Degradation over production.** Loss-of-function variants were commoner in amyloid degradation genes: 9 of 96 patients (9.4%) versus 1 of 96 in production genes (Fisher p = 0.018) and 6 of 368 (1.6%) controls in the same genes (p = 3.5e-14). 75 of 96 patients carried at least one rare likely damaging variant across the 31 genes.
- **Individual variants.** Homozygosity for [[CST3 p.A25T]] was found in 4 of 96 patients versus 6 of 368 controls (4.2% vs 1.6%), with a mean onset of 49.75 years. Two rare [[SORL1]] variants in the VPS10 domain, [[SORL1 p.E270K]] and [[SORL1 p.A528T]], each occurred in one patient.
- **GWAS loci.** Of 69 coding variants in the GWAS loci, 36 were also seen in controls and tested. Three common variants had lower frequency in cases (adjusted p below 0.05; odds ratios roughly 0.3 to 0.5): [[EPHA1 p.V160A]] (odds ratio 0.488, adjusted p = 0.046), [[EPHA1 p.M900V]] and [[CD33 p.A14V]]. [[ABCA7 p.G1527A]] and [[CR1 p.Q1022H]] were only nominally associated.
- **Mouse expression.** Of 17 amyloid degradation genes, five rose up to 8-fold (A2m, Plat, Ctsd, Ctsb, Klk6) and none of the 14 production genes did. Apoe, Cd33 and eight Ms4a transcripts rose up to 7-fold at day 7 in hippocampus and striatum, alongside the microglial markers Aif1 and Cd86, seven matrix metalloprotease genes and 51 lysosomal genes. Expression scaled with hippocampal lesion volume, which the authors take as a consequence of microglial infiltration rather than a cause.
- **Amyloid beta oligomers.** Oligomers appeared in CA1 pyramidal neurons and reactive astrocytes 7 days after surgery, fewer at 2 days (p = 0.032) and almost none in naive mice (p = 0.0006), including regions with gliosis but no lesion on T2-weighted MRI.

## Why This Source Matters

It tests, in a vessel-disease cohort rather than an Alzheimer cohort, which parts of Alzheimer genetics generalise. The picture is selective: the [[APOE]] ε4 allele and the Mendelian amyloid genes do not drive SVID, whereas variants in amyloid clearance and microglial-immune loci ([[CD33]], [[EPHA1]], the [[MS4A6A]] cluster) and the up-regulation of clearance genes under hypoperfusion point to a shared clearance pathway. The oligomer finding gives a route by which ischemia could seed amyloid pathology, supporting the idea that the vascular and amyloid-cascade hypotheses complement each other.

## Assessment

- The cohort is small (96 cases) and the study is powered only for large effects, so the three protective variants need replication; the authors themselves call for a larger cohort.
- Controls come from a public exome database sequenced separately from the patients, which risks batch and ancestry differences in variant calling, and the ε4 comparison uses historical published frequencies instead of matched controls. The ε3/ε4 difference from late-onset Alzheimer patients is described as significant but has p = 0.052.
- The loss-of-function burden test compares 17 degradation genes with 14 production genes of very different length and constraint, and the rare-variant counts are small (9 versus 1).
- Odds-ratio ranges are quoted differently in the abstract, results and discussion.
- The mouse expression result is correlative; the model uses young male C57BL/6J mice, 8 per group, and the authors read the rise as consequential to microglial infiltration. Oligomer counts were made in the less-lesioned hemisphere with a single antibody, and no human tissue was examined.

## Open Questions

- Do the protective effects of EPHA1 p.V160A, EPHA1 p.M900V and CD33 p.A14V on small vessel ischemic disease replicate in a larger cohort with matched, jointly sequenced controls (this study had 96 cases and public-database controls)?
- Is the burden of loss-of-function variants in amyloid degradation genes also seen in late-onset small vessel ischemic disease, not only early-onset cases (all 96 patients were early-onset)?
- Do amyloid beta oligomers appear after hypoperfusion in human small vessel disease brain, and are they followed by plaques (only mouse tissue at 2 and 7 days was examined)?
- Does the CST3 p.A25T homozygous state raise small vessel ischemic disease risk or hasten dementia (4 patients, none yet shown to be demented)?
- Which cell type drives the up-regulation of Apoe, Cd33 and Ms4a transcripts after hypoperfusion (bulk tissue sequencing cannot separate microglia from other cells)?
