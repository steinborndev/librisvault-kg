# ADAM17 p.R215I loss-of-function variant in a late-onset Alzheimer family

A Molecular Psychiatry study that used whole-genome sequencing in one German family with autosomal dominant [[Late-onset Alzheimer disease]] to find [[ADAM17 p.R215I]] (rs142946965), then looked for further carriers in about 5,450 case and control samples from Germany, the UK and the USA and tested the variant in [[SH-SY5Y]] cells. The same work found a strong inverse relationship between [[ADAM17]] and [[APP]] mRNA in human brain.

## Why This Source Matters

[[ADAM10]] was the one alpha-secretase gene with rare variants already tied to late-onset familial disease. This paper adds a second, [[ADAM17]], and links the variant to a mechanism: failed pro-domain cleavage, no mature enzyme, and higher secreted [[Amyloid-beta]] in cells. It also reports a new, non-proteolytic effect, in which wild-type ADAM17 lowers APP transcription, which the variant loses.

## Family and sequencing

- Pedigree with four affected members (reported onset 60 and 69 in two; clinic examination at 87 and 70 in the other two; APOE ε3/4, ε3/4, ε3/3, ε3/3) and six younger or unaffected relatives. The index patient (onset 60) had MMSE 28/30 at diagnosis, temporoparietal and posterior cingulate hypometabolism on FDG-PET, MRI atrophy one year later, and died 10 years after onset. Affected members were negative for [[APP]], [[PSEN1]] and [[PSEN2]] mutations. Two unaffected relatives had depression and one had [[Parkinson disease]].
- [[Whole-genome sequencing]] (Complete Genomics) of two affected and four unaffected members gave 7,009,082 variants; filtering by shared haplotype, dominant inheritance, rarity (allele frequency 1% or less), exonic effect, brain expression, pathogenicity and conservation left 11 candidates, and only [[ADAM17]] and ADRA2A were in known candidate genes.
- ADAM17 p.R215I (NM_003183 c.G644T) was predicted damaging by 7 of 8 programs and conserved by 3 of 4 scores, and lay in the region of the maximal LOD score. Sanger sequencing placed it in all four affected members and in none of the unaffected ones, giving a maximal LOD of 1.81 (1.20 from the sequenced six alone). See [[Pedigree linkage and identity-by-descent analysis]].

## Further carriers and frequency

- Genotyping of a German cohort (969 cases, 76 controls) and exome data from a UK cohort (331 cases, 240 controls) and the WHICAP cohort in the USA (1,567 cases, 2,267 controls) found five heterozygous carriers, all cases: one German (onset 79, APOE ε2/3), one British (death at 92, onset about 84, ε3/4) and three Hispanic Americans (onset 85, 85 and 90; one ε4 carrier). No carrier among controls, nor among 468 older unaffected brain-bank exomes.
- The variant is ultra-rare: ESP6500 European 0.000077, 1000 Genomes European 0.0002, ExAC non-Finnish European 0.0001, gnomAD 9 of 138,390 (0.0000325), only non-Finnish Europeans and only heterozygous.

## Functional findings

- The substitution of arginine by isoleucine at residue 215 sits next to the pro-protein convertase (furin) site at residues 210 to 214. In SH-SY5Y cells overexpressing the variant, pro-ADAM17 rose like wild type, but the mature form rose only with wild type, as with the catalytically dead p.E406A control. The authors read this as impaired maturation and loss of alpha-secretase function.
- In cells that overexpressed APP, wild-type ADAM17 lowered APP protein and mRNA (p = 0.001 for mRNA, n = 9) and secreted Aβ40 and Aβ42; the R215I and E406A forms did not, and Aβ40 and Aβ42 were higher with R215I than with wild type. Six days (not 16 hours) of 100 nM TAPI-0 restored APP mRNA and protein, so the effect depends on ADAM17 activity. Wild-type ADAM17 also lowered APP mRNA in cells with only endogenous APP.
- In prefrontal cortex from 201 Alzheimer cases and 83 controls (Netherlands Brain Bank), ADAM17 and APP mRNA were strongly inversely correlated (r² = 0.55, p = 2.2×10⁻¹⁶), replicated in 57 temporal cortices from Munich (r² = 0.44). Of 71 ADAM17 substrates, only [[PRNP]] (r = -0.77) and APP (-0.74) correlated at 0.7 or more; [[TREM2]] and SORL1 did not. [[ADAM9]] correlated very weakly negatively and ADAM10 weakly positively with APP. Across public expression data the ADAM17-APP correlation was negative in brain (r = -0.27) and absent in non-brain tissue.

## Assessment

- The segregation evidence is thin: four affected carriers and a maximal LOD of 1.81, short of the usual 3, and the authors concede other co-segregating variants cannot be excluded.
- The replication is a handful of carriers (five of 5,450 samples), with a 76-person German control set; the variant is too rare for a formal association test. The pooled carriers have onsets of 79 to 90 years, later than the index family (60 and 69).
- Function rests on overexpression in a neuroblastoma line with the Swedish APP mutation; the maturation defect is inferred from Western blots, the 16-hour and 6-day TAPI-0 results differ, and the mechanism by which ADAM17 lowers APP transcription is not identified. Alpha-secretase cleavage was not measured as sAPPα.
- The brain correlation is cross-sectional mRNA from bulk microarray tissue in both cases and controls, so it does not show the direction of regulation.
- Loss of other ADAM17 substrates (the paper cites more than 80, including TREM2) could contribute and was not tested.

## Open Questions

- Do other families or large exome data sets show an excess of rare loss-of-function or maturation-defective ADAM17 variants in late-onset Alzheimer cases (only one family and five single carriers are reported)?
- Does ADAM17 p.R215I lower soluble APPα and TREM2 shedding in neurons or knock-in mice (only overexpression in SH-SY5Y cells with secreted Aβ was measured)?
- By what pathway does wild-type ADAM17 reduce APP transcription (the paper names receptor shedding and AICD as possibilities but tests neither)?
