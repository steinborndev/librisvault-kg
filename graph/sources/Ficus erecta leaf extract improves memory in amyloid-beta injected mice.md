# Ficus erecta leaf extract improves memory in amyloid-beta injected mice

A preclinical study of an ethanol extract of Ficus erecta Thunb. leaves ([[Ficus erecta leaf ethanol extract]], EEFE) in male C57BL/6 mice given an intracerebroventricular injection of pre-aggregated Aβ1-42. The plant is a seashore species of Korea, Japan and China used as food and as folk medicine for nephritis and arthritis; this is reported as the first test of it in an [[Alzheimer disease]] setting.

## Design

- Extract: dried leaves (3.4 kg) and branches (4 kg) were extracted twice with aqueous ethanol at 80 °C for 3 h, giving yields of 18.71% (leaf) and 7.33% (branch).
- In vitro: thioflavin T fluorescence assay of Aβ1-42 aggregation at 6.25-100 μg/ml, with [[Morin]] as positive control.
- In vivo: Aβ1-42 aggregates (5 μL of 1 μg/μL, incubated 4 weeks at -20 °C) infused into the cerebral ventricle; see [[Intracerebroventricular amyloid-beta injection mouse model]]. Five groups of n = 7: saline control (NOR), Aβ, Aβ plus EEFE 50 mg/kg/day, Aβ plus EEFE 150 mg/kg/day, Aβ plus morin 10 mg/kg/day, all by mouth for 20 days.
- Behaviour: the [[Passive avoidance task]] on days 8-10 and the [[Morris water maze]] on days 15-21.
- Tissue: immunohistochemistry for Aβ, [[RBFOX3]] (NeuN) and [[AIF1]] (Iba-1) with Nissl staining in hippocampus and cortex; western blot for Aβ, phospho-[[CREB1]] and [[BDNF]]; ELISA for [[TNF]] and [[IL1B]].
- Chemistry: HPLC with photodiode-array detection against three standards.

## Findings

- Aβ aggregation in vitro: IC50 43.43 μg/ml for the leaf extract and above 100 μg/ml for the branch extract, so only leaves were taken forward.
- Aβ load in injected mice: Aβ-positive cells in hippocampus and cortex and Aβ protein on western blot were lower with 150 mg/kg EEFE than in the Aβ group; 50 mg/kg had no effect.
- Memory: transfer latency in the passive avoidance task was shorter in Aβ mice and higher with EEFE-150. In the water maze, escape latency was longer in Aβ mice and fell with EEFE-150, which also restored time in the target quadrant and platform crossings in the probe trial. A Y-maze entry count, reported from earlier work, was used to argue that locomotion was not altered by the injection.
- Neurons: Nissl-stained cell bodies and NeuN-positive cells in CA1 and cortex fell with Aβ and were restored by EEFE-150 and morin; see [[Neuronal loss]].
- Signalling: phospho-CREB and BDNF fell in Aβ brain lysates and were reverted by EEFE-150, which the authors read as activation of CREB/BDNF signalling.
- Inflammation: Iba-1-positive cells, IL-1β and TNF-α were raised by Aβ and brought down by EEFE-150; see [[Neuroinflammation]] and [[Microglial cell activation]].
- Chemistry: the extract contained [[Rutin]] 5.49 mg/g, [[Chlorogenic acid]] 1.80 mg/g and [[Kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside]] (nicotiflorin) 1.38 mg/g. The authors propose synergy among these, citing reports that each crosses the blood-brain barrier.
- No toxic side effects were noted at 150 mg/kg, and EEFE-150 acted about as well as morin.

## Why This Source Matters

It adds a food-plant extract to the list of natural products tested against Aβ-driven toxicity and ties the benefit to three recognisable flavonoid and phenolic constituents with known neuroprotective literature. The readouts (CREB/BDNF, microglial and cytokine markers, neuron counts) are the standard panel, which makes the extract directly comparable with other herbal and small-molecule candidates tested in Aβ-injected rodents.

## Assessment

- The abstract and summary say EEFE acts through "upregulation of the inflammatory cytokines", but the results show IL-1β and TNF-α lowered by EEFE; this is a wording error, and the data support suppression.
- Only the 150 mg/kg dose worked, and the dosing is inconsistent in the text (the timeline legend says 100 mg/kg, the methods say 150 mg/kg). There is no EEFE-alone group in uninjected mice, so effects on behaviour or signalling independent of Aβ are not excluded.
- Histology and blot groups were small (n = 3-4 per group, against n = 7 for behaviour), and the legends alternate between SD and SEM.
- The model injects exogenous peptide once, so a lower Aβ signal 20 days later may reflect clearance or reduced neurotoxicity of the injected peptide rather than prevention of aggregation in the brain. In vitro, the thioflavin T assay shows direct inhibition only at tens of μg/ml.
- Mechanism is inferred from marker levels; CREB/BDNF involvement was not tested by blocking the pathway. The three quantified compounds total under 9 mg/g, so most of the extract is uncharacterised, and none of the compounds was tested alone.
- The water maze is described inconsistently (a 7-day schedule with 2 training days in the methods, 5 training days in the results). Only male mice were used.

## Open Questions

- Which constituent of Ficus erecta leaf extract carries the anti-aggregation and neuroprotective effect (rutin, chlorogenic acid and nicotiflorin were quantified but never tested individually)?
- Does Ficus erecta leaf extract lower Aβ and improve memory in a transgenic Alzheimer model with chronic plaque formation rather than a single injection of aggregated Aβ1-42 (only the injection model was used)?
- Does blocking CREB phosphorylation or BDNF signalling remove the memory benefit of Ficus erecta leaf extract (pathway involvement was inferred from marker levels only)?
- Do the oral doses used reach brain concentrations that account for the effect (blood-brain barrier passage was argued from literature on the pure compounds, not measured in the extract-treated mice)?
