# Familial Alzheimer disease mutations in Han Chinese in Taiwan

A retrospective single-centre series from the neurology clinic of Taipei Veterans General Hospital. Between 2002 and 2018, 77 patients (33 men, 44 women; mean age at onset 51.1 ± 9.7 years) with a history suggesting autosomal dominant [[Familial Alzheimer disease]], or sporadic onset before 65, were tested for mutations in [[PSEN1]], [[PSEN2]] and [[APP]]. Seventeen (22%) carried a mutation: 16 in PSEN1 and 1 in APP, none in PSEN2. The single commonest change was [[PSEN1 p.M146I]], in 9 of the 16 PSEN1 carriers (56.2%).

## Methods

- 57 patients were screened by multiplex single-strand conformational polymorphism and Sanger sequencing (13 PSEN1 carriers and 1 APP carrier found); from 2018 another 20 were sequenced by next-generation sequencing on an Illumina MiSeq (3 further PSEN1 carriers). Mean coverage was above 800x; variants were filtered for frequency up to 0.01 in gnomAD, not benign in ClinVar, GERP++ above 2, and at least two of CADD above 20, SIFT and PolyPhen-2 calling it deleterious.
- Frequencies were checked in ExAC, gnomAD and Taiwan Biobank (1517 genomes), and annotations in the Human Gene Mutation Database.
- Family history was scored with a modified Goldman score (1 = at least three affected in two generations, 4 = none or unknown). Earliest MRIs (32 patients) were rated for medial temporal atrophy (0-4), posterior atrophy (0-3) and [[White matter hyperintensity]] (0-3).
- Group comparisons used Mann-Whitney tests after a Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality check (SPSS 18); significance at p below 0.05.

## Findings

- Carriers had an earlier onset than non-carriers (46.2 ± 6.2 against 52.7 ± 10.0 years, p = 0.008) and more often a positive family history (p = 0.001). Sex made no difference to onset.
- On MRI (11 carriers, 21 non-carriers), medial temporal and posterior atrophy did not differ (p = 0.981, 0.865); white matter hyperintensity was higher in non-carriers after age adjustment (B = 1.124, 95% CI 0.526-1.721, p < 0.001).
- The mutations: PSEN1 [[PSEN1 p.P117L]], [[PSEN1 p.M146I]], [[PSEN1 p.G206D]], [[PSEN1 p.G209E]], [[PSEN1 p.E280K]] and [[PSEN1 p.L286V]] and APP [[APP p.D678H]] were known pathogenic changes; [[PSEN1 p.H131R]] was called likely pathogenic and [[PSEN1 p.R157S]] a variant of unknown significance.
- The p.H131R patient (onset 49, affected sister, father died at 65) had extensive cortical and striatal amyloid deposition on Pittsburgh compound B PET and reduced uptake in precuneus and temporo-parietal cortex on [[FDG PET]]; the authors use this to upgrade the variant, first reported as uncertain in a Japanese woman in 2013.
- Six of the nine p.M146I families scored 1 on the Goldman scale. Compared with the seven other PSEN1 carriers, they did not differ in Goldman score (p = 0.148), onset age (p = 0.457), education (p = 0.521) or extra neurological features: [[Myoclonus]] 33.3%, [[Seizure]] 22.2%, extrapyramidal signs 11.1%, similar to published PSEN1 rates (47%, 24%, 14%). Two four-generation pedigrees had onset in the 40s.
- p.M146I had not been reported in Taiwan or elsewhere in Asia before; earlier families were Danish (1996), British and Swedish.
- APP p.D678H is the second report in Taiwan, with two more families in the literature, so the authors judge it the commonest APP mutation there.
- PSEN1, PSEN2 and APP explain only a small share of early-onset familial cases; the authors point to other neurodegeneration genes and to C7, a risk gene proposed in Han Chinese, for the rest. Genetic testing does not change management but informs life and reproductive planning.

## Assessment

- Small, single-centre and retrospective; the authors list few affected families as a limitation. Nine "unrelated" p.M146I index patients rest on self-reported pedigrees, and no haplotype analysis excluded a shared founder.
- The white matter result compares 11 carriers with 21 non-carriers by visual rating; the authors note that a DIAN study using volumetric measurement found more hyperintensity in carriers before onset, so the finding may depend on the rating method and on younger age in carriers.
- The abstract and methods count the cohort differently at first glance (77 patients, 57 plus 20 by two methods); the text is consistent once the two testing eras are added.
- The p.H131R upgrade rests on one amyloid PET scan plus earlier cell data, not on segregation; the p.R157S call is limited by missing family data.

## Open Questions

- Do the nine unrelated PSEN1 p.M146I index patients in Taiwan share a haplotype, which would indicate a founder origin (no haplotype analysis was done)?
- Do PSEN1 p.H131R carriers outside this one family show segregation with disease (only one pedigree with a few affected relatives was described)?
- Is white matter hyperintensity lower in autosomal dominant Alzheimer disease carriers than in non-carriers when measured by volumetric rather than visual rating?
- Which genes explain the 78% of Taiwanese familial and early-onset cases with no PSEN1, PSEN2 or APP mutation (C7 is only named as a candidate)?

## Why This Source Matters

It is the first extensive mutational survey of familial Alzheimer disease in Taiwan, showing that Han Chinese familial disease follows the Asian pattern of PSEN1 dominance, with an APP hotspot at residue 678 and no PSEN2 cases. Its PET-supported reclassification of PSEN1 p.H131R is a worked example of variant interpretation, and its finding that carriers are younger, more often have family history and show less white matter hyperintensity gives a practical rule for choosing patients for testing.
