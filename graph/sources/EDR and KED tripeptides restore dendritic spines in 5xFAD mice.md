# EDR and KED tripeptides restore dendritic spines in 5xFAD mice

A 2021 Pharmaceuticals paper testing two short peptides, [[EDR peptide]] (Glu-Asp-Arg, Pinealon) and [[KED peptide]] (Lys-Glu-Asp, Vesugen), in the [[5xFAD mouse]] model of [[Alzheimer disease]]. It combines in vivo readouts (hippocampal [[Long-term potentiation]] and CA1 [[Dendritic spine]] morphology) with an in silico proposal that the peptides bind promoter DNA of Alzheimer-related genes.

## Design

- Peptides (400 μg/kg, intraperitoneal, once daily for 2 months) or physiological saline in 5xFAD and wild-type mice; 10 mice per group, 5 males and 5 females.
- LTP: Schaffer collateral to CA1 field recordings in acute slices from 4-month-old mice (peptides given from 2 to 4 months).
- Spine morphology: 5xFAD crossed with Thy1-EGFP "M" mice to give 5xFAD-M, so that CA1 neurons express GFP; confocal imaging and NeuronStudio classification of spines as mushroom, thin or stubby in 5-month-old mice, 10 to 15 dendritic fragments per mouse, with males and females also analysed separately.
- Mechanism: [[Molecular docking]] (ICM-Pro) of each tripeptide onto all 2,080 unique hexanucleotide B-form double-stranded DNA sequences, then a search for the best-scoring sequences in the promoters of 11 Alzheimer-related genes taken from the Eukaryotic Promoter Database.

## Findings

- LTP: wild-type versus 5xFAD plasticity values differed only as a trend (p = 0.057), and both peptides gave a positive but non-significant trend toward restoring it; the abstract describes KED as tending to raise neuroplasticity.
- Spine density in all 5xFAD-M mice: EDR raised it by 11% (12.64 versus 11.31 spines per 10 μm, p = 0.039), back to the M-line level; KED did not change density (p = 0.103).
- Spine classes in all mice: KED restored the mushroom fraction to the M-line level (41.25% versus 39.15%, p = 0.003) and lowered the thin fraction by 13%; EDR lowered thin spines by 10% but left mushroom spines unchanged. Neither peptide changed stubby spines.
- Males (more severe pathology): EDR raised density by 13% and KED by 22%, both through a larger mushroom fraction (EDR 40.2% versus 30.2%; KED 41.1% versus 30.2%).
- Females: EDR raised density by 12% without changing mushroom or thin fractions; KED lowered the thin fraction by 14% only.
- Docking: the best EDR complex scored -44.7 and the best KED complex -26.6 (ICM score, lower is stronger binding); both sit in the minor groove, rotated 180 degrees relative to each other, with strand-independent base-pair patterns (weak AT and strong GC masks) that match completely.
- The top EDR mask (SWSWSS, score -38.6) occurs repeatedly in the promoters of [[CASP3]], [[NES]], [[GAP43]] (three times) and [[APOE]] (twice); the authors also list [[SOD2]], [[PPARA]], [[PPARG]] and [[GPX1]] (written GDX1 in the abstract and conclusions) among genes with matching sequences. Binding was judged of low selectivity, with higher affinity for GC-rich sequences.
- The authors propose that EDR acts on the neuronal component and KED on both neurons and cerebral vessel endothelium, and that the peptides modulate gene expression epigenetically.

## Why This Source Matters

It is an in vivo follow-up to earlier cultured-neuron work showing that these two peptides protect spines against amyloid synaptotoxicity, and it shows peptide-specific and sex-specific spine effects in a transgenic Alzheimer mouse. It also offers a testable, if speculative, route by which a tripeptide could regulate transcription: direct minor-groove binding to promoter sequences. Only the spine results reach significance in the main text; the LTP and promoter-binding parts are suggestive.

## Assessment

- LTP rests on a trend (genotype difference p = 0.057; peptide effects not significant), so the "restores neuroplasticity" wording in the abstract and conclusions outruns the data.
- Group size is small (10 mice per group, 5 per sex), and multiple comparisons across spine classes and sexes are not corrected for; the sex-specific differences are not tested as an interaction.
- The M-line control and 5xFAD-M mice are on mixed backgrounds (B6SJL and C57BL/6J); the LTP experiment used 5xFAD and wild type of the B6SJL background.
- Mechanism is computational only: no promoter binding, expression or chromatin experiment is reported, and the docking itself shows low selectivity and no correlation between the best sequences of the two peptides. The word "epigenetic" in the title is therefore not backed by data.
- Whether the peptides reach the brain was not measured; blood-brain barrier crossing is argued from other tripeptides.
- Gene symbol "GDX1" appears in the abstract and conclusions while the methods list GPX1.
- Peptide supplier is a Russian company (GARMONIA); the paper does not state a funding conflict in the text read.

## Open Questions

- Do EDR and KED peptides bind the promoters of CASP3, NES, GAP43 and APOE in cells or brain tissue and change their expression (only docking in silico was reported)?
- Do the peptides reach the mouse brain after intraperitoneal dosing (blood-brain barrier crossing was inferred from other tripeptides and not measured)?
- Does a larger, adequately powered LTP experiment confirm a KED or EDR effect in 5xFAD mice (the reported trends did not reach significance)?
- Is the sex difference in peptide response, with larger mushroom spine rescue in males, reproducible when sex is tested as an interaction term?
