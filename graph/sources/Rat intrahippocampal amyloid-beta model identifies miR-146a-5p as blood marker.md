# Rat intrahippocampal amyloid-beta model identifies miR-146a-5p as blood marker

An experimental study in Sprague-Dawley rats and cultured rat astrocytes that asks which circulating microRNAs (miRNAs) track the presence of aggregated amyloid beta in the brain, and what the one marker that behaves differently, miR-146a-5p, does in astrocytes. The model is a non-genetic one meant to mimic the early phase of sporadic [[Late-onset Alzheimer disease]]: fibrillar Aβ1-42 is injected into the CA1 region of both hippocampi. See [[Intrahippocampal amyloid-beta infusion rat model]].

## Design

- Animals: adult [[Sprague-Dawley rat]] animals (8 to 12 weeks) received 3 µL per hemisphere of fibrillar Aβ1-42 (pre-aggregated 5 days at 37 °C) or PBS, at 1 µg/µL (first cohort) or 2.5 µg/µL (second cohort). Serum was taken by cardiac puncture at 7, 14 and 21 days (n = 8 per group and time point); cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) from the cisterna magna at 14 days.
- Behaviour and pathology: Morris water maze at day 14 (n = 10 per group) and anti-GFAP immunofluorescence of hippocampal sections (n = 5 per group).
- miRNA panel: nine miRNAs chosen from the Alzheimer literature (miR-9a-5p, -29a-3p, -29c-3p, -125b-5p, -106b-5p, -135a-5p, -146a-5p, -181c-5p, -191-5p) measured by qRT-PCR against three spiked-in synthetic miRNAs, and compared with sera of [[APPswe-PS1dE9 mouse]] animals at 4 and 15 months.
- Mechanism: primary rat astrocytes (from newborn pups) exposed to 0.5 to 2 µM fibrillar Aβ1-42, with the NF-κB inhibitor BMS-345541, LPS as positive control, and an antisense oligonucleotide against miR-146a-5p (AMO-146a, 100 nM).

## Findings

- Cognition and gliosis: Aβ1-42 rats needed longer paths and longer escape latency in the water maze (significant on days 4 and 5 for latency), and showed 2.1-fold and 1.7-fold more GFAP-positive cells in CA1/CA2 and in CA3 plus dentate gyrus respectively. See [[Memory impairment]] and [[Astrocyte activation]], and [[GFAP]].
- First cohort (1 µg/µL): of the nine miRNAs, only [[MIR146A]] was significantly lower in serum; miR-9a-5p, miR-29a-3p and miR-29c-3p were lower and close to significance.
- Second cohort (2.5 µg/µL): [[MIR146A]], [[MIR29A]] and [[MIR29C]] were significantly lower at day 21, and [[MIR125B1]] and [[MIR191]] were the most changed. [[MIR9-1]] was not significantly changed in either cohort or in the kinetics.
- Kinetics: miR-29a-3p and miR-29c-3p were reduced at every time point, most at day 7 (p < 0.01), and recovered slightly by days 14 and 21. miR-146a-5p was not changed at day 7 but significantly lower at day 14 (p = 0.022) and day 21 (p = 0.027).
- Model comparison: in 15-month-old APPswe-PS1dE9 mice miR-125b-5p, -29a-3p, -29c-3p and -191-5p were lower, as in the infused rats, but miR-146a-5p was unchanged at either age (p > 0.179). Four-month-old transgenic mice showed no difference. miR-125b-5p fell far more in rats (p < 0.0004) than in mice (p < 0.02).
- CSF: in contrast to serum, miR-146a-5p was higher in the CSF of infused rats (p = 0.004), a serum-versus-CSF discrepancy the authors could not explain.
- Astrocyte mechanism: Aβ1-42 raised miR-146a-5p up to 1.5-fold at 2 µM, and this induction fell back to baseline with BMS-345541 ([[NF-kappaB signaling]] dependence). [[IRAK1]] mRNA fell while [[IRAK2]] and [[TRAF6]] were unchanged, and [[IL6]], [[IL1B]], [[TNF]] and [[CXCL1]] were not induced, whereas LPS induced IL6, IL1B and CXCL1.
- Loss of function: AMO-146a lowered miR-146a-5p (p = 0.01), raised IRAK1 (p = 0.042) and TRAF6 (p = 0.0035), left IRAK2 unchanged, and in Aβ1-42-treated astrocytes induced IL6 (p = 0.003), IL1B (p = 0.05) and CXCL1 (p = 0.01) but not TNF (p = 0.803).
- Interpretation offered: miR-146a-5p acts as a negative-feedback brake on the NF-κB pathway at the early, Aβ-only stage; at advanced stages with strong inflammatory input (for example tau phosphorylation or altered APOE expression) the brake may be overridden. The lower miR-29 and miR-191 levels are linked by the authors to possible de-repression of [[BACE1]], which miR-29a/b is described to target, and so to more Aβ production.

## Why This Source Matters

It separates miRNAs that follow Aβ deposition in both a transgenic mouse and an infusion rat (miR-29a, miR-29c, miR-125b, miR-191) from one, miR-146a-5p, that responds only when the brain is directly exposed to aggregated Aβ, and it supplies a cell-level explanation in astrocytes for why Aβ alone does not trigger cytokines. It adds time-resolved animal data and a direction conflict between serum (down) and CSF (up) that any cut-off based miRNA marker has to face.

## Assessment

- Group sizes are small (7 to 8 sera per group, 5 animals for GFAP, 5 transgenic sera) and tests are non-parametric with no multiple-testing correction across the nine miRNAs; the first-cohort miR-146a-5p finding is a single p < 0.05.
- The two rat cohorts differ in dose and the two models differ in species and age, so the "exclusive" miR-146a-5p signal is confounded by model; the authors themselves note that infusion models inflammation by direct exposure while the transgenic model gives chronic inflammation.
- The infusion model is a partial model and the data are from rodents; human relevance of the serum decrease is untested here.
- Astrocyte experiments were in rat primary cells, with the inhibitor and AMO results from one representative experiment performed twice in triplicate; that TRAF6 level is the switch is inferred, not shown with a TRAF6 intervention. The authors state that other pathways are not excluded.
- Aβ1-42 effects were not tested alongside inflammatory co-stimuli, so the claim that cytokine induction in other studies is driven by those co-stimuli rests on literature comparison.

## Open Questions

- Does serum miR-146a-5p fall, and CSF miR-146a-5p rise, in people with early-stage Alzheimer disease (the evidence is from infused rats only)?
- Why do serum and CSF miR-146a-5p move in opposite directions after intrahippocampal Aβ1-42 infusion (the authors call the reason unclear)?
- Is the TRAF6 level the threshold that switches astrocytes from a quiet to a cytokine-producing state (shown only by a miR-146a-5p inhibitor, not by manipulating TRAF6 directly)?
- Do reduced miR-29a-3p and miR-191-5p raise BACE1 expression and Aβ production in the infused rat hippocampus (proposed in the discussion but not measured)?
