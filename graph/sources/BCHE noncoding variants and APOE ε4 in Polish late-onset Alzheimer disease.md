# BCHE noncoding variants and APOE ε4 in Polish late-onset Alzheimer disease

A small case-control genotyping study with a protein follow-up. In 55 Polish patients with [[Late-onset Alzheimer disease]] and 18 age-matched elderly controls, the authors sequenced four regions of [[BCHE]] and genotyped the [[APOE]] ε4 allele. The well-known butyrylcholinesterase K variant ([[BCHE rs1803274]]) alone did not separate patients from controls, but ten patients (18%) carried it together with two noncoding variants, [[BCHE rs1126680]] in the 5′ untranslated region and [[BCHE rs55781031]] in intron 2, and ε4; no control did. Mass spectrometry of serum BChE from homozygous donors showed that rs1126680 moves the translation start upstream, adding 41 amino acids to the signal peptide (a 69-residue peptide that was not cleaved off), a form the authors call N-BChE.

## Why This Source Matters

The long-running claim that the BCHE K variant acts together with ε4 to raise [[Alzheimer disease]] risk rests on studies that looked only at coding sequence and gave inconsistent results. This paper argues that the missing signal sits in noncoding positions that travel in cis with the K variant, and it offers a molecular consequence for one of them (an alternative translational start, see [[Translational initiation]]). The result is a lead, not a risk score: ten carriers, controls selected to all carry ε4, and no replication.

## Cohort and design

- Patients: 55 with late-onset disease, 26 men and 29 women, mean age 83 (65 to 102), diagnosed by geriatric psychiatrists from interview, imaging, the Hachinski Ischemic Scale, Geriatric Depression Scale, Mini Mental State Examination and Clock Drawing Test. Controls: 18 without dementia, 9 men and 9 women, mean age 76 (68 to 87). All participants were Polish; ethics approval came from the Medical University of Gdansk.
- Four BCHE amplicons were Sanger sequenced; APOE ε2/ε3/ε4 was called from rs429358 and rs7412 (see [[APOE rs429358]] and [[APOE rs7412]]). A separate Polish control collection of 1,200 people was genotyped earlier.
- BChE was purified from 0.1 mL of serum on Hupresin affinity resin and size exclusion chromatography, trypsin digested and read by nanoUPLC Orbitrap LC-MS/MS, searched in MaxQuant against a home-made database including BChE isoforms. The number of donors analysed is not stated.

## Findings

- All 55 patients and all 18 controls carried ε4, so the comparison tests BCHE variants within ε4 carriers only.
- Ten of 55 patients (18%) carried rs1126680, rs55781031 and rs1803274 together with ε4; no control did. A further 8 patients carried the K variant with ε4. In controls the K variant with ε4 was reported in 6 of 18 (the text elsewhere says 6 of 12). Overall about one third of patients (18 of 55) and one third of controls were K-positive, so the K variant alone showed no association.
- Mutations were mostly heterozygous. rs1126680 lies 32 nucleotides upstream of the known start codon and is described as in cis with the K variant and the intron 2 change.
- Mass spectrometry found peptides matching a new start site 69 amino acids before Glu1 of mature BChE; the 69-residue signal peptide stayed on the secreted protein. The prediction program ATGpr found two further in-frame start codons, with the upstream one scoring slightly higher for the Kozak consensus (0.17 against 0.14; 1 would be perfect). That would predict higher plasma activity, but plasma from carriers of all three variants had lower BChE activity ([[Decreased plasma butyrylcholinesterase activity]]).
- RNA folding predictions (MFOLD, RNAsnp) showed no major structural change outside the altered site; the authors suggest the substitution lowers protein binding to the 5′UTR and favours the upstream start.
- The authors propose that the three BCHE variants plus ε4 give "100% confidence" of late-onset disease; they also state that 82% of patients lack the combination.

## Mechanism proposed and background cited

BChE sits in amyloid plaques and neurofibrillary tangles, is mainly glial in brain, and may help convert amyloid-β ([[Amyloid-beta]]) from a benign to a toxic form; the K variant is reported to be less effective than wild type at limiting fibril growth. N-terminally extended membrane forms of acetylcholinesterase ([[ACHE]]) arise by alternative splicing and may play a part in apoptosis; the authors present N-BChE as an analogous extended form produced by an alternative start rather than splicing.

## Assessment

- Controls were all ε4 carriers and numbered 18, so the study cannot speak to ε4 itself, and the reported 0 of 18 versus 10 of 55 difference is a small-numbers result with no stated test or p value. The "100% confidence" wording is not supported by this design.
- Control counts are internally inconsistent (6 of 18 versus 6 of 12; 8 of 45 versus 8 of 55 patients), and patients were seven years older than controls on average.
- Mass spectrometry evidence comes from homozygous serum donors of unstated number; the link from the extended signal peptide to disease mechanism is untested, and activity differences are given without values.
- The Kozak scores predict the opposite of the observed lower activity, which the authors leave unexplained.

## Open questions

- Does the combination of rs1126680, rs55781031, rs1803274 and ε4 occur at a higher rate in late-onset Alzheimer disease in an independent, larger cohort with ε4-negative participants included?
- Does the 69-residue N-BChE signal peptide change secretion, enzyme activity, or association with amyloid plaques in cells (the study measured plasma activity only, without values)?
- Which of the three BCHE variants, if any, drives the lower plasma activity, given that they sit in cis (haplotype data were not resolved by variant)?
