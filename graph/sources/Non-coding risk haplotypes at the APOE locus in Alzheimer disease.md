# Non-coding risk haplotypes at the APOE locus in Alzheimer disease

A fine-mapping study of the extended [[APOE]] region (chr19:45.3-45.5 Mb, GRCh37) in whole-genome sequence and imputed array data from Chinese and non-Asian cohorts. It asks whether the APOE ε4 coding change is the only risk factor in the locus, and finds common haplotypes in the [[NECTIN2]] (PVRL2) and [[APOC1]] regions that raise [[Alzheimer disease]] risk independently of ε4 and ε2, track with memory, hippocampal volume and plasma and cerebrospinal fluid markers, shift brain and blood transcript levels, and sit in brain chromatin interaction hotspots.

## Design

- Discovery cohort: 1172 mainland Chinese participants from Huashan Hospital, Shanghai (477 Alzheimer disease, 253 [[Mild cognitive impairment]], 442 controls), whole-genome sequenced; replication in a Hong Kong sequencing cohort (206 after relatedness filtering: 108 patients, 98 controls) and in the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI, 808 genomes).
- Non-Asian datasets: three array-based cohorts, ADNI, the NIA Alzheimer's Disease Centers cohort (ADC) and the Late Onset Alzheimer's Disease family study (LOAD), plus trans-ethnic GWAS summary statistics (Jun et al.) from the NIAGADS repository.
- Variant calling with GATK HaplotypeCaller and Beagle phasing gave 682 common variants (554 SNPs, 128 indels); [[Statistical fine-mapping]] used PLINK logistic regression and CAVIAR; [[Haplotype association analysis]] pooled cohorts by random-effects meta-analysis (7092 patients, 4856 controls).
- Expression analysis used GTEx and CommonMind genotype and transcript data ([[Expression quantitative trait locus analysis]]); chromatin contacts came from Hi-C in adult and fetal brain ([[Hi-C chromatin interaction analysis]]).

## Findings

- **Signals beyond ε4.** Among APOE ε3 homozygotes a cluster of risk variants near APOC1 remained, led by [[APOC1 rs157592]]. In all mainland participants the top variant was [[APOC1 rs56131196]] (effect 0.869, p = 1.1 x 10^-10). CAVIAR gave nine variants with posterior probability above 10% in three loci; the leading one per locus was [[NECTIN2 rs11668861]] (42.5%), [[APOE rs429358]] (13.9%) and rs56131196 (21.5%). In the trans-ethnic data, [[APOC1 rs12721051]], rs56131196 and [[APOC1 rs4420638]] stayed significant after adjusting for ε4.
- **Haplotypes.** 33 variants in linkage disequilibrium with the nine causal ones formed two blocks. Minor haplotypes PVRL2 alpha and beta and APOC1 gamma, and two long-range ε4-bearing haplotypes (delta and epsilon) spanning the three loci, were enriched in patients and in mild cognitive impairment and co-occurred more often there. Linkage disequilibrium among risk variants was stronger in patients than controls. Phasing accuracy exceeded 95% for haplotypes above 5% frequency; PacBio long reads of nine lymphoblastoid cell lines and an Ashkenazi trio confirmed the haplotypes.
- **Independence from APOE.** After controlling for ε4 and ε2, alpha, gamma, delta and epsilon all kept genome-wide significant meta-analysis p values (below 5 x 10^-8 in the main analysis; meta-p below 0.01 in the pooled forest plots). Among ε3 homozygotes, gamma was associated (effect 2.203, p = 6.8 x 10^-3) and alpha only in women (effect 0.980, p = 0.038 in the mainland cohort; same direction but not significant in ADC and LOAD).
- **Endophenotypes (ADNI).** PVRL2 alpha was associated with worse Everyday Cognition scores, especially memory (study-partner report p = 2.3 x 10^-4), with lower whole brain, middle temporal and hippocampal volume ([[Hippocampal atrophy]], p = 0.021), lower plasma total Aβ1-42 (FDR 0.009) and lower cerebrospinal fluid [[ICAM1]] (FDR 0.054). APOC1 gamma was associated, dose-dependently, with lower plasma free Aβ1-40 (FDR below 0.001) and lower plasma [[CCL7]] (also called MCP3; FDR 0.040).
- **Expression.** Both PVRL2 alpha and beta were linked to lower blood PVRL2 transcript. In brain, alpha and gamma were linked to higher APOE and APOC1 transcript, most clearly in people without ε4 (gamma effect 1.284, meta-p 1.4 x 10^-8), whereas ε4 itself lowered TOMM40, APOE and APOC1 transcript (effects -0.370, -0.392, -0.444). APOE transcript also rose with age in blood and brain. Single variants showed distal effects on brain APOE: [[NECTIN2 rs519113]] and [[APOC1 rs60049679]] (meta-p about 1 x 10^-5), and [[NECTIN2 rs60389450]] a strong local effect on blood PVRL2 (p = 8.8 x 10^-34).
- **Allelic imbalance.** The PVRL2 untranslated-region variant [[NECTIN2 rs6859]] showed fewer minor-allele transcripts in many tissues, strongest in blood (minor fraction 0.332; confirmed by droplet digital PCR at 0.302) and in eQTLGen (n = 29,726, Z = -37.0); haplotype beta lowered the isoforms that carry it. APOE rs429358 showed the same imbalance, including in brain.
- **Chromatin and binding.** Hi-C hotspots covered the PVRL2, APOE and APOC1 haplotype regions; the APOC1 region contacted the PVRL2-TOMM40 region (FDR below 1 x 10^-9) and the PVRL2 region contacted the PVRL2 promoter in adult brain. Gel-shift assays showed that regions around rs6859, rs483082 and the indel rs11568822 bind nuclear protein, and in silico tools predicted that [[MIR595]] binds the major G but not the minor A allele of rs6859.
- **Ancestry.** In the 1000 Genomes data ε4 frequency was 0.086 in East Asians and 0.155 in Europeans; alpha was equally common (0.102 vs 0.103), but beta (0.081 vs 0.318) and gamma (0.066 vs 0.111) were rarer in East Asians, delta peaked in East Asians (0.043) and epsilon in Europeans (0.059).
- **Discussion points.** The authors link elevated brain APOE to ageing, transgenic-mouse plaque load and [[Disease-associated microglia]], and note that PVRL2 is a herpesvirus entry receptor, so that altered blood PVRL2 might bear on viral entry, a possibility they leave open.

## Why This Source Matters

It moves the APOE locus from a single-allele story to a multi-signal region in which neighbouring non-coding haplotypes act in parallel with ε4, and it ties those haplotypes to cognition, brain volume, blood and cerebrospinal fluid markers, gene expression and chromatin contacts. It also shows that haplotype frequencies differ by ancestry, which matters for risk prediction (see [[Polygenic risk score]]) and for the common practice of excluding the whole locus from scores.

## Assessment

- Effects are modest and several endophenotype associations are marginal (cerebrospinal fluid ICAM-1 FDR 0.054; planning p = 0.062); the female-only PVRL2 alpha effect among ε3 homozygotes replicated in direction but not significance.
- Causality is inferred from fine-mapping, expression and Hi-C overlap; gel-shift assays show binding capacity, not allele-specific regulation, and the microRNA result is a computational prediction.
- Hi-C data come from brains without a stated dementia diagnosis (adult cortex from people without psychiatric disorders, and fetal brain), so chromatin states in Alzheimer brain are untested.
- Haplotype claims depend on phasing, validated at above 95% only for haplotypes above 5% frequency; most statistics sit in supplementary tables that were not in the text read.
- The main cohort is Chinese and the replication sets are mostly of European ancestry; ancestry-specific haplotype frequencies limit pooled interpretation.

## Open Questions

- Do the NECTIN2 and APOC1 risk haplotypes change risk or expression in people of African and South Asian ancestry (no sequencing cohorts of these ancestries were analysed)?
- Does the minor allele of rs6859 lower NECTIN2 transcript through lost microRNA or transcription factor binding (only in silico and gel-shift evidence so far)?
- Is the raised brain APOE transcript seen with PVRL2 alpha and APOC1 gamma reproduced in brain tissue from people with Alzheimer disease (the expression data came from donors without a stated diagnosis)?
- Does blood NECTIN2 level in carriers of the risk haplotypes alter herpesvirus entry or outcome (raised by the authors, not tested)?
