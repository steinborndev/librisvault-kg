# FAK overexpression raises amyloid load and impairs memory in 3xTg-AD mice

Focal adhesion kinase ([[PTK2]], FAK) is abundant in developing and adult brain, and its phosphorylation is altered in [[Alzheimer disease]] brain, yet its in vivo role in the disease was untested. The authors injected lentiviruses carrying either a Ptk2 shRNA (knockdown) or Ptk2 cDNA (overexpression) into the hippocampus of 8-month-old [[3xTg-AD mouse]] animals, tested learning and memory three weeks later, examined plaques, neuron and glia morphology, and profiled phosphoproteins with antibody microarrays. Overexpression, but not knockdown, worsened memory and raised amyloid plaque number.

## Design

- Homozygous 3xTg-AD mice (carrying APP Swedish, [[PSEN1]] M146V and [[MAPT]] P301L) and wild-type C57BL/6 males, 8 months old; stereotaxic injection into hippocampus (CA1), behaviour after 3 weeks. Knockdown and overexpression vectors were checked by immunoblot in HEK293T cells, and the shRNA was shown not to lower [[PTK2B]] (Pyk2).
- Behaviour: [[Fear conditioning]] (context and tone) and the [[Morris water maze]]; n = 10 mice per group for overexpression.
- Histology: 6E10 staining for amyloid plaques, Golgi-Cox staining with Sholl analysis for pyramidal neurons (45 neurons from 5 mice per group), GFAP and Iba1 staining for astrocytes and microglia.
- Molecular: an [[In-lysate kinase assay]] with recombinant FAK on pooled control hippocampal lysate, and a [[Kinexus KAM-1325 antibody microarray]] on hippocampi of control and overexpressing mice after the behavioural tasks, with pathway enrichment in DAVID.

## Findings

- **Behaviour.** Knockdown changed neither contextual or cued freezing nor water maze acquisition or probe performance. Overexpression left day-one freezing and acquisition latency unchanged but lowered context and cue freezing (p = 0.0175 and 0.0364) and cut time and entries in the target quadrant at the probe (p = 0.0281 and 0.0452). In wild-type mice the same overexpression changed nothing, so the effect needed the Alzheimer background.
- **Amyloid.** Overexpression raised the number of 6E10-positive plaques in hippocampus ([[Amyloid-beta]]; p < 0.001, 10 fields per group).
- **Neuron shape.** Basal, but not apical, dendrites of CA1 pyramidal neurons had fewer Sholl intersections and shorter filaments; spine density and spine types were unchanged.
- **Glia.** [[GFAP]]-positive astrocytes were fewer, covered less surface and had fewer process intersections, whereas Iba1-positive microglia were unchanged ([[Astrocyte activation]] was therefore reduced, not raised).
- **Direct targets in lysate.** Of 182 tyrosine-phosphorylated proteins, the authors highlight [[ROCK1]] (+53%) and [[ROCK2]] (+192%), [[ABL1]] (up to +101% at Y257), [[MST1R]] (+114%), [[GRIN2A]] and [[GRIN2B]] (Y943 +77%), [[IGF1R]] (+54%), [[ERBB2]] and [[ERBB3]], [[NTRK2]] (Y702 +532%), [[JAK2]], [[STAT1]], STAT5, [[STAT3]] pathway members and [[CDK5]] (+58%). Enriched pathways were integrin signalling, actin organisation, Rho GTPase activation, endocytosis, axon guidance, TNF, PI3K and STAT3 signalling.
- **In vivo signalling after memory testing.** Insulin pathway: [[INSR]] was 47% lower, [[MAPK3]] (ERK1) phosphorylation rose, inhibitory serine phosphorylation of [[IRS1]] rose (S312 +226%, S639 +99%), and [[PDPK1]] (+57%), [[AKT1]] (+62%) and inhibitory [[GSK3B]] S9 phosphorylation (+167%) rose. Pathway analysis showed negative regulation of insulin receptor signalling and raised [[Insulin resistance]].
- **Tau-linked kinases.** [[MAPK14]] (+55%), [[RPS6KB1]] (T252 +286%) and its substrate RPS6 rose, as did [[CDK5]] Y15 and multiple tau phosphosites (S396 +180%, S721 +69%, S739 +63%, T522 +52%), interpreted as [[Tau hyperphosphorylation]]; tau was also a tyrosine target in the lysate assay. Tau was not stained directly.
- **Cell cycle and death.** Higher phosphorylation of [[FYN]], [[CBL]], [[PXN]], [[SRC]], [[CDKN1A]] and [[TP53]] (S37 +95%), inhibitory [[CDK1]] Y15 (+216%), and downregulated [[ATM]] signalling with lower mitochondrial organisation and less negative regulation of neuronal apoptosis led to the model of [[Neuronal cell cycle re-entry]] failing to repair DNA and ending in neuronal death.
- **Cytoskeleton and astrocytes.** Raised [[CFL1]] S3 phosphorylation (+113%), [[PTPN12]] S39 phosphorylation (+77%) and [[PRKCA]] (+148%), with lower [[MARK1]] (-48%), fit the basal dendrite phenotype. Lower [[STAT3]] S727 phosphorylation (-54%), STAT1 (-37%) and JAK2 Y570 (-21%) fit blocked astrocyte differentiation. In vivo NMDA receptor tyrosine phosphorylation fell rather than rose, which the authors attribute to phosphatase-driven receptor endocytosis.

## Why This Source Matters

It gives the first in vivo test of FAK in an amyloid and tau mouse model and shows an asymmetry: removing FAK from hippocampus did nothing to memory, while adding it harmed memory and raised plaque number only in the Alzheimer-model background. The two proteomic screens turn that into a testable map from FAK to cytoskeletal, tau-kinase, cell cycle and brain insulin signalling nodes, and the authors propose FAK as a therapeutic target.

## Assessment

- The causal model rests on antibody-array phosphosite signals from pooled or small-n lysates (four array measurements per set) and was not confirmed by phosphosite immunoblots or tau staining; tau hyperphosphorylation, insulin resistance and cell death are inferred, not measured.
- Plaque counts use fields as the unit (10 per group) and one antibody (6E10); overexpression was by lentivirus that infects neurons and glia, so the cell type driving the amyloid and memory effects is unknown.
- FAK knockdown efficiency is given only as a significant mRNA drop; the lack of a knockdown phenotype could reflect incomplete knockdown or compensation by [[PTK2B]].
- Only male mice at one age were used. The methods text refers to "Pyk2-KD" mice for Golgi staining and the non-transgenic figure panels are cited inconsistently, which looks like carry-over from related work; the enrichment threshold is written as p ≤ 0.5 in legends and p ≤ 0.05 in the text.

## Open Questions

- Does hippocampal FAK overexpression raise tau pathology in 3xTg-AD mice when phospho-tau is measured directly by immunostaining or western blot (the study inferred it from antibody-array phosphosites)?
- Which cell type mediates the plaque and memory effects of FAK overexpression (the lentivirus infected both neurons and astrocytes and no cell-type-restricted promoter was used)?
- Does pharmacological FAK inhibition lower plaque load or rescue memory in 3xTg-AD mice (only knockdown, which had no effect, was tested)?
- Do [[PTK2B]] and FAK compensate for each other after knockdown in hippocampus (the knockdown vector was shown specific in HEK293T cells only)?
- Are [[ROCK2]] and [[ABL1]], the strongest FAK targets in the lysate assay, phosphorylated by FAK in living hippocampal neurons (no phosphosite-specific immunoblot was reported)?
