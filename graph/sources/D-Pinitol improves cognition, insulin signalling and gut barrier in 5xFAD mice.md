# D-Pinitol improves cognition, insulin signalling and gut barrier in 5xFAD mice

A preclinical study in Nutrients testing [[D-Pinitol]], a methylated inositol from carob pods, in homozygous [[5xFAD mouse]] animals and wild-type littermates. The compound was given at 200 mg/kg/day in the drinking water for 18 weeks, from about 3.5 to 8 months of age, and the authors looked at behaviour, plasma metabolic hormones, hippocampal [[PI3K-Akt-GSK3B axis]] and [[CDK5]] signalling, amyloid and tau markers, intestinal barrier genes, plasma [[Lipopolysaccharide]] and cytokines, and faecal microbiota. The pitch is a single oral compound that touches several arms of [[Alzheimer disease]]: insulin resistance, amyloid, tau and the gut.

## Design

- Mice: non-transgenic and homozygous 5xFAD (APP Swedish, Florida and London mutations plus PSEN1 M146L and L286V under the neuronal Thy-1 promoter; B6/SJL background), both sexes. Groups: Non-Tg control 16 (7 male, 9 female), Non-Tg D-pinitol 16 (7, 9), 5xFAD control 14 (7, 7), 5xFAD D-pinitol 17 (10, 7).
- Treatment: 1 mg/mL D-pinitol (98% purity) in water, replaced daily, about 200 mg/kg/day; controls got water. Tissue was taken at 32 weeks of age.
- Behaviour: sucrose preference test (anhedonia cut-off 65%), elevated plus maze, and a [[Morris water maze]] with visible-platform, four-day hidden-platform acquisition, two memory retention tests and a one-day reversal phase.
- Readouts: Bio-Plex plasma insulin, glucagon, leptin, ghrelin and PAI-1 (n = 10); MSD plasma IL-5, IL-6, KC/GRO and TNF (n = 7); plasma LPS by ELISA; hippocampal Western blots for phospho and total PI3K p85, Akt, GSK-3β, CDK5 with p35 and p25, tau (AT8, AT100, total) and [[IDE]] (n = 4); Aβ40 and Aβ42 plaque counts in CA1, CA2 and dentate gyrus (8 mice, 3 sections each); small-intestine qPCR for Cldn3, Ocln and Tlr4 (n = 7); 16S rRNA (V3-V4) sequencing of colon faeces with QIIME2.
- Statistics: one- and two-way ANOVA with Tukey tests; microbiota with Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney tests.

## Findings

- **Mood and anxiety-like behaviour.** Untreated 5xFAD mice drank less sucrose than wild type at baseline (a sign of [[Anhedonia]]); after treatment, 5xFAD mice moved almost to the 65% threshold (p < 0.01). Genotype changed time in the open arms of the elevated plus maze, and D-pinitol shifted it in 5xFAD mice; body weight was not affected.
- **Spatial learning.** All groups improved on the visible platform. In four-day acquisition only the wild-type treated group became faster on day 4; treated 5xFAD mice matched untreated 5xFAD mice, and the first retention test showed no group differences. In the reversal phase, treated 5xFAD mice found the new platform faster than untreated 5xFAD mice, and in the second retention test untreated 5xFAD mice kept searching the old quadrant while treated ones split time between old and new, which the authors read as better cognitive flexibility.
- **Metabolic hormones.** Untreated 5xFAD plasma had lower [[INS]], higher [[GCG]] and a lower insulin to glucagon ratio; D-pinitol brought insulin and glucagon back to wild-type levels. [[SERPINE1]] (PAI-1) fell with treatment in both genotypes, [[LEP]] rose in both, and [[GHRL]] rose in 5xFAD mice.
- **Hippocampal signalling.** Phospho-[[PIK3R1]] (p85, Tyr607), phospho-[[AKT1]] (Ser473) and Ser9 phospho-[[GSK3B]] were low in untreated 5xFAD hippocampus and rose with treatment; total proteins were unchanged. p25 was higher in 5xFAD mice irrespective of treatment, p35 ([[CDK5R1]]) was higher in 5xFAD, and the p35 to CDK5 ratio fell with treatment; total CDK5 did not change.
- **Amyloid and tau.** Treated 5xFAD hippocampus had fewer Aβ40 and Aβ42 plaques (p < 0.0001) and higher IDE protein (p < 0.001). Tau phosphorylated at Ser202/Thr205 (AT8) was no higher in untreated 5xFAD than wild-type mice, but fell with treatment; phospho-Thr212/Ser214 (AT100) and total [[MAPT]] did not change.
- **Gut barrier and inflammation.** In untreated 5xFAD small intestine [[CLDN3]] mRNA was raised, [[OCLN]] lowered against treated wild type, and [[TLR4]] mRNA and plasma LPS were higher; treatment lowered TLR4 and LPS in both genotypes and left claudin-3 and occludin no longer significantly different from wild type. Plasma [[IL5]] was lower in 5xFAD mice regardless of treatment, while [[IL6]], [[CXCL1]] (KC/GRO) and [[TNF]] were lower only in treated wild-type mice.
- **Microbiota.** At family level 5xFAD mice had less Prevotellaceae and more Eggerthellaceae and Streptococcaceae; treatment lowered Streptococcaceae in both genotypes, raised Lachnospiraceae and Acholeplasmataceae in wild type only (Acholeplasmataceae fell in 5xFAD), and raised an Enterobacteriaceae family in treated 5xFAD mice. At genus level Escherichia-Shigella was higher in untreated 5xFAD mice and returned to wild-type levels with treatment, while Streptococcus rose in both treated groups.

## Why This Source Matters

It is one of the few Alzheimer mouse papers to read a single nutraceutical-grade compound across brain, blood and gut at once, so it gives a concrete test case for the idea that [[Insulin resistance]], the [[Gut microbiota]] and [[Increased intestinal permeability]] feed amyloid and tau pathology. It also shows the limits of that reading: most gains are in amyloid load and signalling markers in an amyloid-only model, and several of the headline inflammation and tau claims rest on thin or unchanged baselines (see Assessment).

## Assessment

- Small biochemical groups (n = 4 per group for Western blots, 7 for gut and cytokines, 10 for hormones, 8 for plaques) against 14 to 17 animals per group in behaviour; no sample size is given for the microbiota, and multiple outcomes are not formally corrected despite a stated intention to adjust.
- The tau claim is thin: AT8 phosphorylation was not elevated in untreated 5xFAD hippocampus, so treatment lowered a level that was not abnormal, and AT100 and total tau did not move.
- The CDK5 mechanism is inferred from Western blot ratios (p35 to CDK5); no kinase activity assay, no CDK5 inhibitor and no p25 change with treatment, so the "CDK5 modulation" in the abstract is a hypothesis built on earlier rat data, not shown here.
- The abstract says treatment reduced LPS-associated inflammation, but plasma IL-6, KC/GRO and TNF fell only in wild-type mice, not in 5xFAD mice.
- Leaky gut is inferred from plasma LPS and intestinal TLR4 mRNA; no direct permeability assay was run. Tlr4, Cldn3 and Ocln were measured as mRNA, with protein shown only in supplementary blots.
- The elevated plus maze result is hard to read: the results section reports a genotype effect on open-arm time and a reduction with treatment, while the discussion cites earlier work in which 5xFAD mice spend more time in the open arms and treats treatment as normalising.
- Internal inconsistencies: the microbiota text mentions "8 weeks" of treatment although dosing lasted 18 weeks, the text calls a family Enterobacteriaceae while the figure legend lists Enterococcaceae, and the Streptococcaceae family fell with treatment while the Streptococcus genus rose.
- One dose, no positive-control drug, no dose-response, no brain or plasma exposure data, and mixed-sex groups with unequal sex ratios analysed together; the authors themselves note that 5xFAD models only early-onset amyloidosis and not sporadic disease.

## Open Questions

- Does D-pinitol lower tau phosphorylation in a model that develops real tau pathology, such as a 3xTg-AD or PS19 mouse (the 5xFAD hippocampus showed no raised AT8 at 8 months)?
- Is the lower p35 to CDK5 ratio caused by D-pinitol acting on CDK5 signalling (only Western blot ratios were measured, with no kinase activity assay or inhibitor control)?
- Does orally dosed D-pinitol reach the mouse brain at effective concentrations (no pharmacokinetic or tissue exposure data were reported)?
- Does the gut-barrier benefit hold when permeability is measured directly, for example by FITC-dextran leakage (the paper infers it from plasma LPS and intestinal Tlr4 mRNA)?
- Are the microbiota shifts a cause of the cognitive and amyloid effects (no antibiotic, germ-free or faecal transplant arm was run)?
- Do male and female 5xFAD mice respond differently to D-pinitol (sexes were pooled with unequal numbers and not analysed separately)?
