# Blood multiomic associations of Alzheimer disease risk variants in healthy adults

A phenome-wide association study in 2831 cognitively normal adults of the [[Arivale Scientific Wellness cohort]] (ages 18 to 89+) that asks what 25 genome-wide-significant [[Alzheimer disease]] variants, the [[APOE]] ε2-defining SNP and an Alzheimer polygenic score do to roughly 2000 blood measurements (clinical chemistry, plasma proteins and metabolites). It finds that known risk variants already change blood lipids and immune proteins from early adulthood.

## Design

- Cohort: 2831 Arivale participants with clinical-grade whole-genome sequencing (30x, Illumina) and at least one blood analyte class; 61% female, 22% non-white by self-report, 28% obese, mean age 47.
- Analytes (2008 in total): up to 63 fasting clinical laboratory tests; 274 proteins from three Olink proximity extension panels in up to 2694 people, plus 919 further proteins from ten panels in a subsample of 354 enriched for APOE ε2/ε2 and ε4/ε4; 754 plasma metabolites from the Metabolon HD4 platform in up to 1909 people.
- Genetic exposures: 25 common or low-frequency SNPs (allele frequency above 1%) significant in an updated International Genomics of Alzheimer's Project meta-analysis, linked to 24 genes (two in APOE), plus [[APOE rs7412]]; and an APOE-free [[Polygenic risk score]] built from FDR-filtered, LD-pruned (r2 below 0.2) summary statistics.
- Model: linear regression of each analyte on genotype (0, 1, 2), adjusted for age, sex, genetic principal components 1 to 4 and, for clinical labs, vendor; Benjamini-Hochberg FDR per SNP at 0.05 for main effects and 0.1 for the SNP x sex interaction. Age-group boxplots (by decade) and ANOVA showed how effects vary across adulthood; analyses were repeated in white and non-white strata.
- Post-hoc follow-up of the [[PICALM rs3851179]] interaction in the Alzheimer's Disease Genetics Consortium (12,324 cases, 11,453 controls of European ancestry; HRC-imputed; sex-stratified and sex-interaction logistic regression, METAL meta-analysis), and a GO biological-process network of the sex-interacting proteins with [[ClueGO functional network analysis]].

## Findings

- 33 SNP-analyte associations passed FDR 0.05, most of them for the two APOE SNPs; the only other SNPs with a significant main effect were [[INPP5D rs10933431]], [[NYAP1 rs12539172]] and [[ABCA7 p.G1527A]], so five variants in all.
- The strongest signal was the NYAP1 3' region SNP [[NYAP1 rs12539172]] with plasma [[PILRB]] and [[PILRA]]: the minor allele (Alzheimer odds ratio 0.92) lowered normalized protein expression of both in a roughly dose-dependent way (FDR p = 2.2 x 10^-33 and 2.3 x 10^-17). Using the putative causal PILRA missense variant [[PILRA p.G78R]] (G78R, in LD with the index SNP at r2 = 0.77) strengthened the associations (FDR p = 3.6 x 10^-52 for PILRB and 1.4 x 10^-22 for PILRA), with genotype differences in every age group.
- [[APOE rs429358]] (ε4): higher total and LDL cholesterol and lower HDL, with genotype differences blunted in older groups, probably by statin use (clearer in people not on statins, top marker LDL particle number); higher levels of two proteins, [[PLA2G7]] and [[CD28]]; and higher levels of two [[Diacylglycerol]] species.
- [[APOE rs7412]] (ε2): lower LDL-cholesterol measures (strongest in the 16 ε2 homozygotes, all aged 30 to 59, who still had lower LDL despite the 5-10% risk of type III hyperlipoproteinemia in homozygotes); higher levels of one monoglyceride and four diacylglycerol measures (the same two diacylglycerols that rose with ε4); and six proteins that were higher in ε2 carriers and are reported as novel: [[LDLR]], [[HMOX1]], [[SLAMF8]], [[RNF31]], [[CNTNAP2]] and [[SRP14]].
- [[ABCA7 p.G1527A]] (risk allele, odds ratio 1.15): lower levels of two [[Lactosylceramide]] species from the youngest age group onward, and higher [[DEFA1]] (defensin alpha 1).
- [[INPP5D rs10933431]] (protective): lower [[IDUA]] (alpha-L-iduronidase).
- No analyte was associated with the polygenic score after FDR correction, including after adjusting for ε4 or restricting to non-ε4 carriers, and there was no modification by sex or APOE4 status.
- Sex modified four variants. At [[PICALM rs3851179]] (protective) the minor allele went with higher levels of 30 highly correlated proteins in men and lower in women (mean pairwise Spearman rho 0.49), and a sex-specific effect on five long-chain fatty acids and one polyunsaturated fatty acid, [[Docosahexaenoic acid]]. In the ADGC the minor allele lowered Alzheimer risk in both sexes, more strongly in men. A variant near [[CD2AP]] interacted with sex on three sphingomyelins and three plasmalogens; a variant in [[SPI1]] on [[SMOC2]]; and the missense ABCA7 SNP on [[UBE2F]].
- White-only analyses recapitulated the overall results and the non-white group showed consistent effect sizes, but the small non-white subgroups could not be analysed separately.

## Interpretation by the authors

- The PILRA/PILRB result supports the PILRA missense variant as the causal variant at the locus; lower circulating PILRA in carriers may add to the receptor's reduced ligand binding (including herpes simplex virus 1 glycoprotein B) and so to protection, which the authors tie to a possible viral contribution to Alzheimer risk.
- For ε2, upregulated LDLR may compensate for the weak binding of APOE2 to LDLR and explain the low LDL; HMOX1 and SLAMF8 point to antioxidant effects, RNF31 (HOIP, part of the linear ubiquitin chain assembly complex) to misfolded-protein clearance.
- The PICALM sex interaction is read as a sex difference in neuroimmune response, with a possible link through DHA and cholesterol homeostasis.
- The polygenic score probably dilutes single-gene effects, and may need older or cognitively impaired people to show an effect in blood.

## Why This Source Matters

It maps the peripheral footprint of Alzheimer genetic risk in people without dementia and across the whole adult age range, showing lipid and immune effects of risk variants decades before disease. It nominates the PILRA missense variant as causal at the NYAP1/ZCWPW1/PILRA locus, offers new ε2-associated proteins that may explain ε2 protection, and shows that [[PICALM rs3851179]] acts differently by sex, reinforcing the need to analyse sex as a biological variable.

## Assessment

- The cohort is self-selected, mostly non-Hispanic white, west-coast and probably higher in socioeconomic status; no replication cohort with parallel omics across ages was available, so the novel ε2-protein associations are unreplicated.
- Plasma only: brain or CSF were not examined, and pleiotropic effects cannot be told from causal Alzheimer mechanisms.
- Protein sample sizes differ widely (the extended panels cover 354 people, with ε2 and ε4 homozygotes enriched, and only 16 ε2 homozygotes), and the FDR correction over 2008 analytes is acknowledged as conservative given correlated analytes.
- Only SNPs from WGS were studied (no indels, copy number or repeats), and metabolite speciation limits comparison with earlier metabolomics studies.
- The text gives the NYAP1 SNP as rs12539712 once and rs12539172 elsewhere, and describes the PILRA missense change both as G78R and as an R78 substitution; the figure legends and the rest of the text use rs12539172.
- The sex-specific ADGC supporting results for the SNP-analyte analysis are cited as "data not shown".
- The source states that supplementary tables carry the full results; they were not part of the text read, so effect sizes for most associations are not recorded here.

## Open Questions

- Do the six ε2-associated plasma proteins (LDLR, HMOX1, SLAMF8, RNF31, CNTNAP2, SRP14) replicate in an independent cohort with the same Olink panels (none was identified for all-ages, health-heterogeneous adults)?
- Does the PILRA G78R missense variant itself lower circulating PILRA in an experimental system, separately from its effect on ligand binding (inferred here from LD with the index SNP)?
- Which of the 30 correlated proteins at the PICALM variant carries the sex-specific effect (the proteins are highly correlated, so a passenger effect cannot be excluded)?
- Are the lipid and immune effects of risk variants seen in the blood of healthy adults also found in CSF or brain tissue of the same people (only peripheral plasma was sampled)?
