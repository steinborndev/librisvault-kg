# APOE2 lowers amyloid-beta in isogenic hiPSC models of familial Alzheimer disease

Most work on [[APOE]] has asked why the ε4 allele raises the risk of [[Alzheimer disease]]; this study asks how the protective ε2 allele works. The authors edited APOE3 human induced pluripotent stem cell (hiPSC) lines from patients with [[Familial Alzheimer disease]] into matched APOE2 lines, differentiated them into neural cultures, and compared Aβ, tau and amyloid precursor protein (APP) processing between the two genotypes. They report that APOE2 cut secreted Aβ in all three familial lines, that this depends partly on astrocytes, and that the likeliest mechanism is a shift of [[APP]] processing away from the amyloidogenic route.

## Design

- Editing: the transient reporter for editing enrichment (TREE) method, which the group had published earlier, gave clonal homozygous editing near 90%. Parent lines were homozygous for APOE3 (the ε2 allele corresponds to [[APOE rs7412]]); edited clones kept pluripotency markers, three-germ-layer potential and a normal karyotype. See [[Isogenic hiPSC neural culture model]].
- Lines: three familial pairs carrying [[APP p.V717I]], an APP duplication (APPdp) or [[PSEN1 p.A246E]], plus one pair from a non-demented control (NDC).
- Cultures: neural progenitors expanded on laminin-coated microcarriers and differentiated for at least 30 days into mixed TUJ1, MAP2 and NEUN positive neurons with GFAP positive astrocytes.
- Cell separation: magnetic sorting on the astrocyte surface marker CD44 gave astrocyte populations 92 to 99% CD44 positive and neuron populations negative. RNA sequencing, GO analysis, APOE secretion (higher from astrocytes) and calcium transients (slow in astrocytes, rapid in neurons) confirmed identity.
- Readouts: ELISA for secreted Aβ40 and Aβ42, sAPPα, sAPPβ, APP-βCTF, total APP, phospho-tau (Thr231) and total tau; FAM-labelled Aβ42 uptake by flow cytometry; surface [[LDLR]] and [[LRP1]] on astrocytes. Groups were compared with unpaired two-sided t tests.

## Findings

- **Amyloid.** Converting APOE3 to APOE2 lowered secreted Aβ42 and Aβ40 in mixed cultures of all three familial pairs. The Aβ42/40 ratio fell only with PSEN1 p.A246E, not with the two APP lines (the authors suggest the APP mutations push Aβ42 too high for APOE2 to move the ratio). The non-demented pair showed no change in Aβ or ratio, so the effect appears only where Aβ is raised by a familial mutation.
- **Tau.** The phospho-tau to total tau ratio fell only in the PSEN1 p.A246E pair, the one in which the Aβ42/40 ratio fell, which the authors read as tau pathology following the ratio and not total amyloid. Removing astrocytes did not change phospho-tau. See [[MAPT]] and [[Amyloid cascade hypothesis]].
- **Astrocytes.** Neuron-only cultures secreted more Aβ40 than mixed cultures in all three pairs, more Aβ42 in the APPdp and PSEN1 p.A246E pairs, and a higher Aβ42/40 ratio in those two, pointing to astrocytes lowering neuronal Aβ. The effect of APOE2 therefore has cell-autonomous and non-autonomous parts.
- **Uptake and receptors.** Astrocytes took up far more Aβ than neurons, but only the APOE2 astrocytes with APP p.V717I took up more than their APOE3 partners. LDLR surface level was lower in APOE2 astrocytes in three of four lines, LRP1 did not differ, and receptor-uptake correlations tracked mutation type or disease status, not APOE genotype. Higher uptake by astrocytes therefore does not explain the lower Aβ; see [[Amyloid-beta clearance]].
- **APP processing.** The sAPPβ/sAPPα ratio and the βCTF to total APP ratio were lower in APOE2 cultures of the APPdp and PSEN1 p.A246E pairs (a smaller trend with APP p.V717I), with unchanged total APP, indicating a shift from amyloidogenic to non-amyloidogenic cleavage. Pure neuronal cultures had higher sAPPβ relative to sAPPα than mixed ones, yet the APOE2 effect stayed. See [[Amyloid-beta formation]].

## Assessment

- Three familial lines and one control pair, each from a single parental genetic background, with small n per assay (as few as 3 to 6 in the sorted and βCTF experiments) and no stated multiple-comparison correction; effect sizes differ by line, and several conclusions (tau tracks the Aβ42/40 ratio, astrocytes contribute) rest on one or two pairs.
- Familial mutations drive the phenotypes, so the results may not transfer to sporadic late-onset disease, where hiPSC phenotypes are weak; the authors say APOE-isogenic sporadic lines are still needed.
- The APP-processing mechanism is inferred from secreted fragments; secretase activity, APOE-APP binding and APP trafficking were not tested, and an APOE4 arm and an APOE knockout were not included.
- The section header on astrocytes mediating the effect is stronger than the data: the APOE2 effect on APP processing persisted without astrocytes.
- The ethics statement and the supplementary figures were not available in the text read.

## Why This Source Matters

It is a human cell model that isolates the protective ε2 allele against an otherwise identical background and a defined familial mutation, a gap the authors note since earlier isogenic work addressed ε4. The finding that APOE2 acts upstream, on how much Aβ is made, and not mainly by faster astrocyte clearance, points to APP processing as a target for mimicking ε2 protection.

## Open Questions

- Does APOE2 change how [[APP]] meets β- and γ-secretase (for example through direct APOE-APP binding or APP trafficking), and which step of the pathway is altered?
- Does converting APOE3 to APOE2 lower Aβ and phospho-tau in hiPSC lines from sporadic late-onset Alzheimer disease patients (the study used only familial lines and one control pair)?
- Why does APOE2 raise astrocytic Aβ uptake only with APP p.V717I (receptor knockdown or overexpression was not tested)?
