# Tau antibody 12A12 and amyloid-beta in Tg2576 mouse hippocampus and retina

A preclinical mechanism study of why systemic treatment with the cleavage-specific tau antibody [[12A12 monoclonal antibody]], which neutralizes the toxic N-terminal 20-22 kDa tau fragment (NH2htau) of [[MAPT]], lowers amyloid beta (Aβ) production in the [[Tg2576 mouse]] model of [[Alzheimer disease]]. The authors looked in parallel at hippocampus and retina, on the premise that the eye mirrors brain neurodegeneration.

## Design

- Animals: 6-month-old Tg2576 mice (human [[APP]] with the Swedish K670N/M671L mutation) and wild-type littermates, three groups: wild-type plus saline, Tg2576 plus saline, Tg2576 plus 12A12 (30 μg per dose into the tail vein, two injections a week for 14 days). Tissue was taken two days after the last injection. Group sizes were 8-10 mice, with six or eight per immunoblot panel and metabolic panel (both sexes).
- Endocytic readouts: immunoblots of crude synaptosomal preparations (which retain endosomes and clathrin-coated vesicles) for APP, [[BACE1]], [[BIN1]] (the 65 kDa neuron-specific isoform), [[RIN3]], [[CD2AP]] and [[RAB5A]] (total Rab5, and the membrane-bound, GTP-active fraction against the cytosolic fraction after 100,000 g centrifugation of hippocampal extracts).
- Metabolic readouts: tissue homogenates with intact mitochondria assayed for glucose-driven oxygen consumption (high-resolution respirometry), hexokinase activity, and [[Lactic acid]] content; a glycolytic index was computed as lactate times hexokinase activity divided by oxygen consumption.
- Statistics: one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post hoc tests, GraphPad Prism.

## Findings

- Endocytic adaptors: neuronal BIN1 was lower in Tg2576 synapses in both tissues, while APP and BACE1 were higher. 12A12 restored BIN1 in both hippocampus and retina and lowered APP and BACE1 there. See [[Endocytosis]].
- RIN3 differed by tissue: slightly higher in Tg2576 hippocampus and lower in Tg2576 retina; 12A12 normalized it in the retina only. CD2AP did not differ between the three groups.
- Rab5: total Rab5 fell sharply in Tg2576 retina and was restored by 12A12; hippocampal total Rab5 did not change, the active membrane-bound fraction was lower in Tg2576 hippocampus and was not rescued by the antibody.
- Glucose metabolism: glucose-driven oxygen consumption was about 45% lower in Tg2576 hippocampus and about 30% lower in retina, and 12A12 raised it close to wild-type. Hexokinase activity was about 25% lower in Tg2576 hippocampus and about 100% higher in retina, and 12A12 reversed each (about 30% up in hippocampus, about 65% down in retina). Lactate was roughly 2.5-fold (hippocampus) and 4-fold (retina) higher in Tg2576 and 12A12 returned it toward control. See [[Glycolysis]].
- Glycolytic index: retina 0.41 (wild-type) to 4.48 (Tg2576) to 1.30 (treated); hippocampus 0.045, 0.154 and 0.090. The authors read the retina as a "reinforced Warburg effect" and the hippocampus as glycolysis compensating for impaired oxidative phosphorylation (see [[Mitochondrial dysfunction]]).
- Unpublished RNA-seq (Gene Expression Omnibus GSE223593, cited as in preparation) is said to show that a down-regulated mitochondrial energy pathway is reverted by 12A12 in both tissues; this is mentioned in the discussion, not shown.
- Proposed model: soluble pathological tau, including NH2htau, could disrupt APP trafficking toward [[Amyloid-beta formation]] directly through endocytic adaptors and indirectly through mitochondrial impairment and lactate accumulation.

## Why This Source Matters

It links tau-directed immunotherapy to the amyloid side of [[Alzheimer disease]] through two measurable cellular routes, endosomal sorting of APP and BACE1 and glucose handling, and it shows that the retina and hippocampus respond to the same antibody in partly different ways. That tissue difference (RIN3, Rab5 and hexokinase moved in opposite directions) is a caution for using the eye as a simple readout of brain treatment effect.

## Assessment

- The work is correlational: protein levels and enzyme activities change alongside lower Aβ after 12A12, but no experiment manipulates BIN1, RIN3, Rab5 or lactate to show they carry the antibody effect. Direct causation is left open by the authors.
- Whole hippocampus and whole retina were used, so cell-type specific changes are averaged out. Only steady-state protein amounts were measured for most targets, not activity or localisation, apart from the Rab5 fractionation.
- Increased Aβ clearance or degradation under 12A12 is not excluded, since in vivo Aβ turnover was not measured.
- Aβ lowering itself rests on earlier work and supplementary figures; only a 14-day treatment at one dose and time point at 6 months of age was studied, with no behavioural readout here.
- The glycolytic index uses hexokinase activity as a stand-in for glucose utilization, and the tissue-level interpretation (Warburg-like retina) is inferred rather than tested by isotope tracing.
- BIN1 changes in AD are reported in both directions in the literature, and the paper itself notes a study in 5xFAD mice where suppressing BIN1 did not change Aβ.

## Open Questions

- Does lowering BIN1 or RIN3, or blocking lactate production, abolish the Aβ-lowering effect of the tau antibody 12A12 in Tg2576 mice (only correlations between protein levels and Aβ were reported)?
- Does the tau antibody 12A12 change in vivo Aβ clearance or turnover in Tg2576 mice, in addition to lowering BACE1-driven production (turnover was not measured)?
- Which endocytic proteins, such as SORL1, account for the lower hippocampal Rab5 activity that 12A12 did not restore (proposed in the discussion but not tested)?
- Do the endocytic and glycolytic changes after 12A12 treatment appear in a specific cell type of hippocampus or retina (whole-tissue preparations average over cell types)?
