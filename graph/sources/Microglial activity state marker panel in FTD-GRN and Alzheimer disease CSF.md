# Microglial activity state marker panel in FTD-GRN and Alzheimer disease CSF

A biomarker discovery study that uses the two most opposite microglial states, a homeostatic-locked state in [[TREM2]] knockouts and a hyperactivated state in [[GRN]] (progranulin) knockouts, to find proteins that report microglial activity in cerebrospinal fluid. Candidates from mouse microglia and cerebrospinal fluid and from human iPSC-derived microglia were narrowed with CSF from people carrying GRN mutations, then checked in an Alzheimer cohort.

## Design

- Mice: 12-month-old wild-type, Grn knockout and Trem2 knockout C57BL/6J. CSF from 14 mice (6 wild-type, 4 Grn knockout, 4 Trem2 knockout) and CD11b-sorted microglia from 19 mice (7, 4, 8), measured by label-free LC-MS/MS ([[Label-free quantitative mass spectrometry proteomics]]).
- Cells: one female iPSC line with CRISPR knockouts of GRN or TREM2, differentiated to microglia (hiMGL); cell lysates and conditioned media, n = 6 per genotype and fraction ([[iPSC-derived microglia knockout models]]).
- People, discovery: CSF from 11 symptomatic heterozygous GRN mutation carriers with frontotemporal dementia (FTD-GRN; 3 of them with mild cognitive impairment) and 12 healthy non-carriers from the ALLFTD consortium, run on a timsTOF DIA-PASEF workflow.
- People, validation: published CSF proteomic data from 478 participants of the EMIF-AD MBD cohort, split by diagnosis (126 cognitively normal, 61 subjective cognitive impairment, 198 mild cognitive impairment, 93 Alzheimer disease) and by CSF amyloid-beta 1-42 status.
- Statistics: unpaired t-tests, with false discovery rate correction reported only in the supplement and not used to pick candidates; Spearman correlations; one-way ANOVA with Tukey tests; ROC curves with DeLong confidence intervals.

## Findings

- In Grn knockout mouse microglia, activation markers (Apoe, Lgals3, Lyz2, Clec7a, cathepsins) rose and homeostatic markers (P2ry12, Cx3cr1, Sall1) fell, matching published transcript data; 12 markers were up and 14 down at both protein and mRNA level. Trem2 knockout moved these markers the other way.
- Mouse CSF mirrored the microglial changes only partly: [[CTSB]], Ctsd, Apoe and Lyz1/2 rose in Grn knockout CSF, and [[CHI3L1]] was also higher, while Trem2 knockout CSF showed few changes.
- hiMGL reproduced the mouse pattern: [[CTSD]] and other cathepsins, [[APOE]], LGALS3 and TREM2 rose in GRN knockout cells, [[P2RY12]] fell, and 33% of proteins up in Grn knockout mouse microglia were also up in GRN knockout hiMGL lysates. Over 42% of proteins up in Grn knockout mouse CSF were up in GRN knockout hiMGL media. TREM2 knockout effects were smaller and overlapped little between species (5 shared proteins in cells, none in media versus CSF).
- In FTD-GRN CSF, GRN was lower and [[NEFL]] higher, 88 proteins were up, and 26 of them were also up in GRN knockout hiMGL media. Four of the 26 (FUCA1, HEXB, FUCA2, CAPG) were also up in Grn knockout mouse microglia; see [[FUCA1]], [[HEXB]], [[FUCA2]], [[CAPG]].
- "Panel 6" was drawn from those 26: [[FABP3]], [[MDH1]], [[GDI1]], [[CAPG]], [[CD44]] and [[GPNMB]]. All six rose in FTD-GRN CSF and GRN knockout hiMGL media; FABP3 and GPNMB also rose in cell lysates. In mice only CD44 rose in CSF and only CAPG in microglia. FABP3 rose by more than 50% in FTD-GRN CSF. FABP3, MDH1 and GDI1 were lower in the three carriers with mild cognitive impairment than in the rest of the FTD-GRN group.
- Diagnostic performance for FTD-GRN versus controls: panel 6 together AUC 0.87 (95% CI 0.64 to 1.0), FABP3 alone 0.93 (0.78 to 1.0).
- FABP3, MDH1 and GDI1 correlated with each other and with CHI3L1 in the ALLFTD cohort. GPNMB correlated with CSF TREM2 in FTD-GRN carriers but not in controls.
- In EMIF-AD MBD, only FABP3, MDH1 and GDI1 differed between diagnostic groups, with higher CSF levels in [[Mild cognitive impairment]] and [[Alzheimer disease]] than in cognitively normal people. CAPG, CD44 and GPNMB did not differ. Each of the three separated amyloid-positive from amyloid-negative mild cognitive impairment; MDH1 also separated them within the Alzheimer group.
- Panel 6 reached AUC 0.94 (0.87 to 1.0) for amyloid-positive Alzheimer disease versus amyloid-negative controls; FABP3 0.92, GDI1 0.90, MDH1 0.77. The three markers correlated more strongly in amyloid-positive Alzheimer patients than in amyloid-negative controls, and all three correlated with CHI3L1 there.
- Some proteins moved in opposite directions between models: MDH1, ENOPH1, CD84 and EPB41L3 were up in hiMGL and human CSF but down in mice.

## Interpretation by the authors

The authors read the rise of FABP3, MDH1 and GDI1 in amyloid-positive mild cognitive impairment as an early microglial response to seeded amyloid, in line with earlier soluble TREM2 increases up to 21 years before symptom onset in dominantly inherited Alzheimer disease. FABP3, CD44 and GPNMB point to lipid handling as a shared feature of microglial activation and neurodegeneration, and the authors place this next to the translocator protein (TSPO) PET signal, which depends on mitochondrial cholesterol transport.

## Why This Source Matters

It shows a route from genetically defined microglial states to fluid markers: choose models with opposite activation, keep only proteins that rise in knockout microglia secretions and in the matching human disease, then test them elsewhere. For [[Alzheimer disease]] it adds three CSF proteins that track early amyloid-related microglial responses and can be measured with a discovery proteomics workflow, which matters for trials of TREM2-stabilising antibodies that need target engagement markers.

## Assessment

- The human discovery set is small (11 carriers, 12 controls), clinical data for ALLFTD participants are limited, and the authors say the ROC analysis is a companion to conventional tests. The AUC of 0.87 for the panel has a lower confidence bound of 0.64.
- Candidates were chosen on uncorrected p-values; FDR-corrected results are only in the supplement.
- The panel is not microglia-specific; the authors call it microglia activation-dependent because other cell types express the proteins too. Cross-species agreement is partial (MDH1 goes the opposite way in mice), and the Trem2 knockout arm yielded few CSF candidates.
- The EMIF-AD MBD data are a published dataset analysed after the candidates were chosen; both cohorts are mostly of European ancestry, and no longitudinal or treatment-response data were tested.
- The text states EMIF-AD MBD amyloid status from CSF amyloid-beta 1-42 and gives the abbreviation A- as "amyloid positive, normal levels", an apparent typo.

## Open Questions

- Do CSF FABP3, MDH1 and GDI1 rise or fall in patients treated with a TREM2-activating antibody (not tested; the study has no treatment cohort)?
- Does the amyloid-dependent rise of FABP3, MDH1 and GDI1 in mild cognitive impairment hold in an independent Alzheimer cohort when candidates are chosen with false discovery rate correction (the authors selected candidates on uncorrected p-values)?
- Why does MDH1 rise in human CSF and hiMGL media but fall in Grn knockout mouse microglia and CSF (cell type, species or age effect; not resolved by the data)?
- Is GPNMB, which correlated with CSF TREM2 only in GRN mutation carriers, a better readout of TREM2 pathway engagement than soluble TREM2 itself (only 11 carriers were available)?
