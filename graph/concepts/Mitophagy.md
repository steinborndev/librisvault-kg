# Mitophagy

Mitophagy is the selective autophagic removal of damaged or surplus mitochondria, a core mitochondrial quality control step in neurons. It runs mainly through the [[PINK1]]-[[PRKN]] pathway, receptor-mediated routes, or the [[Alternative mitophagy]] pathway, and is reported to be impaired in [[Alzheimer disease]] models, patient tissue and patient-derived iPSCs.

## Connections

- Hippocampal mitophagy measured with an mt-Keima reporter was about 27% lower in 4-month-old [[5xFAD mouse]] animals than in wild type, and the inducer [[ALT001]] restored it; see [[mt-Keima mitophagy assay]]. Study: [[ALT001 induces Rab9-dependent alternative mitophagy in Alzheimer disease models]]
- Other inducers named as beneficial in Alzheimer models: urolithin A and [[Nicotinamide riboside]]; the uncoupler [[Carbonyl cyanide m-chlorophenyl hydrazone]] is too toxic for therapy.
- Related: [[Autophagy]], [[Mitochondrial dysfunction]], [[Mitochondrial biogenesis]]
- D-ribose lowered PINK1, Parkin and LC3B, LC3A/B-[[TOMM20]] co-localization and mitochondria-lysosome fusion in [[Neuro2a]] cells and [[APP-PS1 mouse]] hippocampus; [[Berberine]] restored them, [[Mdivi-1]] blocked the benefit, and the mechanism was linked to demethylation of the PINK1 promoter. Study: [[Berberine rescues D-ribose-induced Alzheimer pathology via mitophagy]]
