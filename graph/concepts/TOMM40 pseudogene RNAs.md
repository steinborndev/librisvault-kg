# TOMM40 pseudogene RNAs

The human genome carries five intronless copies of [[TOMM40]] cDNA with 87% to 96% identity to it: [[TOMM40P1]], [[TOMM40P2]], [[TOMM40P3]], [[TOMM40P4]] and an unnamed locus near P1 called P1b. All five give RNA in cultured human cell lines, and some of these transcripts resemble TOMM40 mRNA closely enough that standard RT-PCR primers, including junction-spanning ones, amplify them too.

Because of this, a TOMM40 expression value from a conventional assay is the sum of TOMM40 mRNA and pseudogene RNA. A single primer set covering four of the five pseudogenes put their share at 10% to 18% of the total pool in postmortem brain, so the real share is higher. Their function is unknown; the authors suggest they could compete for regulatory proteins or act as an RNA buffer that smooths fluctuations in TOMM40 RNA.

TOMM40L, a paralogous gene with only moderate similarity, is a sixth genomic hit but is a gene rather than a pseudogene.
