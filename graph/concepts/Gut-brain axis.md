# Gut-brain axis

The two-way communication between gut and brain through the vagus nerve, immune signals, enteric hormones and microbial metabolites. In [[Alzheimer disease]] work it asks whether the [[Gut microbiota]] and gut-made peptides such as [[Amyloid-beta]] influence brain pathology.

## Connections

- Gut Aβ42 injected into the colon reached cortex and hippocampus and was taken up by microglia. The vagus nerve carried it in 5-month-old mice but not in 10-month-old mice, where blood transport dominated alongside a leakier [[Blood-brain barrier dysfunction]]. Study: [[Gut-derived amyloid-beta and gut microbiota in Alzheimer disease mice]]
- Proposed as linking ageing, microbiota and Aβ production; the authors also cite gut-to-brain spread of α-synuclein ([[SNCA]]) and cohort links between inflammatory bowel disease and dementia risk.
- Related: [[Fecal microbiota transplantation]], [[Increased intestinal permeability]], [[Neuroinflammation]]
- Oral [[D-Pinitol]] in 5xFAD mice lowered plasma [[Lipopolysaccharide]] and intestinal [[TLR4]] together with brain Aβ plaques and better reversal learning, an association the authors read as a gut route to benefit; no experiment separated gut from brain effects. Study: [[D-Pinitol improves cognition, insulin signalling and gut barrier in 5xFAD mice]]
