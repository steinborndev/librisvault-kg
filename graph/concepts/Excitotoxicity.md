# Excitotoxicity

Excitotoxicity is neuronal damage or death from excessive glutamate exposure and hyperactivation of NMDA receptors, implicated in Alzheimer disease.

## Connections

- Tyr18-phosphorylated tau, made by [[FYN]], raised NMDA receptor-mediated calcium influx in mouse hippocampal neurons, which the review reads as amplifying excitotoxicity. Study: [[Amyloid-beta and tau interplay in Alzheimer disease review]]
- Introduced as the consequence of excess extrasynaptic NMDA receptor activity, which memantine counters; the mutant subunit [[GRIN2C p.A1072V]] raised NMDA-evoked current in rat neurons, offered as a possible route to excitotoxic stress. Study: [[Exome sequencing finds GRIN2C p.A1072V in an Italian late-onset Alzheimer family]]
- Cortical neurons from [[ZDHHC21 T209S knock-in mouse]] animals lost 30.0% viability after NMDA plus glycine against 13.8% for wild-type; [[2-Bromopalmitate]] and [[Cerulenin]] rescued them, linking excitotoxic vulnerability to FYN palmitoylation. Study: [[ZDHHC21 p.T209S in a Chinese Alzheimer family and knock-in mice]]
- Mouse cortical neurons with low gamma-secretase activity, or pre-treated with [[DAPT]], became propidium iodide positive sooner after 100 μM [[Glutamate]]. Study: [[A wild-type neuron subpopulation with familial Alzheimer-like phenotypes]]
