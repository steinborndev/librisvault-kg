# iPSC neuron-microglia co-culture and neuroimmune organoid models

Human induced pluripotent stem cell (hiPSC) platforms that put separately differentiated microglia onto neurons (2D) or into brain organoids (3D), so that a genotype can be tested in one cell type while the other stays constant. Isogenic pairs made by CRISPR-Cas9 editing isolate the effect of a single variant.

## Workflow

- Microglia are made in two stages: CD43-positive haematopoietic progenitors from a commercial kit (about days 9 to 11), then microglia differentiation with IL-34, TGF-β1 and M-CSF, with CD200 and CX3CL1 for maturation.
- 2D co-culture: day-21 neurons receive day-25 microglia at 5:2 and are kept 10 days; readouts include RNA sequencing of CD11b bead-purified microglia, pHrodo phagocytosis assays, lipid droplet and lipid peroxidation dyes, phospho-Tau staining and western blot, and multi-electrode array recording of network activity.
- 3D: brain organoids from wild-type or mutant iPSCs receive microglia at day 50 and are analysed 20 days later by phospho-Tau staining and western blot.
- Insults can be added from human material: Alzheimer postmortem synaptosomes, immunoprecipitated Tau oligomers, or synthetic amyloid beta 42.
