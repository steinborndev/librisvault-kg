# Necroptosis

Necroptosis is a regulated, caspase-independent form of necrotic cell death in which [[RIPK1]] and [[RIPK3]] assemble a complex that phosphorylates [[MLKL]], and MLKL then breaches the plasma membrane; it is triggered by death receptors such as [[TNFRSF1A]] when caspase-8 is blocked.

## Connections

- Raised in [[Alzheimer disease]]: phospho-MLKL was higher in patient cortex and in [[APP-PS1 mouse]] and [[5xFAD mouse]] hippocampus. TNF-α/TNFR1 signalling was needed for it in APP/PS1 mice, and in neurons it was shaped by [[SQSTM1]] binding to RIPK1 and by loss of [[UVRAG]]-dependent [[Autophagy]]. Study: [[TNF-alpha neuronal necroptosis via p62-RIPK1 and UVRAG in Alzheimer models]]
- Alternative death route: [[Apoptosis]]; consequence: [[Neuronal loss]]
- Experimental inducer in culture: [[TNF]] with [[Smac mimetic]] and [[Z-VAD-FMK]]; blocked by [[Necrostatin-1]]
