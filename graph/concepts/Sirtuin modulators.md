# Sirtuin modulators

Compounds that raise or lower the activity of the seven NAD+-dependent sirtuin deacetylases. They belong to the wider family of epigenetic drugs, which also includes inhibitors of DNA methyltransferases and of other histone deacetylases. In general synthetic modulators are more potent, soluble and selective than natural products, but also more toxic.

For [[Alzheimer disease]] the evidence is preclinical. Reported [[SIRT1]] activators include [[Resveratrol]], [[Curcumin]], [[Citicoline]], [[Donepezil]], [[Cilostazol]] and [[Folic acid]]; [[Honokiol]] activates [[SIRT3]]; [[Nicotinamide riboside]] supplies NAD+. [[SIRT2]] inhibitors (AGK-2, AK-7, AK1) lower Aβ production and favour autophagy in models. Epigenetic drugs have been tested without success in Alzheimer models, and no sirtuin modulator has had a clinical breakthrough in the disease.
