# Heterogeneity of late-onset Alzheimer disease

The observation that non-familial [[Late-onset Alzheimer disease]] is a mixture of clinically, pathologically and molecularly distinct subgroups, not one uniform disease, which complicates both trial design and the search for mechanisms.

## Connections

- A 2021 perspective cites a meta-analysis that split non-familial late-onset disease into four subtypes, "typical" being a little over half, and an independent subtyping by tau PET patterns; it also cites links of clinical heterogeneity to [[APOE]] genotype and distinct brain transcriptome patterns tied to polymorphic risk variants and matching differences among mouse models. Unrecognised heterogeneity could sink a trial if treatment and placebo arms are not comparable, and a validated blood or cerebrospinal-fluid biomarker may need to be subtype-specific. Study: [[Genetics and failed Alzheimer disease therapeutics perspective]]
- The same text lists a fourth vulnerability: assuming late-onset disease is a slower form of [[Familial Alzheimer disease]] (a different pathway in rare, fully penetrant [[Early-onset Alzheimer disease]]), and offers a branched network of convergent mechanisms (inflammation, infection, cerebrovascular disease, RNA modification) as the alternative to the linear [[Amyloid cascade hypothesis]].
- Related: [[Mouse model validity for Alzheimer disease]], [[Mild cognitive impairment]]
- Different risk-gene perturbations in mice map to different human processes (immune and microglial for Apoe, neuronal for Clu), offered as a molecular reading of why late-onset cases may fall into distinct subgroups.
