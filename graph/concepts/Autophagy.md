# Autophagy

Autophagy is the lysosomal degradation of cytoplasmic material, including aggregated proteins; impaired autophagy is thought to let amyloid-beta and tau accumulate in Alzheimer disease, and [[MTOR]] activity suppresses it.

## Connections

- Readouts here: [[MAP1LC3B]], [[SQSTM1]], [[MTOR]]. Isoacteoside was the compound linked to this pathway. Study: [[Ajugol and isoacteoside as anti-Alzheimer components of Rehmannia root]]
- Disease context: [[Alzheimer disease]]
- Sirtuin activators such as [[Resveratrol]] and [[Cilostazol]] induce autophagy that lowers Aβ in cell models, while [[SIRT2]] overactivity is linked to impaired autophagy. Study: [[SIRT2 genotype with APOE and CYP2D6 in Alzheimer disease treatment response]]
- Amyloid-beta 1-42 raised [[BECN1]] in young cultured mouse microglia but not in aged ones, and brought low LC3 puncta in aged cells up to the young level, suggesting autophagosomes form but turn over poorly with age. Study: [[Aged cultured microglia respond less to amyloid-beta than young microglia]]
- Selective degradation of mitochondria is covered at [[Mitophagy]]; [[ALT001]] induced it without raising [[MAP1LC3B]]-II, LC3 puncta or cytosolic Keima puncta, so it acts independently of general autophagy. Study: [[ALT001 induces Rab9-dependent alternative mitophagy in Alzheimer disease models]]
- TNF-α, especially with Smac mimetic and Z-VAD-FMK, stalled autophagic flux in [[SH-SY5Y]], [[PC12]] and primary neurons (more LC3-II and p62, fewer autolysosomes), and [[Chloroquine]] worsened the death; loss of [[UVRAG]] was the identified cause, and the resulting p62 build-up fed [[Necroptosis]]. Study: [[TNF-alpha neuronal necroptosis via p62-RIPK1 and UVRAG in Alzheimer models]]
