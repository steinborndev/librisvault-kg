# Neuropsychiatric symptoms of Alzheimer disease

Anxiety, depression, apathy, dysphoria, agitation, aggression, delusions, hallucinations and sleep disturbance occur in 10 to 90% of people with dementia and tend to increase as cognition declines. They speed cognitive decline, reduce quality of life and raise the risk of institutionalisation, and they are usually treated with psychotropic drugs in patients who already take many medicines, which makes drug interactions and adverse reactions common. Mood disorders can also precede dementia, so the relation runs in both directions.

## Connections

- Anxiety and depression in 1006 patients were more frequent and severe in women, were unrelated to vascular risk factors or glycaemia in that cohort, and improved in one month of multifactorial treatment, with response depending on [[APOE]] genotype and on [[CYP2D6]], [[CYP2C19]] and [[CYP2C9]] metabolizer class. Study: [[Pharmacogenetics of anxiety and depression in Alzheimer disease patients]]
- Symptoms: [[Anxiety]], [[Depression]]; drugs: [[Fluoxetine]], [[Paroxetine]], [[Sertraline]], [[Alprazolam]]
- Wider context: [[Sex differences in Alzheimer disease]], [[Pharmacogenetics]], [[Alzheimer disease]]
- Earlier-stage picture: in biomarker-defined amnestic [[Mild cognitive impairment]], informant-rated symptoms (apathy, affective dysregulation, impulse dyscontrol) were higher than in controls and 48.4% of patients met [[Mild behavioral impairment]] criteria on the [[Mild Behavioral Impairment Checklist]], while psychotic and socially inappropriate symptoms were rare; [[APOE]] and [[BDNF p.V66M]] were not associated with severity.
