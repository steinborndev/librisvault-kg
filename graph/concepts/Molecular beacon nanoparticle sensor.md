# Molecular beacon nanoparticle sensor

A turn-on fluorescence probe in which a quantum dot is held next to a quencher by a stem-loop of oligonucleotide. When a target microRNA hybridises to the loop, or an antibody arm binds its antigen, the stem opens, the quencher separates from the quantum dot and fluorescence appears; with no target the signal stays off.

## Design variants

- microRNA sensor: a quantum dot (QD565) on the 5' end of a probe oligonucleotide complementary to the target, a short switch sequence at the 3' end, and a biotinylated linker strand carrying the quencher BHQ2. The two strands are annealed at 93 °C and cooled to 4 °C.
- Antigen sensor: the oligonucleotide arms are joined to an antibody by carbodiimide (EDC) coupling, then annealed to the quencher switch strand in the same way.
- Read-out: probes are immobilised on streptavidin-coated plates or glass through the biotin, and read with a confocal microscope, plate reader or fluorescence spectrophotometer. Quantum dots of 5 nm or less in diameter are proposed, with cadmium-based cores.

## Strengths and limits

- Claimed advantages over FISH and ELISA: lower cost, simpler preparation, faster results, and detection from blood.
- Evidence so far is a small proof of concept (six plasma samples), and cadmium-containing quantum dots raise toxicity questions for any in vivo use.

## Connections

- Applied to [[Alzheimer disease]] with microRNA markers ([[MIR106B]], [[MIR153-1]], [[MIR155]], [[MIR124-1]], [[MIR34A]], [[MIR181A1]], [[MIR146B]], [[MIR200A]]) and protein markers ([[APP]], [[MAPT]], [[APOE]]).
- Tested in [[HT22]] cells, mouse brain sections and human plasma.
