# Amyloid cascade hypothesis

The hypothesis, built on the discovery of [[APP]], [[PSEN1]] and [[PSEN2]] mutations in familial disease, that amyloid-beta accumulation is the initiating event of [[Alzheimer disease]] and that tau tangles follow downstream. A rival dual-cascade view holds that sporadic disease arises from correlated but independent amyloid and tau processes.

## Connections

- Genetic evidence for the dual view: apart from [[APOE]], no locus was shared between CSF amyloid-beta 42 and phosphorylated tau, amyloid being dominated by APOE and tau having several inputs. The authors suggest this may help explain the limited clinical benefit of plaque-lowering drugs. Study: [[Genome-wide meta-analysis of CSF amyloid-beta 42 and phosphorylated tau]]
- Related: [[Amyloid-beta formation]], [[Neurofibrillary tangle]]
- In isogenic familial hiPSC cultures, phospho-tau fell with APOE2 only where the Aβ42/40 ratio fell and not with total Aβ alone, consistent with tau following the ratio. Study: [[APOE2 lowers amyloid-beta in isogenic hiPSC models of familial Alzheimer disease]]
- Cited with the success of amyloid antibodies ([[Anti-amyloid antibody therapy]]), the protective [[APP p.A673T]] variant and familial mutations as support for the hypothesis despite the failure of non-selective BACE1 inhibitors such as [[Verubecestat]]; the substrate-selective answer is [[FAH65]]. Study: [[APP-selective BACE1 inhibitor FAH65 from a phenytoin screening hit]]
- BRI2 ([[ITM2B]]) is proposed as a regulator of APP processing and Aβ fibrillation, giving the cascade a further upstream player; the review suggests a modified etiology hypothesis. Study: [[BRI2 in familial British and Danish dementia and Alzheimer disease review]]
- A review argues that extracellular oligomers of amyloid-beta and tau act synergistically on synapses and memory, in parallel and not only downstream of amyloid as the cascade holds; it still calls Aβ pathology the primary driver, an unresolved tension. Study: [[Amyloid-beta and tau interplay in Alzheimer disease review]]
- Contrasted with a proposal that white matter injury and cholesterol dysmetabolism can start or worsen amyloid pathology. Study: [[White matter injury, cholesterol and amyloid in Alzheimer disease review]]
- A 2021 perspective argues the hypothesis rose on familial genetics ([[APP]] dosage in [[Down syndrome]], APP, [[PSEN1]] and [[PSEN2]] mutations) and became dogma, while tau genetics ([[MAPT]] and [[Frontotemporal dementia]]) pointed away from Alzheimer disease; it ties the failed antibody and BACE-inhibitor Phase-3 trials to four vulnerabilities, the main one being the assumption that late-onset disease is a slower form of familial disease. Study: [[Genetics and failed Alzheimer disease therapeutics perspective]]; see also [[Mouse model validity for Alzheimer disease]] and [[Heterogeneity of late-onset Alzheimer disease]].
