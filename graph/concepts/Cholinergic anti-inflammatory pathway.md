# Cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway

The cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway is the neural reflex through which acetylcholine acting on the alpha7 nicotinic receptor ([[CHRNA7]]) of macrophages and microglia restrains NF-κB-driven cytokine release. It was defined in animals, all of which lack the human fusion gene [[CHRFAM7A]].

## Connections

- In human iPSC microglia-like cells the brake was an active process in cells without CHRFAM7A (nicotine strengthened it, [[Methyllycaconitine]] released it), whereas CHRFAM7A carriers behaved as if the brake was already partly off, with higher baseline NF-κB targets and stronger responses to [[Amyloid-beta]] and [[Lipopolysaccharide]].
