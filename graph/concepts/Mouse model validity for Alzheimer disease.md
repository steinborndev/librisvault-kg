# Mouse model validity for Alzheimer disease

The question of whether a genetically engineered mouse reproduces [[Alzheimer disease]] closely enough to predict the outcome of a human trial. Validity is judged by face (looks like the disease), construct (rests on the same cause) and predictive pharmacological validity (responds to drugs as patients do).

## Connections

- A 2021 perspective judges the accepted mouse genetic models to fail all three tests: mice are short-lived with little neocortex, plaques and tangles were hard to engineer, the "triple transgenic" mouse ([[3xTg-AD mouse]]) combines mutations in [[APP]] and [[PSEN1]] with a [[MAPT]] mutation that causes [[Frontotemporal dementia]] and not Alzheimer disease, and no mouse shows neurodegeneration like the human disease. That the models did not predict the Phase-3 antibody and BACE-inhibitor results is taken as evidence of missing pharmacological validity. Study: [[Genetics and failed Alzheimer disease therapeutics perspective]]
- Better models are on the way: a systematic look at genetic background effects in the 5xFAD line ([[5xFAD mouse]], written FAD5X in the paper) gave a new strain whose transcriptome is closer to [[Late-onset Alzheimer disease]], and the MODEL-AD Consortium aims to build, validate and distribute improved late-onset models. The perspective asks geneticists and neuroscientists to define validation criteria, proposes higher mammals and patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cell lines as parallel approaches, and sees genetic model organisms as whole-animal bioassays for a molecule or pathway. Study: [[Genetics and failed Alzheimer disease therapeutics perspective]]
- Related: [[Heterogeneity of late-onset Alzheimer disease]], [[Amyloid cascade hypothesis]]
- Flies are offered as a complementary, faster whole-animal model: tau, Aβ42 and DYRK1A-orthologue overexpression reproduce degeneration, short life, poor movement, sleep and memory deficits, and one DYRK1A inhibitor corrected most of them; the paper argues rodent familial models miss the roughly 95% sporadic cases and helped drive drug attrition. See [[Drosophila models of Alzheimer disease and Down syndrome]]. Study: [[PST-001 DYRK1A inhibitor rescues fly models of Alzheimer and Down syndrome]]
- Example of a treatment study in an aggressive amyloid line ([[TgCRND8 mouse]]) with a donepezil arm as pharmacological benchmark; treatment began after plaques and memory deficits were established. Study: [[Magnolol improves cognition and neuropathology in TgCRND8 Alzheimer mice]]
- A panel of risk-gene mice ([[Apoe knockout mouse]], [[Clu knockout mouse]], [[GFAP-APOE4 mouse]], [[Bin1 heterozygous mouse]], [[Cd2ap heterozygous mouse]]) was scored by transcriptome similarity to human post-mortem modules, a face-validity style test that the Apoe and Clu knockouts passed at 6 months and the other three largely did not; see [[Mouse-human module comparison]]. Study: [[Late-onset Alzheimer risk gene mouse models mapped to human brain modules]]
- A treatment study in homozygous [[5xFAD mouse]] animals whose authors state the line models early-onset amyloidosis through APP and PSEN1 mutations and not sporadic disease; here untreated mice showed no excess AT8 tau at 8 months, so tau benefits cannot be judged in this line. Study: [[D-Pinitol improves cognition, insulin signalling and gut barrier in 5xFAD mice]]
- Offered as a species-specific reason for the alpha7 translational gap: [[CHRFAM7A]] is absent in all animal models and reverses alpha7's inflammatory effect in human microglia-like cells. See [[Human-specific gene duplications]]. Study: [[CHRFAM7A proinflammatory switch of alpha7 nAChR in iPSC microglia]]
