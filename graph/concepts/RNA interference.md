# RNA interference

RNA interference is sequence-specific silencing of a gene by a short complementary RNA such as a small interfering RNA (siRNA), which guides cleavage or repression of the matching messenger RNA. Compared with small-molecule inhibitors, siRNA is highly specific and long-lasting, but naked siRNA is degraded by nucleases, is cleared quickly, and does not cross the blood-brain barrier, so brain use needs a carrier.
