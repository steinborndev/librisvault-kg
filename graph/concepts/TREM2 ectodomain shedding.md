# TREM2 ectodomain shedding

The release of the extracellular domain of [[TREM2]] as soluble TREM2 (sTREM2) by the metalloproteases [[ADAM10]] and [[ADAM17]], which cut the stalk after His157. Shedding removes the signalling-competent full-length receptor from the cell surface and leaves a C-terminal fragment in the membrane, so it is generally read as a brake on TREM2 signalling; sTREM2 in cerebrospinal fluid is a biomarker, and whether it has its own functions is unsettled.

## Connections

- The stalk-binding antibody [[4D9 monoclonal antibody]] cut shedding in cells (EC50 2.3 nM), but only as a bivalent IgG, not as a Fab, although both blocked ADAM17 cutting of a stalk peptide in vitro; the broad inhibitor [[GM6001]] had a similar effect on membrane and soluble TREM2. The authors recall that the Alzheimer-linked TREM2 H157Y change increases shedding and lowers phagocytosis. Study: [[Dual function TREM2 stalk antibody 4D9 in mouse microglia and APP knock-in mice]]
- Read-out: [[CSF soluble TREM2 target engagement assay]]
- Consequences for microglia: [[Phagocytosis]], [[Disease-associated microglia]]
- Soluble TREM2 is raised in patient CSF and is read in one study as protective: it bound [[TAGLN2]] on neurons and lowered tau phosphorylation through [[RHOA]] and [[GSK3B]]. Study: [[Amyloid-beta and tau interplay in Alzheimer disease review]]
