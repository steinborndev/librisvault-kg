# Adipose-derived stem cell exosome therapy

Exosomes (30-150 nm [[Extracellular vesicle]] particles) released by adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs, a mesenchymal stem cell type) are being tested as a cell-free treatment for [[Alzheimer disease]]. They are thought to cross the blood-brain barrier and carry non-coding RNAs, including circular RNAs, to the brain. Earlier work cited by the authors found that intravenous ADSC exosomes improved learning and memory, reduced plaque deposition and Aβ levels, and normalised cytokines in amyloid mice. Growing the donor cells in 1% oxygen (hypoxic pretreatment) is reported to strengthen the repair effect of mesenchymal stem cell exosomes.

## Connections
