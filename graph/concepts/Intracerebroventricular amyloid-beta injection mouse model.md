# Intracerebroventricular amyloid-beta injection mouse model

A non-transgenic mouse model of [[Alzheimer disease]]-like toxicity in which pre-aggregated Aβ1-42 is infused once into a lateral cerebral ventricle of an adult wild-type mouse. It produces learning and memory impairment, neuron loss and microglial and cytokine activation within weeks, and is used for fast screening of candidate treatments. It models acute exposure to a defined [[Amyloid-beta]] load, not chronic plaque build-up.

## Method

- Aβ1-42 at 1 μg/μL in phosphate-buffered saline is stored at -20 °C for 4 weeks to form aggregates; 5 μL is infused at 2 μL/min under tiletamine-zolazepam and xylazine anaesthesia, with stereotaxic coordinates AP -0.5 mm, ML 1.0 mm, DV 2.5 mm from Bregma. Saline serves as control.
- Treatment began 24 h later and ran 20 days, with the [[Passive avoidance task]] on days 8-10 and the [[Morris water maze]] on days 15-21, then immunohistochemistry (Aβ, [[RBFOX3]], [[AIF1]]), western blots and cytokine ELISA.

## Limits

- A single bolus is cleared over time, so a lower Aβ signal after treatment can mean clearance or detoxification, not blocked aggregation; no tau or synaptic pathology builds up as in transgenic lines.
