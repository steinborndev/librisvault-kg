# Drosophila models of Alzheimer disease and Down syndrome

[[Drosophila melanogaster]] lines that overexpress human [[MAPT]] tau 0N4R, secreted tandem human [[Amyloid-beta]] 42 or the fly [[DYRK1A]] orthologue minibrain (mnb isoform H) in selected neurons, used to model the neurodegeneration, short life, movement, sleep and memory deficits of [[Alzheimer disease]] and of Alzheimer disease in [[Down syndrome]], and to screen drugs quickly.

## Design

- Tissue-specific GAL4 drivers set the assay: GMR (eye degeneration), elav (all neurons: lifespan, climbing), tim (clock neurons: sleep) and OK107 (mushroom body: associative memory).
- Readouts are eye surface area, survival curves, negative geotaxis, activity-monitor sleep and aversive olfactory conditioning with sensory controls.
- Human tau was the most toxic transgene in the eye, then Aβ42, then mnb; Aβ42 gave the largest climbing loss and the shortest life.

## Connections

- The paper argues flies fill a gap left by rodent knock-in and transgenic familial models, which do not reproduce the roughly 95% sporadic cases and whose drug results have translated poorly; see [[Mouse model validity for Alzheimer disease]]. Flies are said to reproduce excitability change, raised calcium signalling, neurodegeneration and poor plasticity, memory and sleep seen in rodent tau, Aβ and DYRK1A models. Study: [[PST-001 DYRK1A inhibitor rescues fly models of Alzheimer and Down syndrome]]
- Test compound: [[PST-001]]; phenotypes: [[Neurodegeneration]], [[Sleep disturbance]], [[Memory impairment]]
- Mammalian counterpart for Down syndrome: [[Ts65Dn mouse]]
