# PPR@siBACE1 nanomedicine

PPR@siBACE1 is a self-assembled nanoparticle in which an siRNA against [[BACE1]] is held by electrostatic binding to a [[Polyamidoamine dendrimer]] that carries polyethylene glycol chains tipped with the [[RVG29 peptide]]. The design aims to cross the blood-brain barrier by receptor-mediated transcytosis, reach neurons through nicotinic receptors, and be engulfed by microglia, so that BACE1 is silenced in both cell types ([[RNA interference]]). The non-targeted analogue PP@siBACE1 and free siBACE1 are its controls.

## Connections

- Particle size about 100 nm by light scattering (about 20 nm by electron microscopy), stable for a week in PBS; escaped lysosomes and entered cells through macropinocytosis, clathrin- and caveolin-dependent routes ([[Amiloride]], [[Chlorpromazine]], [[Genistein]]). See [[Endocytosis]].
- In [[FAD4T mouse]] animals five intravenous doses lowered brain BACE1 and Aβ1-42 (by 52%), restored [[MBP]], reduced glial activation and cytokines ([[TNF]], [[IL1B]], [[IL6]]) and improved memory, whereas PP@siBACE1 and free siRNA had little effect. Study: [[Brain-targeted BACE1 siRNA nanomedicine in Alzheimer disease mice]]
- Cells used: [[bEnd.3]], [[Neuro2a]], [[BV2]]
- Disease: [[Alzheimer disease]]
