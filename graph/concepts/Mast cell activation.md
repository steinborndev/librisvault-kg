# Mast cell activation

Mast cell activation is the triggering of mast cells to release stored mediators (histamine, tryptase, TNF) and to make new cytokines, chemokines and growth factors. In the brain, mast cells lie near nerve endings, meninges and vessels, so their activation reaches both neurons and the blood-brain barrier.

## Connections

- Proposed in a review as the link between psychological stress and Alzheimer pathology: stress and [[CRH]] activate mast cells, and their mediators ([[Histamine]], [[TPSAB1]] tryptase, [[TNF]], [[NGF]]) drive pain, glial activation and loosening of the [[Tight junction]]. Study: [[Stress, mast cells and blood-brain barrier disruption in Alzheimer disease]]
- Inhibited by [[Cromolyn]] and [[Masitinib]]
- Related process: [[Neuroinflammation]], [[Blood-brain barrier dysfunction]]
- Trigger: [[Psychological stress]]
- Interleukins [[IL1B]] and IL-33 are named as activators of mast cells, and IL-37 as an anti-inflammatory counterweight.
