# Cellular senescence

Cellular senescence is the stable arrest of a cell's division and loss of function after stress or aging, usually read by senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) activity. In the brain, senescent or dystrophic microglia are proposed to lose their neuroprotective functions and contribute to [[Alzheimer disease]].

## Connections

- Amyloid-beta 1-42 doubled the share of SA-β-gal positive cells (2.1-fold) in mouse microglia kept 2 days in culture, toward the level of those kept 16 days, and raised [[MIR146A]]; ferritin also rose, less in aged cells. The authors read this as amyloid-beta pushing activated microglia into a senescent-like state. Study: [[Aged cultured microglia respond less to amyloid-beta than young microglia]]

- Aged monkey entorhinal cortex showed more senescence-associated beta-galactosidase staining alongside more Aβ deposits and lower neuron density. Study: [[Single-nucleus atlas of aging monkey entorhinal cortex and Alzheimer disease]]
