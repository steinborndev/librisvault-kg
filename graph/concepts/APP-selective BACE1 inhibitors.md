# APP-selective BACE1 inhibitors

APP-selective BACE1 inhibitors (ASBIs) are small molecules meant to block [[BACE1]] cleavage of [[APP]] while leaving other BACE1 substrates such as [[NRG1]] and [[SELPLG]] largely alone. The idea responds to the failure of non-selective inhibitors ([[Verubecestat]], [[Lanabecestat]]) in [[Alzheimer disease]] trials, where blocking non-APP substrates is the leading explanation for side effects and cognitive worsening. The hypothesised mechanism is binding to APP so that it cannot enter the enzyme's active site, rather than occupying the active site for every substrate.

## Connections

- First reported ASBIs were flavonoids from a library screen (including galangin as a pro-drug); the fluoro aminohydantoin series with [[FAH65]] came from a [[Phenytoin]] hit. Study: [[APP-selective BACE1 inhibitor FAH65 from a phenytoin screening hit]]
- Selectivity is expressed as the ratio of NRG1 to APP IC50 (above 200 for FAH65, under 2.5 for verubecestat and lanabecestat).
- Proposed uses: early treatment in people at risk (for example [[APOE]] ε4 carriers), maintenance therapy after amyloid clearance, and combination with [[Anti-amyloid antibody therapy]]. See [[Amyloid cascade hypothesis]].
