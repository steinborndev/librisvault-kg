# AMPA receptor trafficking

AMPA receptor trafficking is the regulated insertion, recycling and endocytic removal of AMPA-type glutamate receptors at the postsynaptic membrane, which sets synaptic strength: added receptors underlie [[Long-term potentiation]], removed receptors underlie long-term depression. Insertion depends on binding proteins, phosphorylation and ubiquitination, and on scaffolds such as [[DLG4]] (PSD-95).

## Connections

- Aβ, intracellular and extracellular, drives AMPA and NMDA receptor endocytosis and loss of PSD-95; calcium-dependent dephosphorylation by calcineurin ([[PPP3CA]]) promotes removal. Study: [[Endocytic trafficking genes and synapse dysfunction in Alzheimer disease]]
- [[BIN1]] is proposed to supply GluA1 through endosomal recycling, [[PICALM]]/CALM modulates GluA2 endocytosis, and [[APOE]] ε4 may lower AMPA receptor availability through calcineurin.
- Disease: [[Alzheimer disease]]; see also [[Synapse loss]], [[Endocytosis]]
