# Synaptic vesicle cycle

The synaptic vesicle cycle is the repeated fusion of neurotransmitter-filled vesicles with the presynaptic membrane and their retrieval by [[Endocytosis]]. Fusion needs the SNARE complex (VAMP2, [[STX1A]] and SNAP-25) triggered by calcium binding to synaptotagmin-1; retrieval uses clathrin adaptors (AP-2, AP-3, AP180 and [[PICALM]]/CALM), BAR-domain proteins such as [[BIN1]] and endophilin, and scission by [[DNM1]].

## Connections

- Aβ at picomolar concentration raises vesicle recycling, while pathological Aβ inhibits fusion (through syntaxin-1a, synaptophysin-VAMP2 and PIP2 loss) and endocytosis (through dynamin-1 depletion). Study: [[Endocytic trafficking genes and synapse dysfunction in Alzheimer disease]]
- Late-onset risk genes of trafficking ([[BIN1]], [[PICALM]], [[CD2AP]], [[SORL1]], [[APOE]]) all touch this cycle; see [[SYP]] and [[CHRNA7]].
- Disease: [[Alzheimer disease]]
- Genes of the KEGG synaptic vesicle cycle pathway were lower in Alzheimer cortex, and four of them ([[ATP6V1D]], [[ATP6V1G2]], [[CLTB]], [[NSF]]) had genetically predicted expression linked to lower Alzheimer risk. Study: [[Four synaptic vesicle cycle genes protective in Alzheimer disease by MR]]
